### Strongest Counter-Argument
Read strictly, this paper demonstrates that physics-aware scheduling is *different*, not *better* — and its own data are the refutation. The central claim, "we formulate physics-derived objectives within NSGA-III to balance profit, NESZ, and resolution," is tested against comparators the paper itself fielded. Against the one-line squint-minimizing greedy G-SM, MOEA-3 achieves *lower* f3 at every density (0.699 vs 0.722 at S1; 0.680 vs 0.786 at S4) despite G-SM operating at half the coverage, and MOEA-2's f2 surplus over G-SM is at most +1% at S1 (0.394 vs 0.389) — within noise at S2 (0.328 vs 0.357 mean, G-SM's SD 0.078); the multi-objective machinery's only consistent surplus is coverage. The headline numbers "+34% f2 / +41% f3 vs G-BL" are thus gains over a baseline engineered to ignore physics — the weakest comparator in the paper's own table. Worse, the physics content the ablation says matters is the squint term — the same term G-SM already exploits — while the incidence component, which §6.4 crowns as the dominant structural conflict (the "θ-axis trade-off"), is causally inert at every density (variant C, p=0.83). The third-objective "density-dependent" finding is equally fragile: effect sizes (Cliff's δ f3 = +0.52 at S3, +0.32 at S4) remain non-negligible at high density, contradicting the "coincide at S4" narrative. And the robustness controls undermine rather than reinforce: random-init variant D collapses f1 to 0.32 (S3) with 70 selected tasks vs 154 — a metric artifact the control section hides by reporting only f2. The simplest explanation fitting all data: physics awareness helps marginally at sparse density via squint steering, the MOEA machinery adds little beyond coverage preservation, and the dense-regime "saturation" is an artifact of seeding the search with the very solution that defines the coverage anchor.

RECOMMENDATION: Major Revision (the empirical record appears honest and reproducible, but the abstract, contributions, mechanism section, and one control experiment must be rewritten to match the data).

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. **The dense-regime "saturation" is seed-lock, not problem-imposed.** Every non-random solver is initialized from G-BL's solution; the "invariance to objectives at N≥300" and the f1*≈1.0 convergence may simply be the search returning to its own seed. The discriminating experiment — random-init at doubled budget, or random-init on the *profit-only* variant to see whether coverage convergence is objective-independent — was never run; the only random-init dense data (f1*=0.32 at S3) are consistent with a search that never finds high coverage on its own. The paper itself concedes this in Limitations ("whether the MOEA can independently discover high-coverage solutions without greedy seeding remains an open question") — that concession is the sound position for the whole saturation claim, yet the abstract states it as an established discovery.
2. **Selection vs timing: the "quality gains" at S1 may be task-selection artifacts, not physics-aware timing.** G-BL inserts at earliest feasible time; the MOEAs select 15–16 of 20 targets with freedom to drop never-observable or low-quality tasks. A solver that drops tasks trivially raises mean-over-selected f2/f3; the paper never decomposes its headline +34%/+41% into "better timing for the same tasks" vs "dropping low-quality tasks."
3. **G-SM is the field-relevant baseline the paper should have been compared against.** A one-line squint-aware insertion heuristic is the cheapest physics-aware scheduler imaginable; the paper's entire positive narrative (MOEA-3 +41% f3) is refuted on the radiometric axis by this comparator in its own table. The multi-objective contribution reduces to "coverage-preserving quality steering", which needs its own honest quantification against G-SM at matched coverage.
4. **Variant D is not a "no-physics" null — it is a coverage-maximizer with different search geometry.** At S1, D improves f1* to 1.002 (i.e., beats physics-free G-BL on its own axis) while selecting 19.5 vs 15.6 tasks; the ablation's "D" deflects coverage into the higher-f1 axis rather than testing "physics absent" — the correct null for "does physics matter" would keep the quality metrics as post hoc diagnostics while varying only the objective weights, which D does — but then the S1 result reads as "physics objectives cost 18% coverage at S1", a framing the paper never quite embraces.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- Mission operators who must value the f2/f3 gains in task terms: the "14% coverage cost for +34% f2" is never monetized or mapped to downstream task value; at S1 that is ~3–4 of 20 targets.
- Operators whose energy/memory budgets bind: C3–C4 are deliberately non-binding, so the paper's trade-off surface exists only in the resource-unconstrained regime, and its guidance ("MOEA at sparse, greedy at dense") may not transfer to budget-bound missions.
- Dense-monitoring program managers who need to know *which* targets to drop when quality steering is applied: the mean-over-selected metric hides per-target quality degradation (worst-case tasks are averaged away).
- SAR product users (image analysts) for whom NESZ and resolution indices need mapping to detection/classification performance; the paper stops at the index level.

### Unexamined Premise
The entire normalisation structure presumes the greedy baseline is a legitimate anchor: f1* is defined against G-BL, GA-P-BL is *guaranteed* to equal G-BL by its never-worse guard, and the saturation claim is "all solvers converge to the G-BL anchor" — a circle in which the anchor is both the yardstick and the destination of every non-random solver. The paper measures how far smart physics-aware scheduling departs from a physics-blind greedy that it already seeds into every solver, and calls the small distance a discovery. The hidden premise — that this convergence measures something true about scheduling physics rather than about the seeding protocol — is never tested by the one experiment that would do so (random-init, unbudgeted search, quality objectives only, no G-BL anywhere in the pipeline).

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The paper's honesty is real and unusual: p-values for null ablations are reported in full (p=0.46, p=0.83), the +10.8% single outlier against G-BL is disclosed, GA-P-BL's identity with G-BL is stated repeatedly, and the HV disclaimers are in plain text. The defects above are defects of *framing and inference*, not of data concealment — every quantity I cite was found in the repository's own JSON/CSV artifacts.
- The most striking non-defect: G-SM (one line of squint logic) achieving higher f3 than a full NSGA-III at every density, at 29–51% coverage, is arguably the paper's most publishable finding — it would make an excellent, honest "the trivial heuristic wins the radiometric axis" result if the paper were restructured around it instead of defending against it.
- The self-correction of the dense-regime correlation story (from "strengthens with density" to "no reliable density trend; residual −0.15 to −0.23") is a good-faith revision; M3 shows some of its leftover rhetoric ("moderate ≈ −0.2", "coincide") still needs alignment with the corrected data.
- The random-init S3 collapse (f1*=0.315 for MOEA-2; 0.319 for variant D) is itself a strong, honest control result for the *coverage-conditional* claim — the paper should lead with it rather than obscure it.
