# EIC Review — Physics-Aware Multi-Objective Scheduling for an Agile SAR Satellite
**Panel:** 2026-08-18 pre-submission extra round
**Seat:** Editor-in-Chief
**Manuscript:** `papers/single-sat-quality/small-paper-ijae.tex`

## Recommendation
**Minor Revision.** The manuscript is competently executed, clearly scoped, and suitable in subject matter for *Advances in Space Research*. Its core claim — an empirical, density-dependent saturation of the benefit from physics-aware multi-objective scheduling in single-pass, single-satellite Stripmap operation — is honestly reported and adequately bounded. The main editorial concern is claim calibration: the word “boundary” in title/abstract/conclusion is stronger than the evidence supports, and the “five-solver” comparison includes one solver (GA-P-BL) that is numerically identical to the greedy baseline by construction. These are framing revisions, not new experiments.

## Strengths
1. **Clear, honest scope.** The paper states the single-pass, single-satellite, Stripmap boundary in title (line 23), abstract (lines 38–39), contributions (lines 139–147), and limitations (lines 1289–1300). This prevents over-generalization.
2. **Negative result framed as a result.** Rather than claiming a universal solver winner, the manuscript explicitly says it does not identify a superior solver (abstract, lines 56–58; conclusion, lines 1361–1363). That is appropriate for the observed dense-regime convergence.
3. **Reproducibility posture is strong.** Public repository, fixed seeds, explicit CALIBER/data-availability statement (lines 1388–1394), and AI disclosure (lines 1367–1372) all meet current ASR/Elsevier expectations.
4. **Mechanistic explanation supports headline finding.** The envelope/null/schedule correlation analysis (§6.4, lines 970–1064) gives a geometric explanation for sparse-vs-dense behavior instead of leaving the saturation as an empirical curve.
5. **Constructive limitations section.** The authors explicitly bound hot-start dependence (lines 1301–1309), fixed dwell time (lines 1316–1317), absent timeliness objective (lines 1317–1321), reference-point asymmetry (lines 1322–1325), and missing learning-based baselines (lines 1325–1329).

## Weaknesses and required revisions

### 1. “Boundary” is stronger than the evidence supports
- **Location:** title (line 23); abstract (lines 57–58); conclusion (lines 1361–1363).
- **Issue:** The paper estimates a logistic midpoint \(N_{50}\approx 50\) but reports a very wide bootstrap interval, \([30,62]\), and only two informative density points (lines 888–895). Densities \(N=50,400\) are omitted (lines 653–654). The paper also shows hot-start conditioning (lines 1085–1113), non-binding resource constraints (lines 484–488), and a fixed 30 s dwell (line 1316). A “boundary” implies a portable threshold; the evidence supports a coarse transition band under specified assumptions.
- **Required revision:** Replace “boundary” with “transition,” “regime,” or “saturation range” where it appears as a general claim; retain “boundary” only if qualified as “empirical, within the tested configuration.” State explicitly that \(N_{50}\) is not a deployable threshold.

### 2. The “five-solver” comparison needs clearer editorial framing
- **Location:** abstract/solver overview (lines 122–125); GA-P-BL guard (lines 571–577); results (lines 730–735); discussion (lines 1233–1237).
- **Issue:** GA-P-BL is not an independent competitor because its never-worse-than-baseline guard makes it numerically identical to G-BL “on every scenario” (line 575). Describing five solvers may overstate the comparative evidence; the substantive comparison is among G-BL, G-SM, MOEA-2, and MOEA-3, with GA-P-BL acting as a control.
- **Required revision:** In the abstract/first solver description, call GA-P-BL a “single-objective GA control” rather than implying it is an independent fifth solver. Keep the explanation, because it is useful, but prevent reviewers from reading it as a straw comparison.

### 3. Hot-start conditioning should appear earlier in the editorial narrative
- **Location:** abstract caveat (lines 50–51); controls (lines 1085–1113); limitations (lines 1301–1309).
- **Issue:** The central saturation result is conditional on G-BL hot-start initialization. The abstract mentions this (lines 50–51), but the conclusion’s dense-regime statement (lines 1347–1353) could be read as a property of the MOEA formulations themselves. This matters for ASR readers interested in operational use.
- **Required revision:** Add one sentence in the conclusion restating that dense-regime convergence applies to G-BL-seeded MOEA runs and does not establish that unseeded NSGA-III independently recovers the same coverage.

### 4. Operational significance is modest and should be stated plainly
- **Location:** operational implications (lines 1239–1271).
- **Issue:** The operational guideline is conditional and splits by density: MOEA may help sparse requests, greedy methods suffice for dense monitoring (lines 1258–1264). That is useful but narrow. The paper does not establish how common each regime is for real single-satellite Stripmap tasking, so the practical impact remains abstract.
- **Required revision:** Add a short paragraph or sentence positioning the result as a planning heuristic under the stated assumptions, not a validated operations policy. No new experiment is required.

### 5. Title/abstract framing should foreground the actual contribution
- **Location:** title (line 23); abstract (lines 35–58).
- **Issue:** The title emphasizes “Balancing Profit, NESZ, and Geometric Resolution,” but the paper’s main finding is that this balancing is valuable primarily at low density and becomes nearly operationally irrelevant at high density under hot-started search. The result is scientifically valid, but the title sounds like a conventional multi-objective optimization paper rather than a saturation/mechanism paper.
- **Optional revision:** Consider a title that includes “scale-dependent,” “density-dependent,” or “saturation” (e.g., “Scale-Dependent Saturation of Physics-Aware Multi-Objective Scheduling…”). This is not mandatory; the abstract already states the saturation result.

## Minor presentation points
- Lines 45–47 compare MOEA-3’s \(f_1^*=0.85\) with G-SM’s task-count retention of “44%.” These are related but not identical quantities (normalized profit vs scheduled-task ratio). Clarify “85% of G-BL profit” versus “44% of G-BL tasks” to avoid a reader inferring both are on the same normalized scale.
- Line 158 uses “generic image-quality composite” for Wei2025MONP; ensure the citation accurately supports this distinction and is not over-reading the prior work.
- Lines 673–678 state no runtimes are reported because claims concern quality. For ASR operations readers, at least an order-of-magnitude runtime note (seconds/minutes per scenario class) would strengthen practical relevance even if not used in rankings. This is optional.
- The manuscript is dense but well organized. No major restructuring needed.

## Final comment
This is a sound, well-bounded manuscript with strong reproducibility practices. The main editorial work is to reduce claims from “boundary” to a qualified transition and to ensure the conditional nature of the dense-regime result remains visible from abstract through conclusion. I recommend **Minor Revision**.
