## Summary judgement
The SAR physics is largely correct and the platform is realistic (693 km, D = 12.3 m, B = 56.5 MHz, 18–47° incidence), and the manuscript's disclosure of what its NESZ proxy leaves out is unusually candid. The two genuine problems are structural rather than arithmetic: the paper's own ablation shows the incidence-angle content of the objectives is inert, so the "three-objective physics-aware" formulation is in practice coverage versus squint; and the headline quality gains are measured partly against a baseline whose geometry is pinned by construction to the visibility-window edge and partly through a mean-per-task metric that rewards dropping badly-geometried targets. Literature framing is fair to the cited works but treats "physics-derived" versus "attitude-angle proxy" as a sharper distinction than the shared inputs support, and the squint/Doppler/yaw-steering passage is not physically self-consistent. Contribution is real but is a measured operating envelope, not a new physics-aware scheduling paradigm: the value appears in sparse requests on wide-squint platforms, and the most operationally important finding (platform agility envelope dominates optimizer choice) is buried.

## Strengths
- **The NESZ modelling-choices paragraph is the best part of the paper.** "This plane-Earth range model omits the Earth's curvature and the antenna elevation-pattern roll-off; at the 47° edge of the tested incidence range (curved-Earth slant range 964 km versus 922 km plane-Earth) it underestimates slant range by roughly 4% and R³ by roughly 13%, and it suppresses the sinθ factor that a ground-resolution reference area adds to standard NESZ expressions." (§3.2, ~l414–419), followed by "Recomputing the sparse-regime comparison ... with the retained factor enlarges the MOEA-3 gain over G-BL from +40% to +94%" (~l433–437). Very few scheduling papers quantify the error of their own quality proxy; this one does.
- **Realistic, internally consistent platform.** 693 km sun-synchronous at 97.8°, D = 12.3 m, B = 56.5 MHz, bilateral 18–47° incidence, and the access band "cross-track half-width roughly ±608 km, a ground central angle of about ±5.5°" (§5.1, ~l723–726) are mutually consistent and give ρ_az ≈ 6.15 m, ρ_rg ≈ 5.3–8.6 m. A SAR engineer can therefore read the objective values as engineering numbers, not arbitrary scores.
- **Correct geometry chain, including a distinction many scheduling papers get wrong.** "off-nadir angle ξ = arctan2(sqrt(l_x²+l_y²), l_z), incidence angle θ (via the Earth-curvature-corrected mapping θ = arcsin((R_e+H)/R_e sin ξ))" and "squint angle ψ_sq = arcsin(|l_x| / R)" (§3.1, ~l334–341); the manuscript keeps satellite-side ξ/θ_elev separate from target-side incidence θ and states the difference and why ordering survives.
- **The right control for the third objective.** "we re-selected both fronts under a matched radiometric-aware rule, which gives a controlled margin of +4.9% (p = 2.3×10⁻¹¹, 47 of 50), against +0.05% (p = 0.86) for a radiometric-blind rule" (§6.4, ~l1194–1198), plus the Das–Dennis p = 4 control ruling out reference-direction density. This is exactly the experiment a sceptical referee would demand, and it is well executed.
- **The operational sensitivity chain is the paper's most actionable result.** "MOEA-3's f2 advantage shrinks from +28% at the 45° cap to +9% at 25° and +2.3% at 15°, while its coverage cost shrinks from 15% to 4% to ≈0% ... absolute geometry improves for every solver under the tighter cap (G-BL f2 rises from 0.295 to 0.405 to 0.448)" (§7.2, ~l1403–1411). This is a monotone, physically explainable boundary and deserves to be in the abstract.
- **Limitations are comprehensive** for a SAR referee: multi-pass/multi-satellite, dynamic arrivals, ScanSAR/Spotlight/TOPS, fixed orbit, hot-start conditionality, the unexplained variant-D S1 coverage reversal, resource caps, fixed 30 s dwell, no latency objective, small control samples, no learning-based comparison (§7.4, ~l1459–1512).
- **Citation hygiene.** The bibliography is metadata-complete with DOIs, and Romano2006 carries an explicit note that unverifiable fields were removed rather than guessed — rare and creditable.

## Findings

### R2-01 [MAJOR] The three-objective formulation is a two-objective one in the tested scenario family: the incidence channel is inert
- Quote: "Variant C (no incidence) produces no measurable change in any reported metric. This inertness is consistent with the window geometry: the elevation-plane incidence angle varies by only a few degrees across a visibility window (mean ~1.5° in the S1 scenarios), whereas the squint angle sweeps ~20–35°, so the θ_elev factor in f2/f3 acts as an approximately common scale within each window" (Physical-Objective Ablation, ~ablation l128–137). Table `tab:ablation` S1: variant A f2 = 0.376, f3 = 0.698, f1* = 0.850; variant C f2 = 0.375, f3 = 0.703, f1* = 0.845.
- Problem: contribution (i) in the Introduction sells "A three-objective formulation ... that decomposes image quality into closed-form NESZ (∝ R³) and geometric quality (sinθ cosψ_sq ...) objectives inside NSGA-III" (§1, ~l153–159). But deleting the incidence term from both objectives changes nothing measurable, at S1 or at any denser class. The measurable physics content therefore lives entirely in the squint variable — the same variable that drives f3 — and the "geometric" objective reduces to cos ψ_sq, i.e. azimuth resolution. A SAR referee will read the ablation table as evidence that the three-objective decomposition and the third objective are bookkeeping, not physics.
- Fix: state plainly in the abstract and §7.1 that the measured value of the formulation is concentrated in the squint channel, and either (a) present f2 as an azimuth-resolution objective with the θ term as a cross-window ordering device, or (b) show a scenario family where θ acts — the S5 along-track-spread class already hints at this ("the full-removal variant D is significantly worse than A in f2 (−11.2% ...)", ~ablation l161–163) and deserves a headline rather than a footnote.

### R2-02 [MAJOR] The mean-per-task quality metric rewards dropping badly-geometried targets, so the headline gain conflates "same tasks imaged better" with "different tasks imaged"
- Quote: "All quality objectives use the mean over scheduled tasks, making them independent of the number of scheduled tasks m = |S| and thereby producing a genuine 'quantity versus quality' Pareto trade-off." (§3.2, ~l365–367); headline: "re-optimising observation times buys large quality gains at a stated coverage price: +40% in radiometric quality at a 15% coverage cost, +33.7% in geometric quality at 14%" (§8, ~l1522–1525).
- Problem: independence from m is also the licence to raise mean quality by discarding acquisitions whose geometry is poor; the 14–15% coverage cost is the visible symptom of exactly that. No experiment holds the selected task set fixed. This matters quantitatively: f2 = sinθ_elev cos ψ_sq and within-window incidence moves only ~1.5° (§3.2, ~l424), so a large part of the +33.7% cannot come from retiming the same tasks. The paper's own variant C, which optimises squint alone, already reaches f2 = 0.375 against G-BL's 0.295 (Table `tab:ablation`) — nearly the full-physics value of 0.376.
- Fix: one timing-only arm (retime G-BL's selected task set, no reselection) and one selection-only arm (quality-ranked selection at fixed count). This single experiment converts the headline from a composite into a mechanism and would also let the paper say which channel carries the value — which is the missing result for a mission planner deciding whether to re-time or to re-task.

### R2-03 [MAJOR] The quality baseline is pinned by construction to the visibility-window edge, and a one-line heuristic beats the MOEA on the radiometric objective
- Quote: "Tasks are sorted by earliest start time t_start and greedily inserted into the schedule at their earliest feasible time. This earliest-time placement fixes each task's observation geometry implicitly through the window's incidence–squint profile rather than through any quality criterion." (§4.2, ~l612–616). Also: "The single-point squint-minimized heuristic (G-SM) reaches higher f3 than MOEA-3 in sparse scenarios but only at roughly half the coverage of G-BL (f1* ≈ 0.51)." (§1, ~l138–139). And under a tighter cap, "G-BL f2 rises from 0.295 to 0.405 to 0.448" (§7.2, ~l1410–1411).
- Problem: windows are generated with the squint cap as their boundary (C1), so earliest-start placement lands near maximum admissible squint. G-BL therefore images at f3 = 0.497 (S1) where G-SM reaches 0.722 and MOEA-3 0.698. No operational SAR planner images stripmap at the edge of the visibility window; near-zero-Doppler timing is standard practice. Read that way, "+40% radiometric quality" largely measures departing from a deliberately naive rule, and the honest claim is "MOEA-3 delivers near-G-SM radiometric quality at a fraction of the coverage cost". The tight-cap experiment makes the point sharper: a 15°-cap platform scheduled greedily attains higher absolute geometric quality (0.448) than a 45°-cap platform scheduled by MOEA-2 (0.393) — the platform's agility envelope dominates the optimizer choice. The paper states G-SM's numbers (it is not hiding them), but the abstract and conclusion still lead with the weaker comparison.
- Fix: add a zero-Doppler/squint-minimising baseline that keeps G-BL's task set (retime only) and quote the headline gain against it; keep G-BL as the *coverage* reference, not the quality reference. Move the 15°-versus-45° comparison into the abstract — it is the paper's most decision-relevant number.

### R2-04 [MAJOR] Squint, Doppler centroid and yaw steering are not mutually consistent; the achievable squint envelope is asserted without support
- Quote: "the Doppler centroid f_dc ≈ (2v/λ) sin ψ_sq (of order 10² kHz at the scheduled 16–24° squints, versus a PRF of order 10⁰–10¹ kHz, large enough that operational Stripmap systems employ yaw steering, with Doppler-centroid tracking handling the residual ...)" (§3.3, ~l521–525), together with the azimuth resolution used in f2, "ρ_az = D / (2 cos ψ_sq)" (§3.2, ~l385).
- Problem: a geometric squint of 16–45° produces a Doppler centroid two orders above the stated PRF. Yaw steering compensates the residual (Earth-rotation-driven) Doppler centroid; it does not make a 24° geometric squint processable at 1–10 kHz PRF. So one of two readings must hold, and the paper supports neither: if ψ_sq is the LOS offset from the zero-Doppler plane, the platform family cannot image at the scheduled squints and the entire 45°→15° envelope sweep is testing an unattainable regime; if ψ_sq is the residual antenna squint after steering, then the D/(2 cos ψ_sq) azimuth penalty in f2 is largely spurious, because steering removes the penalty. The same sentence also implies a 1–10 kHz PRF for a 56.5 MHz C-band instrument, and cites no source for either the PRF or the yaw-steering practice. §7.2 gestures at the issue ("or without Doppler-centroid compensation for large squints", ~l1414–1416), but by then the objective has already been built on it.
- Fix: define ψ_sq once and say which quantity enters f2, f3 and the C1 constraint; cite a source for the achievable squint envelope and for Doppler-centroid management at large squint; and run the envelope sensitivity over an attainable range, or state explicitly that the 45° setting is a computational what-if (as §3.3 already half-says for C1: "This bound is an experimental setting, not a claim about any specific platform's demonstrated operating mode or capability", ~l505–507).

### R2-05 [MAJOR] The NESZ proxy's suppressed factor is stated in two opposite directions, and "cannot reorder by construction" does not follow from monotonicity
- Quote: "it suppresses the sinθ factor that a ground-resolution reference area adds to standard NESZ expressions" ... "In fact the suppression is ordering-preserving by construction: the ground incidence is a monotone function of the same off-nadir angle ..., so dividing by sinθ reweights but cannot reorder acquisition geometries" ... "Recomputing the sparse-regime comparison of §... with the retained factor enlarges the MOEA-3 gain over G-BL from +40% to +94%" (§3.2, ~l418–437).
- Problem: three sentences, three different operations — suppress the factor, divide by it, retain it. In the standard radar-equation form over a resolution cell of area ρ_rg·ρ_az ∝ 1/sinθ, NESZ carries sinθ multiplying R³, so restoring the true form is a multiplication, not a division. Whichever was done, the monotonicity argument fails as written: cos³ξ decreases with ξ while sinθ increases with it, so a product of the two is not automatically order-preserving; it is order-preserving only for one particular placement of the factor (1/sinθ) and even then should be demonstrated over the stated 18–47° band, not asserted. A referee who tries to reproduce +94% cannot tell from the text which operation produced it. Separately, the real NESZ-versus-incidence curve is dominated by the elevation antenna pattern and by ambiguity noise; the paper discloses the pattern omission (~l414–415) but not that ambiguity-limited NESZ can be the binding term at the same geometries.
- Fix: print the exact standard NESZ expression being proxied, the exact algebraic step that yields +94%, and replace "by construction" with an explicit monotonicity statement for the tested band — or, cleaner, keep the proxy but report the recomputation as the primary number so the ordering argument is not load-bearing.

### R2-06 [MINOR] "Single-pass" collides with standard SAR usage
- Quote: "Throughout this paper, 'single-pass' denotes a single satellite operating over a two-orbit scheduling horizon (~200 min, at most one observation per target), not a single overflight." (§1, ~l81–82).
- Problem: in SAR, single-pass versus repeat-pass is the interferometry distinction; a two-orbit horizon of 30 s stripmap acquisitions is neither. The gloss is explicit, but the term recurs ("single-pass, single-satellite Stripmap setting", §7.4, ~l1461), and every recurrence re-activates the wrong reading.
- Fix: use "single-satellite, two-orbit horizon" throughout; keep one parenthetical definition and delete the term.

### R2-07 [MINOR] Quality indices are never expressed in physical units
- Quote: "Higher f2 indicates a smaller per-task ground-pixel-area factor (better geometric resolution)." and "Higher f3 indicates a lower per-task NESZ factor" (§3.2, ~l391, ~l406).
- Problem: a SAR reader needs metres and decibels to judge whether a gain matters. The paper does the conversion once for geometry ("roughly a 25% smaller per-task geometric area factor", §7.2, ~l1386–1388) and never for radiometry: 0.497 → 0.698 in f3 is opaque without a dB translation.
- Fix: add one sentence or one table column giving approximate ground pixel area in metres and the NESZ change in decibels for the S1 baseline→MOEA-3 comparison, carrying the existing proxy caveat.

### R2-08 [MINOR] The "independent-angle null" adopts the most negative of three baselines, and the abstract quotes only that one
- Quote: "A permutation null that shuffles the visibility-window-grid θ_elev and ψ_sq marginals within each density class gives r_null = −0.60 to −0.62 ... and Monte Carlo sampling over the uniform box gives r_MC = −0.28 ... We adopt the empirical-marginal permutation value as the factorized null" (§6.4, ~l1111–1119); "the envelope itself ... gives corr(f2,f3) ≈ −0.36" (~l1145); abstract: "r ≈ −0.62, close to the independent-angle null of −0.60" (Abstract, ~l52–53).
- Problem: the permutation null deliberately removes the real θ–ψ coupling induced by the LOS vector, so it is a constructed baseline; the physically meaningful reference for "how much conflict does visibility geometry itself carry" is the envelope value (−0.36). Quoting −0.62 against −0.60 in the abstract, and −0.36 only in the body, leaves the reader with the least interpretable pair.
- Fix: in the abstract, quote the schedule value against the envelope value (−0.62 vs −0.36) and add one clause saying the −0.60 null removes the geometric coupling on purpose.

### R2-09 [MINOR] Resource constraints are dimensionless placeholders, and the fixed 30 s dwell removes a central SAR quality lever
- Quote: "where E_max = 10⁷ and M_max = 10¹¹ (arbitrary units) and typical per-task consumptions are e_i = 5×10⁴ and m_i = 5×10⁸. Both caps therefore translate into the same ceiling of 200 concurrently scheduled tasks." (§3.3, ~l557–562); "the dwell time is fixed at 30 s, removing the dwell-versus-SNR trade-off that an agile scheduler could otherwise exploit" (§7.4, ~l1494–1495).
- Problem: both are disclosed, but the consequence is not carried forward: the dense-regime conclusion partly reflects a 200-task artefact (the paper says so at ~l566–570 but §7.1 and §8 do not repeat it), and dwell — the one lever that trades azimuth resolution against NESZ and dwell — is frozen, so the reported quality envelope is a floor rather than what an agile scheduler could reach. For a paper whose whole claim is that modest geometry changes matter, freezing the dwell knob deserves a sentence in the conclusion.
- Fix: restate the 200-task ceiling beside the dense-regime claim in §7.1/§8, and state explicitly that the fixed dwell makes the measured quality envelope a lower bound for an agile platform.

### R2-10 [MINOR] Two opposite sign conventions in the ablation table and the adjacent prose
- Quote: "Degradation is reported as Δ% = (A − V)/A × 100 ... negative values indicate that the variant outperforms the baseline on f1*" and "Percentages quoted for the quality metrics f2 and f3 in the text follow the direct relative-change convention (V − A)/A (negative values indicate degradation), the opposite sign of the Δf1*% column" (Physical-Objective Ablation, ~ablation l43–50).
- Problem: in the same table and the same paragraph a minus sign means "better" in one column and "worse" in the other. Referees and copy-editors misread tables of this shape.
- Fix: one convention (V − A)/A everywhere, flipping the f1* column's sign and the bold annotations accordingly.

### R2-11 [MINOR] The "physics-derived versus attitude-angle proxy" contrast is weaker than the text implies
- Quote: "in this literature imaging quality is a scalar attribute or coarse proxy rather than a decomposed physical objective" (§2.1, ~l242–244); "ChangZhou2025Memetic ... quality enters as an attitude-angle proxy attached to each observation opportunity" (~l238–240).
- Problem: this paper's f2 = sinθ_elev cos ψ_sq is itself a function of the same two attitude-derived angles; what differs is that the physics is carried by explicit closed-form expressions (c/(2B sinθ), D/(2cosψ), R³) decomposed into two separately optimised objectives rather than collapsed into one composite. As phrased, the contrast invites a "same inputs, different bookkeeping" rebuttal from precisely the authors whose work is labelled a proxy.
- Fix: state the distinction as decomposition into two separately optimised closed-form functions derived from the resolution and radar-equation expressions, and drop "physics-derived" where it implies a different class of input.

### R2-12 [MINOR] SAR operational constraints are absent from the scheduling model
- Quote: the model's constraint set is C1 (incidence and squint), C2 (attitude manoeuvre and non-overlap), C3–C4 (energy and memory) (§3.3, ~l488–570); no PRF/ambiguity switching, echo-window/duty-cycle, or downlink/data-volume constraint appears anywhere.
- Problem: in operational SAR planning, PRF and echo-window retuning between acquisitions, and downlink capacity, are frequently the binding constraints; the model pays the attitude-transition cost but not the radar-timing or data-flow cost. A SAR referee's first feasibility question — "could this schedule actually be commanded?" — is not answerable from the paper.
- Fix: add one paragraph (in §3.3 or §7.4) stating that PRF/echo-window and downlink are omitted, that the omission relaxes feasibility, and that reported coverage is therefore an upper bound relative to an operational planner's.

## Suggested references (candidate gaps only, no fabricated claims)
- **Achievable squint envelope and Doppler-centroid management for squinted stripmap.** The 45°→15° contraction is the paper's headline boundary, yet the only support for the operating mode is a textbook citation plus an uncited claim about yaw steering. A source on squinted-SAR geometry, Doppler-centroid ambiguity and steering practice would let the envelope be defended rather than declared.
- **Multi-mode SAR observation scheduling (stripmap / ScanSAR / spotlight / TOPS).** Mode choice is the natural next quality lever and the paper explicitly defers it; at least one anchor would keep the "beyond Stripmap" sentence from being an unsupported exclusion.
- **SAR-specific operational-constraint modelling in mission planning (PRF/ambiguity switching, duty cycle, downlink volume).** This is what makes the "arbitrary units" energy/memory caps legible and would let the dense-regime ceiling be discussed in radar terms.
- **Quality-aware / resolution-aware scheduling in optical EO (cloud- and GSD-driven).** The §7.3 transferability claim rests on the geometric-GSD analogy alone; a quality-aware optical scheduling reference would let the transfer be positioned rather than merely predicted.
- **SAR constellation scheduling with imaging requirements beyond the Kramer line.** The paper's negative existence claim ("no existing SAR scheduling method treats continuous, physics-derived quality functions as decomposed optimization objectives in their own right", ~l248–252) is hedged with "To our knowledge" and is fair given the cited set, but it is a broad claim and broader SAR-constellation scheduling coverage would firm it up or narrow it.
