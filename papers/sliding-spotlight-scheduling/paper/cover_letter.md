Cover Letter
============

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

I am pleased to submit my manuscript, "Resolution-Driven Scheduling of Agile SAR Satellites: From Fixed to Endogenous
Imaging Dwells," for consideration in Aerospace Science and Technology.

Satellite schedulers conventionally treat a task's imaging duration as a
fixed constant known before planning. For a sliding-spotlight SAR platform
this assumption is quantifiably false: the dwell is produced by the imaging
mode and the selected start time, and it feeds the inter-task transfer
constraint that decides what a single pass can carry. My manuscript makes
the dwell endogenous, carries the customer's resolution requirement into the
scheduler as a per-task hard constraint, and measures what tightening that
requirement costs in coverage.

Three features distinguish the submission:

1. A preregistered, frozen analysis: the nine-point demand grid, the three
   shape criteria and the caliber labels were registered before this scenario
   world's data were read, and the criteria are reported with their failures
   rather than re-tuned to the data.
2. Deliberately bounded claims: the per-pass capacity law is stated with its
   registered caliber but no number, because the serial bound has not been
   recomputed in the present scenario world; image quality is a platform
   capability and a reported reading, not an optimization target; and the
   conclusions are scoped to requirement sensitivity under a fixed mode assignment.
3. Auditable results: every headline reading is recomputed from the promoted
   artifacts by a verification script --- all nine grid points per family
   (curve values and the infeasibility status alike), the as-placed costs,
   the demand-satisfaction count, the retention, dip
   and per-seed displacement readings --- and each reading carries its
   caliber where it is stated.

The manuscript has not been published previously and is not under
consideration elsewhere. I approve the submission and declares
no competing interests. I believe the paper fits Aerospace Science and Technology's scope in
space-systems engineering and mission planning, and I thank you for your
consideration.

Sincerely,

Liu Shuhao
(corresponding author)
