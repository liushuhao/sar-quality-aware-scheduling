\begin{abstract}
Agile Earth-observation satellites that steer a single antenna across stripmap, sliding-spotlight, and spotlight modes are usually scheduled under an exogenous, fixed imaging duration. For a sliding-spotlight platform that assumption fails measurably: planning with the fixed library dwell --- the zero-squint value, the most fragile fixed-dwell convention --- violates the transfer constraint in 27--42\% of consecutive pairs on the dense families of this scenario set, and after repair the replayed plans land 7--30 points of served priority below the endogenous-dwell schedule, the span depending on the repair rule (its lower end from a rule added after the sweep). This paper makes the dwell endogenous: the mode parameters follow the required resolution, the delivered resolution equals it at the selected start by construction --- an identity the solver cannot violate --- and the stripmap antenna floor is the one place a finer requirement excludes solutions outright under this study's frozen mode assignment. Served priority is the only objective; image quality is reported, never optimised. Three scenario families sweep a frozen nine-point requirement grid under a frozen mode assignment: the dense family drops 26.0 percentage points to the next tighter point and 46.6 to the tightest feasible one (the stripmap floor accounting for 20.0 and 44.8 of those points), the all-stripmap control becomes infeasible at the tightest point, and the family without stripmap stays within 0.27 points of its as-placed coverage at every tighter point. Loosening recovers 4.15 and 2.36 points (3.98 and 0.93 paired per seed, respectively). Which mode a task receives decides what shape the cost of a requirement change takes; across the three families, density and mode composition covary by design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

Agile Earth-observation satellites steered by a single antenna can operate their synthetic aperture radar (SAR) in a continuum of imaging modes: stripmap, sliding spotlight, and spotlight are one physical mode family parameterised by where the beam's virtual rotation centre sits. Which point of that continuum a task receives is not a free stylistic choice --- it is fixed by the requested resolution --- the requirement, as this paper calls it from here on --- the scene, and the geometry of the pass, and it determines how long the satellite must dwell on the scene. Scheduling therefore cannot treat the imaging duration as an exogenous constant, as the classical agile-scheduling literature largely does \citep{lemaitre2002selecting,long2024deep,qin2024multisatellite,li2024earth}: in sliding-spotlight operation the dwell is produced by the imaging physics, it changes from task to task, and it feeds back into how many tasks a pass can carry and at what image quality. In that literature the resolution requirement, when it appears at all, is an attribute of the request that is settled before the schedule is built: the observation duration is a task attribute fixed in advance \citep{chen2024learning,qin2024multisatellite}, and on the SAR side the required resolution already fixes the synthetic-aperture time and steering span of a designed observation \citep{kim2020optimal}.

This paper studies that coupling on a single satellite in a regime of single-pass dense task clusters under true orbit geometry. Scheduling decisions select which tasks to serve and when to start imaging within each task's visibility windows; the mode and its parameters follow from the product requirement, and the dwell, the maneuver, and the image quality follow from the mode and the window. The density ladder is fixed at registration (Section~\ref{sec:families}). On this scenario set the coupling is measurable and large: tightening the dense family's requirement one grid step below the as-placed point costs 26.0 percentage points of served priority, and the all-stripmap control admits no feasible schedule at all at the tightest grid point (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

Two discipline risks shape the methodology as much as the physics does. First, a measured effect on coverage can be a property of the \emph{search} rather than of the \emph{scenario}: in this scenario set the greedy anchor already attains the best coverage found in this study in 258 of the 260 sweep units, so the coverage response reported here is produced by the mode and parameter adaptation rather than by the optimiser's ability to find it. Second, quality statements are only as good as their measurement conventions: several formally plausible formulations (an ambiguity sum that includes the principal image, a range resolution that varies per task, a squint-dependent routing resolution) are registered as not used, and the paper says so where it matters.

\subsection{Contributions}

The paper's contributions are three: the platform that carries the requirement into the physics, the mode-dependence of what a requirement costs in coverage, and the discipline that keeps both claims testable:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An enabling physics-to-scheduler platform} (the platform leg). A three-mode continuum formalisation (Section~\ref{sec:model}) that carries the scene-level dwell closures and the maneuver-coupled transfer feasibility into one scheduling loop: the imaging mode is resolved from the product requirement, the mode parameters follow the requirement where the mode has them --- so that the delivered resolution equals the requirement at the selected start by construction, an identity the search cannot violate rather than a constraint it could --- and the requirement excludes solutions in exactly one place, the stripmap antenna floor, where a finer order has no admissible mode. The dwell duration is a consequence of the requirement rather than a constant --- under a frozen mode assignment (Section~\ref{sec:axis}).
\item \textbf{The mode-dependence of what a requirement costs} (main result). Three scenario families swept over a frozen nine-point demand grid at a single search budget and under a frozen mode assignment: a floor step on the stripmap family, a non-monotone response on the 50-task family whose two dips are traced to the greedy anchor construction, tightening costs of 26.0--46.6 percentage points on the dense family against a 0.27-point plateau on the 50-task family, as-placed costs of 4.15 and 2.36 percentage points against each family's own saturation, and a fixed-dwell counterfactual that quantifies what the endogenous dwell buys (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
\item \textbf{A discipline that keeps the result falsifiable} (methodology). Shape criteria (S1--S3) registered before this scenario set's data were read --- the grid and the criteria are the pre-registered content, while the main-result form and the verdict-reading refinements were registered afterwards and are dated as such (Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}) --- and reported with their failures rather than re-tuned to the data; measurement-convention labels that keep the two quality conventions apart and keep the fulfillment ratio a reading; and an explicit separation of the search face from the construction face --- the anchor attains the best-found coverage in 258 of 260 units, so the coverage response is a construction result, not a search finding (Sections~\ref{sec:testdesign} and~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, the trade-off the study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum; the antenna-design question of resolution against swath is a sensor-layer consequence that this simulation does not model, and it is treated as a limitation with future work (Section~\ref{sec:scope}). The serial-bound reading of how many dwell-coupled tasks a pass carries is stated with a per-pass convention and without a number, because its serial bound has not been recomputed in this scenario set; it is carried as a scope statement (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) rather than as a result.

The move this paper makes is to take the resolution off the request and put it into the constraint set: the requirement is what the scheduler must satisfy task by task, the mode parameters are what carry it into the physics, and the coverage loss is what the paper measures. The claim is scoped to the literature surveyed below: the survey rests on the project's curated literature pool --- a converted and catalogued bibliography of the field plus an incremental paper-search sweep over the months preceding writing, whose nearest neighbours Section~\ref{sec:related} engages directly. What the tested coupling in fact produced in this scenario set is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}: the coverage--demand curves of the three families, the floor step and the two anchor-carried dips, the as-placed costs against each family's own saturation, and the demand-satisfaction count --- the platform's value is that each of these readings is registered and reproducible.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

This section positions the paper along three lines: sliding-spotlight SAR imaging (where the dwell physics lives), agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling (where the fixed-dwell assumption lives), and quality-aware scheduling (where image quality enters planning decisions).

\subsection{Sliding-spotlight and spotlight SAR imaging}

The sliding-spotlight family is standardly formulated as steering about a fixed virtual rotation centre. The concept has its lineage in the wide-swath sliding configurations of Currie and Brown \citep{currie1992wideswath} and the two-step focusing of hybrid stripmap--spotlight acquisitions \citep{lanari2001hybrid}; the TerraSAR-X extended chirp scaling processor is built on that geometry \citep{mittermayer2003sliding}, the baseband azimuth-scaling processor of Prats et al.\ handles both sliding spotlight and terrain observation by progressive scans (TOPS)\citep{prats2010processing}; in sliding spotlight the rotation centre lies beyond the swath, in TOPS it lies behind the sensor. Kim et al.\ derive the attitude-maneuver and synthetic-aperture time formulas for squint staring and sliding spotlight on the same geometry \citep{kim2021optimal}. Operational descriptions sometimes state the steering as a constant angular rate --- Zhang et al.'s Gaofen-3 (GF-3) account does so explicitly and describes it as a small-angle simplification of the same geometry \citep{zhang2018sliding} --- and processing work that pushes to high squint is likewise explicit that the linearised steering assumption breaks there \citep{guo2011extended,ren2025high}. This paper adopts the rotation-centre formulation throughout (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}); its scheduling windows reach squints where only the exact geometry is valid, which is why the two readings must not be mixed.

Two quantitative anchors from the imaging line matter to this study, and both are quoted as their own platforms' literature values. First, Kim et al.'s Table~2 defines a $\pm 12^{\circ}$ task envelope for its reference mission; the design envelopes registered in this paper --- $\pm 45^{\circ}$ for stripmap/spotlight and $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$ (a registered conservative value, a design choice) for sliding spotlight --- are separate fields and are never merged with the literature task domain \citep{kim2021optimal}. Second, Song et al.\ report for their sparse-imaging platform that the azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio is anti-correlated with the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) and degrades as the scan angle grows (by about 8.6~dB beyond $1^{\circ}$ of scan for conventional sliding spotlight, and about 15.2~dB for the sparse variant), alongside a range-ambiguity reduction of about 10.7~dB for the sparse mode \citep{song2025spaceborne}; as Section~\ref{sec:direction} shows, the direction of the ambiguity-versus-squint dependence in our model is the opposite of that literature anchor, and the contrast is a result of this paper rather than a contradiction to be smoothed over. The standard ambiguity and resolution relations otherwise follow the textbook treatment \citep{cumming2005digital}.

The dwell time itself is treated in the imaging literature as a deterministic function of mode and geometry --- Kim et al.'s synthetic-aperture time chain is exactly that \citep{kim2021optimal} --- but it stops at the imaging problem boundary: in the imaging references this paper draws on, the scheduling layer above it is not part of their treatment.

\subsection{Agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling}

The agile-scheduling literature is broad and mature: hybrid genetic and simulated-annealing planners for moving-target observation \citep{qin2024multisatellite}, deep reinforcement-learning planners for large mission-planning instances \citep{long2024deep,chen2024learning,zhang2025deep}, adaptive large-neighborhood search with learned guidance or with neighbourhood repair \citep{du2025satellite,he2018a}, constraint-programming formulations for super-agile platforms \citep{caleiras2026a}, memetic and multi-chromosome cooperative planners \citep{wang2024memetic,wang2024research}, and profit models in which the observation value depends on the interval between revisits \citep{li2024earth,peng2019agile}. Across the agile-scheduling formulations surveyed here, the dominant structural assumption is that the imaging duration of a task is an exogenous constant of the task, known before the scheduler runs --- the SAR-specific line below is the nearest neighbour, and even there the geometry is fixed upstream of the planner. The scheduler then packs fixed-length intervals into time lines, and whatever the objective --- profit, coverage, latency --- the duration never feeds back.

For an agile SAR platform in sliding-spotlight operation that assumption is false in a specific, quantifiable way: the dwell is produced by the mode physics (the tan-exact closure chain of Section~\ref{sec:dwell}), it varies per task with the assigned mode and the squint of the selected start, and it enters the inter-task transfer constraint that decides what else the pass can carry. This paper makes the dwell endogenous, carries the required resolution as a per-task constraint on that dwell, and measures what tightening the requirement costs in coverage.

\subsection{Quality-aware scheduling}

Image quality reaches the planning literature in three roles, and the difference between them and this paper is worth stating plainly. Image-quality research on the SAR side carries a substantial assessment literature --- fidelity metrics, quality checkers, and radiometric corrections applied to formed images \citep{cumming2005digital,mittermayer2003sliding,prats2010processing}. Imaging-mode design starts from a resolution requirement and trades it against swath \citep{song2025spaceborne}, and mission-level SAR planning computes the required synthetic-aperture time and steering span from the required resolution and the incidence angle \citep{kim2020optimal}. Multi-objective Earth-observation and SAR mission studies optimise more than one criterion explicitly alongside coverage or priority \citep{chang2021integrated,kim2019multi,kim2020optimal}; that line of work exists, it is not the line this paper takes, and nothing here claims its absence. What changes here is the \emph{placement} of quality. It leaves the objective set: the per-mode index of Section~\ref{sec:quality} states a platform capability and supplies the numerator of a reported fulfillment ratio.

\subsection{Positioning}

The three lines meet at a seam rather than at a gap, and the seam is narrow. The imaging line supplies the resolution-to-aperture chain and stops at the imaging problem boundary: a required resolution and incidence angle fix the synthetic-aperture time and steering span of a designed observation \citep{kim2020optimal}, and the sliding-spotlight processors deliver such a design at a fixed rotation centre \citep{mittermayer2003sliding,prats2010processing}. The scheduling line supplies the packing machinery for tasks whose duration is an attribute fixed before the schedule is built \citep{chen2024learning,qin2024multisatellite}. The quality-aware line supplies assessment conventions and mission-level weightings \citep{cumming2005digital,kim2019multi}. This paper puts the required resolution into the scheduling constraint set: the mode parameters follow the requirement where the mode has them, the resolution delivered at the selected start equals the requirement, and the coverage cost of tightening the requirement is measured on a frozen grid under a fixed mode assignment (Section~\ref{sec:model} and Section~\ref{sec:results}). Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:results} build that problem on a registered, preregistered footing --- the demand grid, the shape criteria and the convention labels are fixed before this scenario set's data were read --- and report what the tightening costs.

A recent SAR-specific line is the closest neighbour and is worth separating explicitly. Liu et al.~\citep{liu2025a} and Xin et al.~\citep{xin2024sar} plan SAR regional observation with weighted multi-objective and genetic-algorithm planners; Shen et al.~\citep{shen2024multistrip} plan multistrip stitching missions under an onboard energy budget; Li et al.~\citep{li2026efficient} generate visible windows and screen them for quality before any planning runs. Across these four, the imaging geometry is fixed upstream of the scheduler --- by the request, the strip design, or the generation-side screen --- and none carries a per-task dwell that responds to a per-task resolution requirement inside the scheduling loop. Two adjacent lines sharpen the placement rather than close it: in the staring and video-satellite line the observation duration is itself a planning quantity --- Cui et al.\ schedule multi-object staring imaging for a video satellite with the dwell entering the plan \citep{cui2018mission} --- but there the duration is a decision the planner sets, not a quantity the imaging physics derives from a per-task resolution requirement; and mode-aware SAR planners carry spotlight attitude profiles into the mission plan \citep{kim2020optimal} while still taking the mode itself as given upstream. \section{Problem Formulation and Scheduling Model}\label{sec:model}

This section formalises the observation geometry, the three-mode imaging library, the dwell-duration closures, the per-mode image-quality model, and the constraints and objectives of the scheduling problem.

\subsection{Observation geometry and imaging modes}\label{sec:geometry}

We adopt the X-band reference platform of Kim et al. \citep{kim2021optimal}: altitude $h=570$~km, inclination $45^{\circ}$, wavelength $\lambda=0.031$~m, azimuth beam width $\theta_a=0.36^{\circ}$, and a square scene of 5~km side length. The derived geometry is $D=\lambda/\theta_a=4.9338$~m (antenna azimuth length), aperture-centre slant range $R_0=775.4$~km, and inertial scene velocity $v_s=7578.05$~m/s. This is the platform inertial velocity, registered as the scene-traversal velocity of this scenario set rather than the beam-footprint ground velocity; it enters every dwell closure and the Doppler bandwidth as the single frozen value, so cross-mode and cross-squint dwell ratios are invariant to the choice, while absolute dwell durations and every reading that mixes dwells with velocity-independent constants (the coverage readings) or evaluates the ambiguity ratio at a fixed PRF carries the convention. Each task is a scene; the scheduled quantity is \emph{when} to image it, not \emph{whether} to reconfigure the payload.

The three imaging modes --- stripmap (STR), sliding spotlight (SLS), and spotlight (SPT) --- are treated as a single-parameter continuum over the position of a fixed virtual rotation centre \citep{kim2021optimal,zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}: writing $k$ for the rotation-centre parameter and $A=1-k$ for the sliding factor, $k\to 0$ places the rotation centre at infinity (stripmap, parallel antenna axis), $0<k<1$ places it below the surface beyond the scene (sliding spotlight), and $k\to 1$ places it at the target (spotlight). The registered operating point of this study is $k=0.797317$, i.e.\ a rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance $r_0=197\,119$~m $\approx 197$~km. We stress the symbol discipline that the two distances are dimensionally identical but semantically distinct: $r_0\approx 197$~km is the rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance, whereas $R_0\approx 775.4$~km is the aperture-centre slant range; their ratio is $r_0/R_0=0.254207$ (ratios are computed from the full-precision distances, not from the rounded values quoted above), and the two are never interchangeable (Fig.~\ref{fig:modes}).

The sliding factor: under the rotation-centre semantics adopted here, $A=r_0/(R_0+r_0)$ \citep{zhang2018sliding}; the form $A=1-R_0 k_\theta/v_g$, with $k_\theta$ the azimuth steering rate and $v_g$ the ground speed (Song Eq.~(1)) \citep{song2025spaceborne}, is a restatement within the same rotation-centre model --- for the steering rate that places the rotation centre at $r_0$ --- not an independent literature identity. The per-mode azimuth resolutions at zero squint are $\rho_{a,\mathrm{STR}}=D/2=2.4669$~m, $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SLS}}=A\,D/2=0.5000$~m, and $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SPT}}=\lambda/(2\beta_{\mathrm{span}})=0.3000$~m with $\beta_{\mathrm{span}}=2.9603^{\circ}$ the spotlight sweep span (library design constant).

Under the rotation-centre semantics the spectral compression factor is a constant at every squint. Presenting the Doppler rates in the aperture-range form,
\[
K_a=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda R_0},\qquad
K_c=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda\,(R_0+r_0)},\qquad
a_{\mathrm{res}}=1-\frac{K_c}{K_a}\equiv A,
\]
the $\cos^3$ factors cancel at beam-centre crossing, so $a_{\mathrm{res}}$ does not degrade with squint; the sliding-spotlight design azimuth resolution is the constant $A\,D_a/2$ \citep{zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}.

The registered squint domains are separate registered fields, listed apart from the literature task envelope of Kim et al.\ ($\pm 12^{\circ}$, their Table~2 \citep{kim2021optimal}): STR and SPT carry a design domain of $\pm 45^{\circ}$ --- for SPT the reachable start domain is narrower, because the formation-point clip $|\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}|+\beta_{\mathrm{task}}/2\le 45^{\circ}$ bounds starts to about $43^{\circ}$ at the finest order --- and SLS carries a registered conservative envelope of $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$. The SLS cap is a \emph{conservative design choice} --- antenna steering margin plus frozen-data continuity --- and not a physical boundary, with no analytic derivation claimed (the cap sits $0.14^{\circ}$ above a retired linearisation boundary of $26.7567^{\circ}$; the proximity is a numerical coincidence, not a physical relation): under the rotation-centre semantics $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A>0$ at every squint, so high-squint reachability is limited by the registered cap rather than by mode physics. Two extrapolation disclosures attach to the $45^{\circ}$ design domain: the azimuth-resolution projection correction (resolution-ellipse rotation) is outside the closed form, and the $45^{\circ}$ dwell readings of STR/SPT are extrapolated, because the planar tan-difference closure omits Earth curvature against the full-curvature $R_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).

\subsection{Dwell duration and visibility windows}\label{sec:dwell}

The dwell of a task is the synthetic-aperture time (SAT) at scene level: the time the beam needs to sweep the full square scene at the task's squint, where $W_a$ denotes the scene (square) size --- 5~km on the reference platform --- entering as a scene-length traversal term. The production closures follow the reference geometry's scene-level sweep-span construction \citep{kim2021optimal}, evaluated tan-exactly about the centre squint (the symmetric construction quantified against the exact chain below). For STR the scene dwell is the exact tan difference of the beam-edge crossing angles plus the scene traversal $W_a/v_s$; for SLS it is the symmetric construction of Eq.~(22)/(24) with the task-level sliding factor $A_{\mathrm{task}}=2\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/D$ in the denominator; for SPT it is the exact Eq.~(10)--(11) tan-difference with the task-level sweep span $\beta_{\mathrm{task}}=\lambda\sec\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$. The SLS and SPT closures are evaluated at these task-level parameters derived from the requirement (constraint C5, Section~\ref{sec:constraints}), so the delivered resolution equals the required resolution by construction and a tighter requirement spends more dwell; STR keeps its library-floor values, and the mode assignment itself is frozen under the demand scan (grandfathered, Section~\ref{sec:axis}). The production construction itself is a further approximation to the exact rotating-centre chain of the same source (the Eq.~(20)--(23) quadratic for the end squint, implemented and used here as an internal identity check): evaluated over the registered SLS envelope and the fine-tier requirement range, the symmetric construction deviates from the exact chain by at most $1.9\%$ ($+1.87\%$ at the envelope corner, $+0.31\%$ at the reference squint of $12^{\circ}$ with the sign depending on the requirement), and the registered scene term $W_a$ enters unscaled while the planar oracle applies the squint correction to it --- both semantics are carried with the closure. A planar closed form with the $\sec^2\bar\psi$ squint correction, $T_{\mathrm{SLS}}=(R_0\theta_a+W_a)/(A\,v_s)\cdot\sec^2\bar\psi$ (scene size inside the correction), survives as the test-time oracle: at the Kim reference squint of $12^{\circ}$ it deviates from the production chain by $+0.4185\%$, and the deviation grows to $+2.1\%$ at the SLS cap and $+5.3\%$ at $45^{\circ}$ (an extrapolated-domain reading, Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), which is what triggered the kill-test switch from the planar form to the tan-exact chain in the first place. A frozen window-tail pad of 1\% of the dwell is carried as a conservative margin on the production path; it is not claimed to bound the production closure's own squint error, which is not separately quantified here. Two provenance notes complete the closure. First, the Kim Eq.~(22)/(24) chain is a zero-Doppler-clamped (broadside) attitude construction; applying it at nonzero squint is a registered approximation, and the growth of the planar oracle's deviation with squint, quantified above, bounds the oracle's linearisation error rather than the production chain's --- it is evidence that the broadside-derived closure carries a modelling gap rather than numerical noise. Second, the resulting dwell feeds the inter-task transfer requirement (constraint C2, defined in Section~\ref{sec:constraints}) that determines pass capacity, which is why the deviation pattern and the frozen window-tail pad are carried with the constraint semantics (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}) rather than with the numerics. Figure~\ref{fig:dwelllaw} draws the resulting law: flat for stripmap, steepening for the two fine modes, with the sliding branch degenerating onto the stripmap transit at the floor.

The zero-squint anchors close an identity: $\theta_a/A=\lambda/(2\rho_{a})$ holds at the library design point ($0.006283185307/0.202683397006=0.031000000000$~rad $=\lambda/(2\times 0.5\,\mathrm{m})$) with a full-precision residual of $3.5\times 10^{-18}$. The surviving guard of this identity is a dedicated regression test in the duration-model suite.

Each task carries one or more visibility windows produced by ground-track pass enumeration over the scenario span; a window fixes the closest-approach epoch $t_{\mathrm{ca}}$, the per-window slant range, and the earliest/latest feasible imaging start. The decision variable $\tau\in[0,1]$ of a selected task is mapped onto the feasible start interval of its window (Section~\ref{sec:solver}). The squint at the selected start, $\bar\psi$, is measured at the window's geometry.

One scheduling-level geometric quantity needs a definition here because it enters the inter-task constraint: the maneuver angle between consecutive tasks $i,j$, $d_\psi=|\psi_i-\psi_j|$, is the difference of their start squints. This is a planar-convention \emph{lower bound} on the true attitude excursion: it omits the sweep \emph{during imaging} (the antenna keeps steering at $0.4233$--$0.6686\ ^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}$, i.e.\ $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ over a $4$~s dwell) and the start-squint difference omits the roll-axis slew between the two pointings, so the true off-vertical attitude excursion is understated by an unquantified amount. The full disclosure of the two-sided understatement and the margin-pad argument is given in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}.

\subsection{Image quality model}\label{sec:quality}

The per-task quality index is an inverse-pixel-area form whose readout is a non-normalised $\mathrm{m}^{-2}$ quantity, its range dependence carried as a relative $(R_0/R)^3$ factor rather than an absolute calibration,
\[
f_{q,i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}\,\rho_{a}(m_i)}\;g_T(m_i)\,\Big(\frac{R_0}{R}\Big)^{3},
\]
where $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is the ground-range resolution, $\rho_a(m_i)$ the zero-squint azimuth resolution of the assigned mode, and $g_T$ the dwell-gain ratio --- the mode's point-target integration time over the stripmap point-target integration time (exactly $1/A$ for sliding spotlight); no antenna gain enters it. The $(R_0/R)^3$ factor carries the range dependence of the radiometric term, and its derivation runs through the integration time, not the cell: two-way spreading falls as $R^{-4}$ while the coherently integrated energy grows with the integration time $T\propto R/\rho_a$ at fixed resolution, so a noise-equivalent backscatter referenced to a fixed-resolution cell scales as $R^{3}$; in this model $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ and $\rho_a$ are range-independent constants, so the factor is carried as a \emph{relative} normalisation without calibration, at $R=R_0$ the identity chain collapses to $f_q=(g_T/\rho_a)\cdot(1/\rho_{\mathrm{rg}})$ \citep{song2025spaceborne}. A scope note on the name: the index is dominated by its azimuth-resolution term, and no \emph{calibrated} radiometric quantity --- noise-equivalent sigma zero, signal-to-noise ratio, or radiometric resolution --- enters the index: the only radiometric-shaped factor is the uncalibrated relative range dependence above, and it is evaluated at each window's own slant range, so the index of a task depends on which of its windows is scheduled (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}); the ambiguity ratio enters separately as an admission constraint, not as a traded objective. ``Image quality'' in this paper therefore denotes spatial-resolution quality under a gain-shaped multiplier.

The index has two roles in this paper: it states a platform capability, and it supplies the numerator of a reported reading. As a capability statement, at the zero-squint library convention the per-mode constants are $0.8107$ (STR), $19.7352$ (SLS) and $54.8322$ (SPT)~$\mathrm{m}^{-2}$: magnitude ratios of $24.3\times$ (SLS over STR), $67.6\times$ (SPT over STR) and $2.8\times$ (SPT over SLS). Under the registered fixture two of these constants also admit a direct recomputation form: the stripmap and sliding-spotlight dwell gains carry the exact product identity $g_T(m)\,\rho_a(m)=D/2$, so with the held $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}=0.5$~m they are equivalently $D/\rho_a^{2}$; the spotlight gain is the tan-exact point-dwell ratio rather than the tier ratio, so its constant sits a fraction of a percent above that form. Because the squint factor cancels inside the gain term (exactly for STR and SLS, and to within $0.07\%$ for SPT across the full envelope), these constants --- not squint-dependent readings --- are what separates the modes on this axis, and they are properties of the mode library rather than results of a schedule. One construction choice of the index is stated with them: the explicit $1/\rho_a$ factor sits on top of a gain term that itself scales as $1/\rho_a$ (their product is the registered constant $D/2$), so the index deliberately weights azimuth resolution twice, and the magnitude ratios quoted above ($24.3\times$, $67.6\times$, $2.8\times$) are properties of this chosen index rather than radiometric capability statements.

The reported reading is a fulfillment ratio. For a served task it is the $f_{q}$ at the realised window and the selected start's squint, divided by the best $f_{q}$ the task could reach over all of its windows at zero squint, so the ratio is at most one. It is computed on the library convention, averaged over all tie-set members rather than read off the anchor solution, and reported as a reading with no optimization role. Its label travels with every number it states: \emph{[library convention $f_{q}$ $\mid$ per-task $f_{q}$ at the realised window and squint over the zero-squint optimum over all windows $\mid$ mean over all tie-set members $\mid$ reported reading, not an optimization target]}.

Three convention statements are frozen with this definition. First, $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of $0.5$~m (registered at $45^{\circ}$ incidence, scene-centre semantics); it is not recomputed per task, and the magnitude narrative carries a limitation (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). Second, $g_T$ is \emph{self-derived} and \emph{internally verified}: no external literature source carries the noise-equivalent sigma zero (NESZ) equation set for this mode family, so the verification route is the internal identity chain above (and its regression guard), and the absence of external cross-validation is disclosed. Third, the squint-dependent achieved resolution $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}(m,\bar\psi)=\rho_a(m,0)/\cos\bar\psi$ is registered for reporting; the router consumes the zero-squint constant, and the only bandwidth consumer of $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}$ is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$ --- there is no consumer of the achieved resolution in mode routing. The projection relation behind the anchor is $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}=c/(2\,B_{\mathrm{rg}}\sin\eta_i)$ with the range bandwidth $B_{\mathrm{rg}}$ frozen at the design incidence; the planar evaluation layer holds the incidence at that design value, which is why $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ enters the objective as a constant, with the incidence-band variation disclosed separately in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}.

The azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio (AASR) is defined with the ambiguity sum \emph{excluding} the \emph{zeroth} (principal-image) term,
\[
\mathrm{AASR}=\frac{\sum_{m\neq 0}\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G\big(f_a+m\cdot\mathrm{PRF}\big)\big|^{2}\,df_a}{\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G(f_a)\big|^{2}\,df_a},
\]
following the standard convention \citep{cumming2005digital}: keeping $m=0$ would place the principal image in its own numerator and force $\mathrm{AASR}\ge 1$ identically. The frequency-axis rescaling by $(1-K_c/K_a)\approx(1-k_\theta R_0/v_g)$, a flat-ground bridge between the $K_c$ and $v_g$ forms per the frozen formula registration, under rotation-centre steering is what couples the steering geometry into the ambiguity integral \citep{song2025spaceborne}. The displayed integral is Song's general form, with the absolute-centre shift $f_{ac}$ carried in the integration limits; the implementation integrates the same sums about the DC-relative coordinate, where that shift cancels and the absolute centre drops out (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). A footnote on the spotlight special case: Song et al.'s spotlight AASR \citep{song2025spaceborne} is the degenerate integral of the same form in which neither numerator nor denominator contains $f_a$ after the rescaling, which is why the SPT ambiguity in our model is squint-invariant. Integration convention: the ambiguity sum runs over the azimuth-frequency axis alone --- the two-way pattern enters as a function of azimuth frequency, with no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so far-ambiguous-pulse attenuation from the elevation plane is not modeled; the principal effect of that simplification falls on the absolute ambiguity level, and it is registered with the model caveats (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).

Admission uses a \emph{discrete} worst-case readout, \emph{not continuous} evaluation: the AASR of a task is evaluated at $11$ equally spaced \emph{in-window} samples and the maximum must not exceed the registered $-13$~dB admission threshold at generation time --- a deliberately permissive screening value: admission screens pathological geometry rather than certifying product-level image quality, and in this scenario set it binds on no task (Section~\ref{sec:families}). Feasibility statements in this paper inherit that convention --- a selection is feasible with respect to the generator's 11-point admission rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own.

\subsection{Constraints and objectives}\label{sec:constraints}

The decision vector of a scenario with $N$ tasks is $x=(s,\tau)\in\{0,1\}^{N}\times[0,1]^{N}$: a selection bit per task and a start-time fraction per selected task. The imaging mode $m_i$ is \emph{not} a decision gene: the pair (required resolution, scene size) is mapped to a mode by the library table, a \emph{system design input with product requirement semantics} --- the platform is told what product a customer requires --- and the mode assignment is frozen for the data of this paper (Section~\ref{sec:axis}). What the scheduler does inherit from the requirement is the mode's parameterisation: for sliding spotlight and spotlight the design parameters are functions of $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}$ (constraint C5), so the requirement reaches the dwell through the mode rather than through a decision variable. Joint mode-and-time allocation is future work.

Five constraint families apply.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 (geometry, generation side).} A selected start must fall inside a visibility window of the task whose node satisfies the serviceability evidence: \emph{every} node in the dense domain carries \emph{at least one} in-band transit over the span ($25/25$ nodes over 72~h, per-node reading taken on the registered domain construction).
\item \textbf{C2 (inter-task transfer).} For consecutive tasks in start-time order, the gap must cover $(1+0.25)\,T_{\mathrm{dwell},i}+t_{\mathrm{man}}(d_\psi,\omega_{\mathrm{start}},\omega_{\mathrm{end}})+t_{\mathrm{settle}}$: the $25\%$ pad multiplies the \emph{dwell} (Kim's own margin semantics, stated in four places in \citealt{kim2021optimal}), never the maneuver, and the settle term is an unpadded measured constant ($t_{\mathrm{settle}}=2.0$~s). Production evaluates $t_{\mathrm{man}}$ as a registered hybrid: the analytic model stands alone only where it reaches at least half of the measured calibration anchor ($1.84$~s, Kim scenario~1); below that trigger the per-transition maneuver bound falls back to the worst measured required maneuver time ($4.07$~s, pure maneuver, Kim scenario~2) --- a conservative substitution that raises a visible warning in the implementation. The conflict predicate uses the bare dwell: pads are scheduling margin, not physical occupancy.
\item \textbf{C3 (energy/duty).} Per-task duty is normalised against the mode-library envelope reference $T_{\mathrm{env,max}}=10.5830$~s (the largest feasible scene dwell at the envelope-edge squint, attained by SPT; a planar-closure reading at the $45^{\circ}$ edge, inheriting the Earth-curvature extrapolation of Section~\ref{sec:geometry}) with mode weights STR/SLS/SPT $=1.0/1.15/1.3$; the normalisation denominator is the largest weight times the envelope reference, $1.3\,T_{\mathrm{env,max}}$ (the C4 reference below omits this weight factor).
\item \textbf{C4 (solid-state memory).} Per-dwell bytes follow $\mathrm{PRF}\cdot T\cdot N_r\cdot b_q\cdot 2/8$ with $N_r=10\,000$ range samples and $b_q=3$ bits per component (TerraSAR-X-style block-adaptive quantisation), normalised against the same per-window reference as C3 (each measured window's own slant range, the convention registered for this scenario set) under the single production PRF of $3630$~Hz.
\item \textbf{C5 (required resolution, evaluation side).} Every selected task must be imaged at its required azimuth resolution $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}$. For sliding spotlight and spotlight the mode parameters are derived from the requirement at the selected start's squint $\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ --- $A_{\mathrm{task}}=2\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/D$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{task}}=\lambda\sec\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$ --- so the delivered resolution equals the requirement by construction; the identity is re-verified at every dwell evaluation against a $10^{-12}$ tolerance, and a violation raises instead of being scored. The claim is pointwise at that start: inside the imaging interval the delivered resolution follows $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/\cos\psi(t)$, so this paper does not claim a constant delivered resolution across a whole imaging pass. The stripmap mode (STR) delivers the library floor of 2.4669 m: it has no task-level parameter, so a requirement finer than the floor has no admissible mode for that task, and a tighter requirement there is a step at the floor rather than a continuous degradation.
\end{itemize}

Two structural facts about C3/C4 are disclosed as design semantics rather than discovered: both budgets are \emph{structurally} \emph{never binding} inside the squint envelope (their normalised values cannot exceed 1.0 there, so the binding channels are C2 and interval conflicts), and the task priorities that drive the objective are drawn uniformly at random as $\mathrm{randint}(1,10)$, i.e.\ they carry \emph{no physical} provenance --- they are demand-side bookkeeping, not measured value. The non-binding property is itself the product of a dated design decision, not an accident: the normalisation reference was redefined on 2026-10-20, on the day the sweep ran, from the mode-library constant dwell to the per-task envelope-maximum task-law dwell, after the task-law dwells were measured to exceed the library reference by up to $1.2008\times$ on the 50-task family (theoretical worst $1.274\times$) and had produced false violations; the redefinition removes those violations by construction, and both the date and the rationale are part of the record. Across the five constraint families, the set that actually binds in this scenario set is exactly two: the C2 transfer chain and the stripmap floor. C1 filters at generation time, C3/C4 are structurally slack inside the envelope, and C5 is a raise-guarded identity rather than a scored constraint; the inventory above is the registered model, not a claim that five mechanisms bind.

The problem maximises the served priority fraction $f_1=\sum_{i\in S}p_i/\sum_{i}p_i$ over the selections $S$ that satisfy C1--C5. Coverage is the only objective. The search optimises one criterion, coverage; a front is a tie-set on coverage, and the quality index of Section~\ref{sec:quality} appears only as the fulfillment reading. The search method is described next.

A quality aggregate traded against served priority would be the conventional objective choice in this literature, so this paper's different choice is stated explicitly. This is not a re-parameterisation of the same problem: the search returns a tie-set instead of a front, and the quality index moves from a decision criterion to a measured property of whatever the scheduler serves. The change is registered as an amendment to the project's preregistration, which vacates the quality-headroom estimand and registers the coverage--demand curve of Section~\ref{sec:axis} as the main result form.

\subsection{The demand-tightening axis}\label{sec:axis}

The experimental axis of this paper is external to the scheduler. A scalar $s$ multiplies every task's required resolution, $\rho_{\mathrm{req},i}(s)=s\,\rho_{i}^{\mathrm{req}}$, with $s=1$ reproducing the requirement as placed and $s<1$ a tighter requirement; nine values are frozen, $s\in\{0.8,0.9,1.0,1.1,1.2,1.3,1.4,1.5,2.0\}$. Scaling touches the requirement only: the scenario population --- geometry, task set, and visibility windows --- is not regenerated, so every point of the grid is one scenario set seen through a different requirement. Terminology: \emph{requirement} names the per-task ordered resolution and \emph{demand} names that same requirement scaled by $s$ across the task book --- the demand grid and the requirement grid are one object --- and \emph{coverage} is the served priority fraction $f_1$ of Section~\ref{sec:constraints}.

The axis is swept under a frozen mode assignment. Which mode a task receives is not re-derived as the requirement tightens: the assignment is the one the scenario set was generated with, inherited unchanged (a dated comparability decision in the project's design record). The reason is comparability --- re-cutting the mode mix at every grid point would confound the coverage response with a routing change --- and it fixes the scope of every conclusion that follows. The results are requirement sensitivity under a fixed mode assignment, not a general statement about how coverage trades against resolution when a router is free to move tasks between modes; the experiment that would answer the general question is a different one and is not run here.

The mode assignment matters most on the stripmap family, because that is where the floor sits. A family whose tasks are all stripmap cannot respond to a tighter requirement continuously: the delivered resolution is the antenna floor, so the family either serves at the floor or finds no admissible mode. The all-stripmap control family R1C meets that floor inside the frozen grid --- at $s=0.8$ the family has no feasible individual in 10 of 10 seeds --- and that outcome is an infeasibility record, not a measured coverage of zero. The two statements are kept apart everywhere in this paper: an infeasible grid point is not a point on the curve, and it is never plotted, averaged, or quoted as one.

Three different mode rules are in play in this project, and a reader should not mistake one for another. (i) The admission path routes a requirement to the coarsest library tier whose zero-squint resolution meets it (the coarsest-sufficient tier routing); that rule consults the requirement and the library alone, one tier per task, with no scene-size test. (ii) The registered selection policy adds the feasibility test ($A_{\mathrm{task}}\in(0,1)$, the mode envelopes, the spotlight footprint bound, the ambiguity gate) and a two-key rule on scene size --- spotlight inside the footprint, sliding spotlight beyond it, stripmap at the coarse end --- and is the policy registered for narrative and for future families (the same dated design record). (iii) The data of this paper follow neither of those at run time: they carry the grandfathered assignment described above. Where this paper says that the mode follows the requirement, it means that the parameters of the \emph{assigned} mode follow the requirement, not that the assignment itself changes.

Coverage is measured on this axis as a reading: for each family and each grid point, the median over ten seeds of the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, at a single search budget. Three shape criteria are frozen against the curve --- monotone non-decreasing medians across the grid, a saturation arm ($s=2.0$ at least $s=1.5$), and a tight arm ($s=0.8$ below $s=1.0$) --- and they are inherited unchanged from their earlier registration rather than tuned to this scenario set's data. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports them as measured, failures included. Coverage admits no pass/fail verdict here: every unit's front is a tie-set carrying one coverage value, so there is nothing for a verdict to decide, and the curve is reported as a reading.

\section{Solving Approach}\label{sec:solver}

In scheduling-theory terms the problem of Section~\ref{sec:model} is a single-machine selection-and-scheduling problem with multiple time windows per task, start-time-dependent setup times, and start-time-dependent processing times: the satellite is one resource, each task carries visibility windows, and the inter-task transfer of constraint C2 charges a maneuver time that depends on the squint of \emph{both} endpoints' selected starts --- a setup that is start-time-dependent (both endpoints move with the start decisions), the strictly harder generalisation of the classical sequence-dependent setup. The imaging dwell adds the second dependence: because the dwell follows from the mode and the squint of the selected start, a task's processing time is a non-monotone function of where it is started within its window (largest away from the closest-approach squint zero), with window-choice discontinuities --- a start-time-dependent processing time whose per-window shape is U-shaped rather than the monotone linear form studied in the time-dependent scheduling literature \citep{gawiejnowicz2009equivalent,gawiejnowicz2015structural,he2019time}, so none of those structural results transfer as statements about this problem. Nor does the dwell behave as a controllable processing time \citep{shabtay2007survey}: the scheduler does not buy a shorter dwell at a cost --- the mode and the requirement derive it. Even the fixed-duration core already embeds orienteering (select a value-carrying subset under traversal costs), hence NP-hardness; no further complexity claim is made in this paper: no hardness proof or approximation result is attempted beyond that containment, and the evolutionary search of this section is the measurement instrument through which the registered scenario set is read. Bounding methods for the fixed-dwell agile-observation problem exist \citep{habet2010bounding}; how they transfer when the processing time is endogenous is an open question this paper does not address.

\subsection{Encoding and evaluation}

The solver is a single-objective evolutionary search over the chromosome of Section~\ref{sec:constraints}: a selection bit and a start fraction $\tau\in[0,1]$ per task --- no mode gene, since the mode is fixed by the product requirement --- so the densest real-window family (R3H, defined with the scenario families in Section~\ref{sec:families}) encodes its 99--100 admitted tasks (of 100 drawn) over 731--779 enumerated windows as $2N$ decision variables with $N=99$--$100$; at the coarsest end the control family (R1C, 20 drawn and 20 admitted) encodes 40. Decoding projects $\tau$ onto the union of feasible window intervals of the admitted windows: a served task is feasible with respect to the generator's discrete 11-point admission rule (Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}), and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own; feasibility statements in the results are relative to that generation-side convention, not to an independent physical guarantee (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}).

\subsection{Anchor-seeded initialization}

The initial population contains the greedy anchor: a bounded single-pass re-timing repair of the all-selected schedule. The seed is what makes the $f_1$ normalisation meaningful --- every front carries a solution anchored at the served-priority reference --- and its injection into the population is checked by a dedicated cross-check that once exposed spurious violations from time-inverted index pairs, which is also why the evaluation loop sorts by start time before charging the C2 chain.

\subsection{Search operators and selection}

Variation applies simulated binary crossover ($\eta=15$, per-variable probability $0.9$) and polynomial mutation ($\eta=20$) uniformly over the real-valued chromosome --- the selection bits are ordinary real genes decoded by a $0.5$ threshold, so no separate binary operator acts on them. Because the problem carries one criterion, the non-dominated sorting of the standard machinery ranks by that single value alone \citep{deb2002a}, and the survivor set of a unit is a tie-set on coverage rather than a trade-off front: the search returns the best-covered selections it reaches and the tie-set records their common coverage (Section~\ref{sec:quality}). The search runs under a fixed random seed per unit, single-threaded, with manifest task-list order everywhere (no set or dictionary iteration order may influence a run), so every unit is bit-reproducible.

\subsection{The measure normalisation of $\tau$ and its implicit bias}

One parameterisation choice is disclosed as intentional. A start fraction $\tau$ indexes position within the \emph{feasible measure} of a task's window union, not within wall-clock time: decoding is the measure projection of Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}. Because the projection is measure-normalised, a uniform grid on $\tau$ samples wider windows more finely --- the parameterisation carries an implicit weighting toward wider windows. This bias is registered as a deliberate design decision: it is not part of the main specification or its preregistration, and its registration is carried in the project's dated design-amendment record. No selection mechanism in the loop undoes the weighting; it is part of the search design, and every reading in Section~\ref{sec:results} inherits it.

\subsection{The search face and the construction face}\label{sec:faces}

The response this paper reports has two faces, a search face and a construction face, and the separation between them is measured rather than assumed. Over the 260 units that produced an artifact (nine demand points $\times$ three families $\times$ ten seeds, less the ten units of Section~\ref{sec:floor} that exit with the no-feasible-individual code), the greedy anchor already attains the best coverage found for the unit in this study in 258; the search exceeds its own anchor in exactly two units --- the control family R1C at the as-placed point, seeds 100 and 105 --- by $+0.0427$ and $+0.0463$ of the served priority fraction. That is a statement about where the coverage response comes from, not a statement about solver quality. That the deterministic anchor attains the best-found coverage in 258 of the 260 units is itself a finding: the selection problem is, in this scenario set, essentially solved by a single construction pass --- which is what makes the coverage response a construction property rather than a search artifact. The chromosome can do only two things: move a start inside the feasible measure of its window union, and drop tasks from the selection. Which tasks remain serviceable at a given requirement is therefore decided before the search runs, by the mode assignment and by the parameter adaptation that carries the requirement into the dwell; the search's job is to reach the level that admission sets. Within this scenario set the anchor is what reaches it, and the two units where the anchor does not are recorded rather than smoothed. The consequence for reading Section~\ref{sec:results} is direct: the coverage differences between demand points are a property of the construction, and no statement in this paper attributes them to the optimiser.

\section{Experimental Design and Setup}\label{sec:testdesign}

This section describes the scenario set, the family and convention system, and the preregistered test design.

\subsection{Scenario construction}\label{sec:scenarios}

The scenario set is built from \emph{real ground-track transit enumeration}, not from an arbitrary orbit phase point: visibility windows come from enumerating true ground-track passes over the scenario span, with a 15~s scan step, gap merging at 300~s (a registered band parameter, invariant across the registered 150/600/1200~s sensitivity band, not a physical lower bound), and 33-point edge refinement. The registry's own ledger records why: under the earlier phase-point pass rule only 0 of 8 clusters were serviceable after the incidence fix, against 6 of 8 under true enumeration. The demand mix itself --- resolution requirements and scene sizes --- is a registered synthetic template parameter, not a measured campaign profile; the priorities that drive the objective are uniform random integers with no physical provenance (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}), so the mode composition of the served set reflects the template's requirement distribution. The template draws each task's resolution requirement from two frozen tiers --- a coarse tier spanning $[2.5, 3.0]$~m and a fine tier spanning $[0.3, 0.6]$~m --- so the stripmap floor of $2.4669$~m (Section~\ref{sec:quality}) sits just below the coarse tier, and the tight end of the demand grid scales requirements below what the floor's mode can serve; Section~\ref{sec:knobs} shifts the tier multiplicatively and watches the floor step move to the predicted cell.

Orbit propagation is two-body only --- no $J_2$ or atmospheric terms --- so the registered repeat figure is the \emph{simulated} ground-track repeat of 14.97 orbits $\approx$ 23.93~h (one sidereal day over the two-body period); the figure that includes $J_2$ nodal drift describes the physical orbit and is deliberately not the one this simulator produces. The surface normal used in incidence computations is an \emph{approximate} normal: its maximum deviation from the true ellipsoidal normal is $0.125^{\circ}$ (at ${\approx}30^{\circ}$ latitude; $0.109^{\circ}$ at the dense-domain centre), with no effect on band-boundary judgements at the registered margins. The dense domain is a 1000~km square centred at 20°N/0°E, a $5\times 5$ node grid at 250~km spacing with per-cell target jitter, a 3-day span in coordinated universal time (UTC). The serviceability criterion is per-node: every node carries at least one in-band transit within the span, measured 25/25; the literal best-incidence reading of the dense-domain constraint fails at the centre node (near-nadir passes exist there but yield no in-band window), which is why the operative criterion is the per-node one. The $25/25$ reading is a construction-time measurement on the full-span domain; the manifest ledger stores aggregate admission counts, not per-node columns. Figure~\ref{fig:scenario} shows the resulting set: target geometry, mode composition, and passes per task.

The AASR admission criterion is evaluated per family, and the two families differ by design. Synthetic M-family windows are admitted by a window-edge single-point readout (the AASR at the window edge squint only), whereas the real-window R families sample the AASR at 11 evenly spaced in-window instants ($\mathrm{lo}+(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})\,j/10$, $j=0,\dots,10$) and admit the window only if the worst of the 11 samples meets the threshold. A sample outside the mode envelope contributes an exclusion, so it can never mask an unserviceable window. The window-edge single-point rule \emph{underestimates} the in-window worst case by about 4~dB, so the two admission rules are stated per family wherever admission is discussed; the R-family rule is the discrete in-window worst-case convention that all feasibility statements in this paper inherit (Section~\ref{sec:quality}). This sampling is \emph{discrete}, \emph{not continuous} evaluation: a window admitted under it is feasible with respect to the 11-point rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own. Two further conservative narrowings bound the admissible window set and are carried with the same convention: a pass is not admitted when the scene along-track footprint exceeds the beam-limited bound $R_0\,\theta_a$ at the closest-approach slant range of that pass (binding for SPT), and the envelope mask admits starts only inside a $1\%$ inward guard of the mode envelope, within a $\pm120$~s span around the pass closest approach, with each admitted mask run inset by one geometry sampling step (${\approx}1$~s) per end. Across the 30 released R-family manifests the generation ledger records zero tasks removed by the AASR threshold (removals: aasr 0, footprint 0, below-finest 0; no-window 15), so the threshold is part of the admission convention without ever binding in this scenario set.

\subsection{Families and conventions}\label{sec:families}

Three real-window families form the experiment matrix, and their sizes are stated in the two numbers the registered density ladder fixes, the tasks drawn and the tasks admitted (serviceable): R1C (20 tasks drawn, 20 admitted, 145--162 windows) is the all-stripmap control; R2F (50 drawn, 46--50 admitted, 337--391 windows) and R3H (100 drawn, 99--100 admitted, 731--779 windows) are the dense-cluster treatments. Counts are stated as intervals over the ten seeds of each family; a single-seed value is not a family constant. Task counts follow the registered density ladder $N=20/50/100$; R4H ($N=500$) was retired at registration because the single-transit STR pass-count scaling exceeds the registered span at $N\ge 150$ (the retirement rationale is registered; no capacity number is asserted anywhere in this paper, since the serial bound has not been recomputed under the present real-window scenario set).

The mode composition of each family is inherited from the grandfathered assignment of Section~\ref{sec:axis} rather than re-derived at run time: every R1C task is stripmap, R2F routes its tasks between SLS and SPT and carries no stripmap task, and R3H mixes all three modes. That composition sets the scale of the quality index a family can reach (Section~\ref{sec:quality}), but it is a property of the demand template that generated the scenario rather than of any schedule, and no reading in this paper compares index levels across families. The control family's served fraction saturates at 1.0 from the as-placed point upwards; the saturation is anchor-construction-carried (Section~\ref{sec:demand}) and is read descriptively, never as a performance result.

The artifacts are the released artifact set of this scenario generation (measurement conventions v5), and they are not a refresh of the previous generation but an explicit re-judgement. A registered decision made the sliding-spotlight and spotlight parameters follow the requirement per task (constraint C5), so the delivered resolution at the selected start equals the requirement by construction; that changes what is admissible at the data level rather than merely how a reading is labelled. The released set re-runs the sweep over the same frozen scenario population --- the task set, the visibility windows and the mode assignment are carried over unchanged, so the demand axis is the only thing that moves --- at a single registered budget of 40/60 population/generations. The artifact set carries its own lineage --- parent generation, frozen code state, and adjudication --- in its manifests, and every reading in Section~\ref{sec:results} is recomputed from those artifacts by the manuscript-number checker before the section is read as a result.

The R-family f$_q$ is \emph{not normalised}: the per-window slant-range geometry expands the units scene-dependently, so index levels are incomparable across families and are never pooled into a single span or compared across families. The synthetic M families remain available as a controlled reference; their one still-valid artifact is annotated, wherever cited, as a version-2-convention controlled reference whose output provenance is unregistered, and no M-family number is mixed with R-family numbers in this paper.

\subsection{The demand scan and its frozen criteria}\label{sec:hypotheses}

\paragraph{The scan} The experiment is an external scan rather than a hypothesis test about a control: a scalar $s$ multiplies every task's required resolution (Section~\ref{sec:axis}), nine values of $s$ are frozen, $s=1.0$ reproduces the requirement as placed (the as-placed point), and each grid point is read as the median over ten seeds --- ten independent scenario draws, each solved once, with the same seed driving both the scenario draw and the search, so scenario-sampling noise and search noise are not separated by the design --- of the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, at the registered 40/60 budget. The scenario population is not regenerated between points, so within a seed the nine readings are nine views of one scenario set through nine requirements. The search-noise half of that confound is bounded by the construction face: the anchor already attains the best-found coverage in 258 of the 260 sweep units, so the coverage response is carried by the construction rather than by solver stochasticity (Section~\ref{sec:faces}).

\paragraph{Three criteria, frozen before this scenario set's data were read} Three shape criteria are registered against the curve and are inherited unchanged from their earlier registration rather than tuned to the data. They are labelled S1--S3 here to keep them apart from the constraint families C1--C5 of Section~\ref{sec:constraints}. S1 asks that the medians be monotone non-decreasing across the grid; S2 is the saturation arm, the median at $s=2.0$ at least the median at $s=1.5$; S3 is the tight arm, the median at $s=0.8$ below the median at $s=1.0$. A cell whose ten seeds all exit without a feasible individual enters the criterion evaluation as a zero-coverage median, and it is the tight arm (S3) and the monotone arm (S1) that read that cell; the saturation arm (S2) compares the two loosest points and does not. The zero-coverage entry is a recorded outcome rather than missing data, and the reporting discipline of Section~\ref{sec:floor} keeps such a point off the coverage curve and never averages it into one. They are the registered content of the preregistration amendment that also vacated the quality-headroom estimand of the earlier model generation and registered the coverage--demand curve as the main result form (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}). Their verdicts are reported as measured, failures included, and the criteria are not re-tuned after the fact: a failed criterion is a result about this scenario set, not a defect in the criterion to be edited away. The registration timeline is part of the record and is stated exactly: the nine-point grid and the three criteria were registered before this scenario set's data were read; the amendment that registered the coverage--demand curve as the main result form is dated 2026-10-21, after the sweep ran on 2026-10-20, so the headline curve is a post hoc descriptive reading and the pre-registered content is the grid and the criteria themselves. The refined reading of the criteria verdicts (an infeasible cell is undefined; the tight arm reads pairwise per-seed deltas against a one-priority-unit tolerance) is likewise a post hoc refinement, registered 2026-10-23 after the 2026-10-22 external review; the frozen criteria text is not edited. The one-priority-unit tolerance is one count of the priority numerator, $1/\sum_i p_i$ of the scenario --- the smallest nonzero priority movement --- which, at the median scenario of each family, is 0.89 (R1C), 0.38 (R2F) and 0.19 (R3H) points at the registered task counts; across R1C's ten seeds the unit ranges 0.76--1.08.

\paragraph{What the amendment retired} The earlier design carried an effect size, a power calculation and a power curve, all of them calibrated on the quality headroom of a front over its anchor. That estimand does not exist in this model --- a unit's front is a tie-set on coverage, so there is no quality quantity to be gained --- and the power figure is retired with it rather than reproduced: a power curve about an estimand this paper does not report would be a number in search of a claim. Nothing in Section~\ref{sec:results} depends on it.

\paragraph{One budget, stated} Every point of the grid is read at the single registered budget of 40/60; no grid-wide budget ladder is swept for this scenario set, and no frontier-richness statement in either direction is made from these artifacts. The wall-clock cost of one unit at that budget ranges from 1.3 to 23.3~s across the released sweep log (single-threaded; median 5.6~s for the 20-task control, 11.1~s for the 50-task and 17.8~s for the 100-task family on the registered workstation), so the full 270-unit sweep with the counterfactual arm is a single-afternoon computation.

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{sec:results}

All readings in this section are taken from the released artifacts at measurement conventions v5 (Section~\ref{sec:families}) and are cross-checked against the reading ledger that stores each of them with its unit, convention label and source path. The grid is the nine-point demand scan of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}, every point read at the registered 40/60 budget.

\subsection{The coverage--demand curves}\label{sec:curve}

The result of the paper is the coverage--demand curve: the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, median over ten seeds, as a function of the demand-tightening factor. Three families produce three different shapes, and the difference between the shapes is the result rather than a nuisance variation to be averaged away.

The 100-drawn / 99--100-admitted family is the one the tightening actually reaches. Its median coverage falls monotonically with the tightening: 0.4827 at $s=0.8$, 0.6884 at $s=0.9$, 0.9485 at the as-placed point $s=1.0$, then 0.9766 at $s=1.1$, 0.9812 at $s=1.2$, 0.9882 at $s=1.3$, 0.9882 at $s=1.4$, 0.9900 at $s=1.5$ and 0.9900 at $s=2.0$ (family R3H). Tightening the requirement from the as-placed point to the tightest point that still has a feasible individual costs about half of the served priority mass; loosening it from the as-placed point to the coarsest point buys back under five points. Most of the response therefore sits on the tight side of the as-placed point, which is the side an operator would actually consider. Table~\ref{tab:modes} decomposes how much of that tight-side response is the stripmap floor rather than the dwell mechanism. The floor-exclusion upper bound --- every non-stripmap task servable, each stripmap task servable only if its scaled requirement still reaches the $2.4669$~m floor, priority-weighted and medianed over seeds --- already caps the family at 0.748 at $s=0.9$ and 0.501 at $s=0.8$: the floor accounts for 20.01 of the 26.01-point drop to $s=0.9$ and for 44.78 of the 46.58-point drop to $s=0.8$, and the residual gap between the bound and the observed medians (6.0 and 1.8 points) is the schedule falling short of a bound that is itself floor-driven. The three families covary in mode composition as they do in task count --- the control family is all stripmap, the 50-task family has no stripmap task, and the dense family is mixed --- which is why the same tightening reads as a floor step, a plateau, and a floor-dominated fall (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}; Section~\ref{sec:families}).

\subsection{The as-placed point, the floor step, and the cost of a tighter requirement}\label{sec:asplaced}

Tightening is where the cost concentrates: from the as-placed point to $s=0.9$ the median coverage drops 26.0 percentage points for R3H and 46.0 for R1C, and to the tightest feasible point 46.6 for R3H, while R2F holds within 0.27 points (0.00 at $s=0.8$).

Every cost in this subsection is measured against the family's own saturation point --- the first grid point at which its median reaches the family's maximum --- and never against another family or another generation. Every cost is a within-family quantity. R3H pays a coverage cost of 4.15 percentage points at the as-placed point against its own saturation at $s=1.5$, the first point at which its median reaches 0.9900. R2F pays a coverage cost of 2.36 percentage points at the as-placed point against its own saturation at $s=2.0$, where its median first reaches 1.0000. Both costs also have a paired per-seed form --- the median over seeds of each seed's own saturation-minus-as-placed difference --- which reads 3.98 points for the mixed family and 0.93 for the 50-task family; the paired statistic is smaller than the median-of-medians because several seeds already saturate at the loose end. Both as-placed costs are readings under the anchor construction at the registered budget --- no upper bound tighter than the trivial 1.0 is available at $s\ge 1.0$ (the floor-exclusion bound is trivially 1.000 there, Table~\ref{tab:modes}), so they are not attributed between genuine C2/interval infeasibility and construction shortfall. A sixteen-fold evaluation budget (population and generations each four-fold, 160/240) leaves the residual unmoved as well: at $s=0.9$ the mixed family's ten re-run units keep the median exactly at 0.6884 with the search never exceeding the anchor, so the 6.0-point residual between the floor-exclusion bound and the observed median is a construction/C2 property at the registered margin policy, not a budget artefact. The as-placed point behaves the same way under the same check: the mixed family's ten re-run units at $s=1.0$ hold the median at exactly 0.9485 with the search again never exceeding the anchor, so the 4.15-point as-placed cost is likewise not a budget artefact. R1C's cost against its own saturation is 0.00 percentage points, and that zero is a statement about where the family's saturation sits --- the as-placed point is its saturation point --- not a claim that a tightening is free for every family. Own saturation is the grid argmax by construction, and its placement carries the grid: R1C saturates at the as-placed point itself, R2F's maximum sits on the grid boundary at $s=2.0$, and R3H's plateau is flat to within one priority unit from $s=1.4$ upwards. The premiums are read against those points, not against a grid-independent optimum.

\label{sec:floor}The control family's tightest grid point is a different kind of fact and is separated from the curve on purpose. R1C at $s=0.8$ has no feasible individual in 10 of 10 seeds: every unit of that cell exited with the no-feasible-individual code, so the family admits no admissible schedule at that requirement under the frozen mode assignment. That is not a coverage of zero, and the two must never be interchanged. A zero would say that the search considered schedules and served none; what happened is that the requirement at $s=0.8$ asks for a resolution finer than the stripmap floor the family is fixed to, so there is no admissible schedule to consider. The figure draws the point as a cross against the axis carrying that label, the ledger stores it as a status rather than as a value, and no average, no smoothing and no line segment passes through it. Every statement about this point in the rest of the paper keeps the same distinction.

\subsection{The frozen criteria and their verdicts}\label{sec:criteria}

Two of the three frozen criteria fail on the 50-task family --- the monotonicity criterion and the tight-arm criterion --- and the control family's tight arm is the one cell that changes verdict between the frozen and the refined reading (Table~\ref{tab:criteria}). Table~\ref{tab:criteria} reports the three criteria of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} under the refined reading registered on 2026-10-23: an infeasible tight cell is undefined rather than a zero-coverage median, and the tight-arm verdict reads the median of the pairwise per-seed deltas ($s=1.0$ minus $s=0.8$) against the one-priority-unit tolerance of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}. The frozen verdicts remain on record, and the only cell where the two readings differ is the control family's tight arm: under the frozen zero-coverage scoring convention it read ``met''; under the refined reading it is undefined, because the tight cell admits no feasible individual in any seed. Why that cell is infeasible is arithmetic, not search: the control family is fixed to the stripmap mode, whose floor is $2.4669$~m, and its coarse-tier requirements span $[2.5, 3.0]$~m, so at $s=0.8$ every scaled requirement $0.8\times[2.5, 3.0]\in[2.0, 2.4]$~m sits strictly finer than the floor --- no admissible mode exists for any task, and the infeasibility is an exact consequence of the frozen bandwidth and the frozen grid. The monotone verdict is met under both readings, and the control family's saturation arm is met trivially --- its median is 1.0000 at both loose grid points, a ceiling artefact, so that cell carries no discriminating content. The 50-task family fails S1 under both; its tight arm does not fall under either reading (the two dips of Section~\ref{sec:dip} sit below the one-priority-unit tolerance), and both failures are reported as results about this scenario set.

S3's failure carries information about the model, and it is the falsification of a reading rather than a null result. In the retired v4 model generation (2026-10-17) this family's tight arm carried a large penalty --- the median at the tightest point was 0.446 of the served priority mass --- and that penalty is \emph{withdrawn}: under the convention-v5 model the same family reads 0.9764 at $s=0.8$, equal to its own as-placed value at the displayed precision --- not below it --- so the tight arm no longer falls at all. The withdrawn value is a historical comparison and is labelled as such wherever it appears.

What replaces it is smaller and better localized. The tightening still costs this family something, but the cost is measured at the as-placed point against the family's own saturation (2.36 percentage points, Section~\ref{sec:asplaced}), not at the tight arm. The mechanism is the parameter adaptation of constraint C5: the mode parameters are derived from the requirement at the selected start, so the delivered resolution equals the requirement by construction, and a tighter requirement therefore spends dwell rather than serviceability. The family pays in imaging time; it does not pay by losing tasks, until the dwell term grows enough to collide with the transfer constraint. The tight-arm penalty is gone; the as-placed cost is not.

S1's failure is the pair of local declines that Section~\ref{sec:dip} accounts for, and it is not removed by re-tuning. A criterion edited after its verdict is not a criterion: both failures stand as this scenario set's answer to questions that were fixed before its data were read.

\subsection{The two local declines: an anchor-construction effect}\label{sec:dip}

Two local declines interrupt the flat band of the 50-task family: 0.268 percentage points between $s=0.8$ and $s=0.9$, and 0.359 percentage points between $s=1.3$ and $s=1.4$. Both are attribution results, and the attribution is not the one a reader would expect.

Neither is a search miss. Over all 180 sweep units of the two treatment families the best-covered member of a unit's tie-set equals the greedy anchor's value exactly, and the search-only maximum is empty; across the whole 260-unit sweep the search exceeds its anchor in the two control units of Section~\ref{sec:faces} and nowhere else. A decline produced by a search that never misses is not a search miss. It is produced by the anchor construction itself, which is deterministic and reproducible: the anchor re-times the all-selected schedule in a single bounded pass, and at a given requirement that pass can serve one task fewer or one task more than at the neighbouring requirement.

The per-seed accounting makes the mechanism visible. Between $s=0.8$ and $s=0.9$ two seeds move in opposite directions --- one loses 1.83 percentage points and one gains 0.42. Between $s=1.3$ and $s=1.4$ the same shape recurs --- the same seed gains 0.42 points as in the first dip, and a different seed loses 1.14. In both cases the decline is the median of ten readings following two of its members, not a property of the demand axis.

This is a different object from the search-artifact verdict registered for the earlier model generation, and the difference matters for how the reading is used. That verdict retired a reading because a larger search budget removed it: what was retired was an artifact of the search. Here the anchor component of the reading is budget-independent by construction, and the search component was nil at the registered budget, and a sixteen-fold increase in evaluations (population and generations each four-fold: 160/240, 38{,}400 against the registered 2{,}400) leaves it nil at both dip points of this family: over the twenty re-run units the search never exceeds its anchor (largest movement 0.00 points), so the dips are not waiting for a larger budget to disappear --- consistent with the registered single-budget decision (Section~\ref{sec:preanswers}) and with the construction carrying the response. An anchor-free random initialization at the registered budget finds no feasible individual on 12 of the 40 re-run cells (all in the dense family) and never exceeds the anchor arm's construction on any of the 28 feasible cells; on the probed seed a sixteen-fold budget recovers a front still 15.5 points below the anchor, so the dips are not reproducible without the construction. And nothing physical follows from either decline: two opposite single-seed displacements are not a monotonicity claim in either direction, and no statement about the demand axis is made from a movement in the third decimal place. Figure~\ref{fig:probes} shows both probes on one ruler: the anchor-free arm never crosses the identity line, and the sixteen-fold budget arm coincides with it on every cell.

\subsection{Demand satisfaction and retention}\label{sec:demand}

The curve says how much priority mass is served; two further readings say what that service amounts to. The served-task fraction (selected tasks over admitted tasks, median over seeds of the best-covered member) tracks the priority fraction closely on every family --- 0.507/0.714/0.935 for the mixed family at $s=0.8/0.9/1.0$ against priority fractions of 0.483/0.688/0.948, and 0.979--0.980 across the whole tight side for the 50-task family against 0.974--0.976 --- so the uniform random priorities are close to inert in practice: what the schedule drops under tightening is, to within about two points, a proportional slice of the task set rather than a priority-selected one (the small residual means dropped tasks carry slightly above-average priority). The all-stripmap control is deliberately omitted from this reading: its served fraction equals its priority fraction wherever it is feasible (both saturate together), and its step cell is arithmetic rather than a schedule outcome (Section~\ref{sec:criteria}). Over the nine demand points the tie set serves 477,308 task instances, distributed over 9,430 tie-set records: a served task instance is one tie-set member's selection of one task, not a distinct task --- the best-covered member of each unit serves 13,845 distinct tasks in total over the grid, a factor of 34.5 below the instance count --- and the count is the sum over every record of the tasks that record selects. The delivery identity that constraint C5 enforces is a per-mode rule: for sliding spotlight and spotlight the delivered resolution equals the requirement at the selected start by construction, and for stripmap the library floor is delivered at or coarser than the requirement. The identity is checked by a raise rather than by a score, so no artifact stores a per-record verdict; the observable is the constraint violation, whose maximum over every record is 0.0, and no violation is recorded relative to the evaluator for any unit of the sweep. Ten of the 270 launched units exited with no feasible individual (the control family at $s=0.8$) and are excluded from the served count by the driver ledger rather than folded into it as zeros.

The retention reading measures the same service from the schedule's side, and it travels with a convention that must travel with it. On the STR-clip-only convention --- retention is the fraction of the tie-set feasible measure that remains feasible after the stripmap clip (numerator: the tie-set measure still feasible after the clip, denominator: the tie-set measure before it), evaluated at the as-placed point --- retention reads 51.14\% for the control family, 100.00\% for the 50-task family and 71.00\% for the 100-task family. The retention figures of the earlier model generation were computed under the retired SLS/SPT narrowing and are superseded.

The violation rate --- median per-seed rates 27.4 and 42.4; aggregate counts 253 of 926 pairs for the 100-task family and 199 of 458 for the 50-task family over the ten seeds --- is measured before any repair runs and does not depend on what is done next; it is the robust, repair-independent reading of the counterfactual. What the violated plan then costs does depend on the repair rule, so three rules are reported, all evaluated on the same stored freeze-dwell plans (rules (ii) and (iii) are reanalysis additions of this revision, dated 2026-10-24, not preregistered arms): (i) the drop-later rule registered with the counterfactual --- drop the later task of each violating pair, priority-blind --- under which the replayed schedule lands 18.25 and 30.44 percentage points (family medians; per-seed ranges 11.99--25.56 and 24.54--35.18) below what the endogenous dwell delivers; (ii) a priority-aware drop --- drop whichever endpoint of the violating pair carries the lower priority, with backward re-check --- which moves the medians only to 17.06 and 30.11; and (iii) a bounded re-timing --- push the later task to the earliest feasible start inside its own windows, drop only if none exists --- which cuts the 50-task gap to 7.08 but \emph{raises} the 100-task gap to 25.33, because on the dense family the pushed starts collide with later tasks and cascade further drops. The span 7--30 points is therefore a property of the repair capability, not a single number, and the two rules bracket it: the measured consequence of flying a plan that is infeasible against the real dwell is double-digit under every rule tested on the dense families, and which end of the span a deployment approaches depends on how much re-timing authority its repair has. The control family --- sparse, and all stripmap --- is essentially untouched: no violating pair in nine of ten seeds and a 0.0-point median gap under every rule (one seed carries a single violating pair), a reading taken relative to the evaluator as everywhere in this paper. That contrast is the signature of the mechanism: the fixed-dwell assumption fails exactly where the C2 chain is tight, and a planner working from a library-dwell model does not learn this from the plan itself --- the computed schedule looks feasible until it is replayed against the real dwell. The counterfactual is a reanalysis of the registered artifacts rather than a new experiment; the fixed-dwell ablation run under the retired v4 model generation (2026-10-17) is not mixed in. A conservative fixed-dwell baseline --- each task's dwell taken at the larger-|$\psi$| window endpoint, clamped to the SPT start envelope where it binds --- removes the violation channel in this scenario set (no violating pair among the 324 and 354 consecutive pairs of the two dense families) at the price of a sparser plan: the replayed schedule serves a median 28 and 56 points less than the endogenous-dwell schedule (family medians; per-seed ranges 15.15--33.46 and 51.98--69.10), so the sign of the fixed-dwell cost is not an artefact of the zero-squint baseline, while its magnitude and the family ordering are convention-dependent.

\subsection{Policy-knob sensitivities: transfer margins and tier placement}\label{sec:knobs}

Two policy knobs sit under the curve --- the transfer-margin policy that prices every inter-task transition, and the demand-template tier placement that fixes where the stripmap floor bites --- and both admit reanalyses of the frozen artifacts.

\paragraph{Transfer margins} Re-running the greedy anchor (the construction face that carries the response, Section~\ref{sec:faces}) over the same frozen mixed-family scenarios under four margin policies --- the registered one (25\% dwell pad, 4.07~s fallback maneuver bound), the pad halved to 12.5\%, the fallback bound replaced by the median measured model value (2.409~s), and both together --- moves the median coverage curve by at most 0.7 percentage points anywhere on the grid (the largest movement is 0.9766 to 0.9833 at $s=1.1$ under both relaxations) and leaves the floor-dominated cells $s=0.8$ and $s=0.9$ unchanged under the pad-only and maneuver-only policies (0.4827 and 0.6884, the registered readings; the combined relaxation moves each by 0.18 points, so the floor remains the dominant exclusion there and the margin is a secondary contributor only). The registered-policy arm reproduces the stored sweep anchors exactly on all 90 replayed units, so the comparison is anchored. The margin policy therefore prices the absolute level by tenths of a point; it does not generate the tightening response.

\paragraph{Tier placement} Where the floor step sits is a property of the demand template, not of the platform, and the sweep can measure that directly: multiplying the whole coarse tier by $1/\phi$ is arithmetically identical to reading the same frozen manifests at demand factors $s/\phi$ (requirements, modes, windows and priorities all unchanged), so the all-stripmap control family admits an exact in-sweep tier-shift experiment. Under $\phi=1.1$ the predicted all-infeasible threshold moves to $s^{*}=2.4669/(3.0/1.1)=0.905$, and the observed step moves exactly there: the shifted $s=0.9$ cell ($s_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.818$) is infeasible in 10 of 10 seeds, and the shifted $s=1.0$ cell reads a median of 0.568 --- the same partial-band shape the original family shows at its own $s=0.9$ (0.540). Under $\phi=0.9$ the threshold leaves the grid ($s^{*}=0.740$) and no infeasible cell remains: the tightest cell reads a partial 0.469 and the next saturates. The step location is thus a direct function of the tier knob, and the platform's contribution is the floor value itself. The corresponding operational caveat is registered with the result: real requirement changes move one product tier at a time rather than scaling the whole demand book uniformly, and per-tier tightening --- the axis an operator would actually use --- is future work; the uniform scan here is the controlled version of the question, not the operational one.

\subsection{Reported readings}\label{sec:readings}

One reading is reported beside the curve. It travels with the convention label that defines the quantity it states.

\paragraph{The fulfillment ratio} For a served task the ratio is its f$_q$ at the realised window and the selected start's squint, over the best f$_q$ the task could reach over all of its windows at zero squint, so the ratio is at most one; it is averaged over all tie-set members and reported, never optimised. At the as-placed point the fulfillment ratio reads 0.6580 for R1C, 0.7764 for R2F and 0.7447 for R3H. Its label travels with every number it states: \emph{[library convention $f_q$ $\mid$ per-task $f_q$ at the realised window and squint over the zero-squint optimum over all windows $\mid$ mean over all tie-set members $\mid$ reported reading, not an optimization target]}. What the reading says is narrow and is kept narrow: it measures how much of the zero-squint optimum over a task's own windows survives the choice of window and start, not how good the image is by any calibrated radiometric standard (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).


\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scope and negative claims}\label{sec:scope}

Five scope statements are registered before anything is claimed. First, the capacity law carries a per-pass convention: the registered serial bound is defined per pass, not per orbit, and the closure (the served fraction of the bound) is a containment reading --- it says the schedule fits inside the bound, not that the bound validates the scheduler. The minimum-over-pairs transfer time that enters it is a legal lower bound on the true transfer, never an average or a worst case. Under the present real-window scenario set the serial bound has not been recomputed, so this paper reports no capacity number in any form; the law, its convention, and its discipline are the contribution, and the recomputation is future work.

Second, the main claim is bounded by its mode assignment. The results of Section~\ref{sec:results} are requirement sensitivity under the frozen mode assignment of Section~\ref{sec:axis}: the curve answers what tightening the requirement costs when the mode a task receives does not change. It does not answer how coverage trades against resolution when a router is free to move tasks between modes, and that experiment is not run here. The freeze also makes the floor cost policy-specific rather than platform-physical: under the registered selection policy (Section~\ref{sec:axis}) a coarse-tier order whose scaled requirement slips below the stripmap floor remains routable to sliding spotlight at moderate squint ($A_{\mathrm{task}}\in(0,1)$ admits it), and the frozen assignment forbids exactly that rescue --- part of the floor-dominated cost is the comparability policy, not the platform. Third, the curve is read at one registered search budget (Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses}), so it is a reading of this scenario set at that budget and not a statement about a converged frontier in either direction.

Fourth, the trade-off this study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum. The antenna-design question of resolution against swath is a sensor-layer consequence that requires a width-versus-factor model this simulation does not have; it is reported as a limitation and future work, not as a demonstrated result. Fifth, the operational layer: the model covers single-pass scheduling decisions only --- no dynamic task arrival, no downlink or ground-station modeling, and no replanning loop --- and the only wall-clock figures registered are the per-unit medians of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} (1.3--23.3~s), with no end-to-end campaign or operational timing study, so the platform is offered as a falsifiable scheduling substrate, not as an operationally timed planner; operational-cost evaluation is future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Azimuth ambiguity versus the literature: a direction contrast}\label{sec:direction}

The registered correction behind this contrast is blunt: in this model the ambiguity-versus-squint direction is the \emph{opposite} of the literature anchor it would most naturally be cited against. Under the production PRF and the registered calibration grid, the sliding-spotlight and stripmap ratios improve monotonically with squint --- from $-21.35$~dB at zero squint to $-23.32$~dB at the $26^{\circ}$ calibration-grid edge (the registered SLS cap is $26.9^{\circ}$) --- and the spotlight ratio is squint-invariant at $-7.05$~dB, exactly as the degenerate-integral structure of Section~\ref{sec:quality} predicts. That the stripmap and sliding-spotlight calibration sequences read identical values is exact, not a transcription error: with $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SLS}}=A\,D/2$ the sliding-spotlight Doppler bandwidth, once compressed by the sliding factor $A$, equals the stripmap bandwidth for every $A$. Song et al.'s platform, by contrast, degrades with scan angle (the anchors quoted in Section~\ref{sec:related}) \citep{song2025spaceborne}; those values are cited as that platform's literature readings only. The contrast is a difference of registered model behavior, not a measurement that refutes the anchor: under the rotation-centre semantics the compression factor $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A$ is squint-independent (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), so the only squint-dependent channel into the ambiguity integral is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$, which squint narrows, and a narrower band aliases less of the ambiguity power --- a difference of mechanism, not merely of sign, carried together with the registered omission of the absolute Doppler centre (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}); the literature-side mechanism --- a degrading compression factor competing with bandwidth narrowing, and a scene-ensemble scan span that grows the Doppler occupancy with scan angle --- is not modeled here, no experiment in this paper discriminates the two steering semantics, and the scene-scan-span channel is the candidate reconciliation a targeted experiment would test first. Two scope notes close the contrast: the production PRF is a registered platform constant of this scenario set, no PRF-selection or band-widening criterion is exercised, and the SLS and SPT processing bandwidths at zero squint, 15.2 and 25.3~kHz, are several times the PRF, entering the admission readings through the AASR model evaluated at the registered PRF rather than being avoided by a PRF choice; and $-7.05$~dB is the library-edge constant evaluated at the platform-level scene extent (5~km, worst edge), whereas admission evaluates each task at its own scene extent through the 11-point in-window rule of Section~\ref{sec:quality}, so the two figures are different conventions of the same model, not a contradiction --- the admission readings are the ones every feasibility statement inherits.

\subsection{Model caveats}\label{sec:caveats}

\paragraph{f$_{ac}$ absolute position is omitted} The ambiguity folding is integrated about the direct-current (DC)-relative coordinate, so the absolute Doppler centre $f_{ac}$ never enters the model; only the bandwidth and the compression factor depend on squint. The measured magnitude of the omission at the design-domain edge (worst side, $45^{\circ}$, an extrapolated-domain reading) is 345.7~kHz (95.2 times the production PRF of 3630~Hz, and 300.6 times the 1150~Hz registration key), so the folded ambiguity structure sits far outside the band and the omission is defensible; it is disclosed regardless, and pushing the platform to its full $45^{\circ}$ design domain must carry this number with it. A second simplification of the same family: the antenna pattern carries azimuth-frequency dependence only --- there is no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so the absolute ambiguity level is an azimuth-integration reading; no verdict in this paper rests on the unmodeled elevation-plane attenuation, and the direction contrast of Section~\ref{sec:direction} is carried by the bandwidth channel alone. For azimuth aliases --- which return from the same slant range and elevation angle as the desired signal --- a two-way elevation pattern cancels to first order between numerator and denominator of this azimuth-only integral, so the omission is near-benign for what is integrated here; range-type far ambiguities would lose elevation-plane attenuation, and the absolute level carries that unquantified reserve in the conservative direction. Its magnitude is not quantified here.

\paragraph{The C2 maneuver angle is a two-sided lower bound} The inter-task requirement of Section~\ref{sec:dwell} forms the maneuver angle from the difference of the tasks' start squints. This is a lower bound on the true attitude excursion from two independent sides: it omits the beam sweep during each dwell (the per-task sweep rate is $0.6686^{\circ}$/s at $30^{\circ}$ incidence, $0.5599^{\circ}$/s at $45^{\circ}$, and $0.4233^{\circ}$/s at $60^{\circ}$, i.e.\ a $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ excursion across a 4~s dwell that the start-point difference never sees), and it omits the roll-axis slew between consecutive pointings, so the true off-vertical excursion is further understated by an unquantified amount. The 25\% inter-task pad is therefore a \emph{margin argument}, not a correctness argument: it buys slack against a bound that is systematically low, and the paper never describes the bound as an exact measure of the attitude change. One cross-reference closes this caveat: the per-task sweep rates quoted here are the antenna steering rates of Section~\ref{sec:dwell}'s definition, and the antenna's own sweep during a dwell is the quantity the $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ excursion describes --- the dwell that feeds C2 comes from the broadside-derived closure whose registered approximation status Section~\ref{sec:dwell} records.

\paragraph{g$_T$ has no external cross-validation} The dwell-gain term is self-derived and internally verified; the registered search for an external source found no noise-equivalent equation set in the Song platform paper (zero hits across its full text) and the textbook is not in the library with resolvable pages \citep{song2025spaceborne,cumming2005digital}. The verification route is therefore the internal identity chain of Section~\ref{sec:quality} and its regression guard, and the absence of external cross-validation stands as a limitation in its own right. Consistent with the objective-level scope note of Section~\ref{sec:quality}, no \emph{calibrated} radiometric quantity enters the objective; the gain term shapes the resolution index and the uncalibrated relative range factor carries the range dependence, but no noise model is imported.

\paragraph{The range-resolution anchor is a design constant} $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of 0.5~m, registered at $45^{\circ}$ incidence with scene-centre semantics. Under the real-window incidence band (30--60$^{\circ}$) a physical range resolution would vary by roughly $-18\%$/$+41\%$ across the band (the $\sin\eta_i$ projection of the frozen design-incidence bandwidth: $-18\%$ at $60^{\circ}$, $+41\%$ at $30^{\circ}$, each relative to the $45^{\circ}$ anchor); the constant is a modeling choice that keeps the quality readings comparable across windows, and the magnitude narrative of any quality reading inherits this caveat.

\paragraph{The quality index depends on the scheduled window} The range factor $(R_0/R)^3$ of Section~\ref{sec:quality} is evaluated at the slant range of the window in which the selected start falls, and a task's windows are enumerated at different transits, hence at different slant ranges. The index of a task is therefore not a property of the task alone: moving a selection to a different transit changes its index, and with a frozen mode assignment that change is one of the quantities a schedule can still choose. The variation is not corrected anywhere in this paper, and it is the same non-normalisation that leaves index levels incomparable across families (Section~\ref{sec:families}).

\paragraph{The convention that travels with the reading} The fulfillment ratio is computed on the library convention, and its limitation is stated with it: it measures a task's realised value against its own zero-squint optimum over its own windows --- a within-task, within-family quantity. It is not a calibrated radiometric statement, it does not compare across families (the units expand scene-dependently, Section~\ref{sec:families}), and it is not the delivered resolution of the schedule.

\paragraph{The maneuver fallback and the transfer-time composition} The production transfer charge is a per-pair hybrid anchored on the preregistered fallback: the two-phase maneuver model under-predicts the measured Kim RMT it is calibrated against (0.000~s at the zero-angle calibration point against a $1.84$~s measurement), so a pair whose model prediction falls below half of that anchor is charged the measured 4.07~s fallback bound instead of the model value, and the remaining pairs carry the model value itself. Replayed over the 1,577 consecutive pairs of the registered schedules, the model value sits below the bound in 84\% of pairs (median 2.409~s, range 0.021--6.88~s), and the pairs above the bound are the large-angle transitions, charged at the model value, whose residual under-prediction the 25\% inter-task pad is the declared margin for. The median transfer requirement decomposes as 5.643~s of pad-carrying dwell, 2.784~s of charged maneuver time and the 2~s settle. The charged median sits above the model-only median because the fallback bound replaces the smallest model predictions; pad and settle enter linearly, so halving the pad moves the median requirement by roughly 5\% --- a sensitivity stated here rather than swept. The scene-velocity convention (inertial, Section~\ref{sec:geometry}) scales every dwell linearly but leaves the maneuver bound and the settle term unchanged, so the C2 chain does not scale uniformly: under the ECEF-relative convention ($7221.69$~m/s) every dwell lengthens by about $4.9\%$, the C2 margin tightens at the boundary, and the coverage readings move with it --- they carry the convention (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), as dwell ratios and all cross-mode comparisons do not. The inertial choice is calibrated rather than arbitrary: under it the registered chain reproduces the reference platform's published scenario-level aperture times to $0.2$--$0.5\%$, against $4.7\%$ under the ECEF-relative figure.

\paragraph{Per-mode conventions are not economically comparable} The spotlight admission in the mode library is defined per point target while the sliding-spotlight scenes are scene-level, and the scheduler consumes the scene-level dwell; the two conventions are registered as asymmetric. Any statement that trades dwell economy across modes --- a spotlight dwell against a sliding-spotlight dwell as if they purchased the same thing --- is outside what the model supports, and none is made.

\subsection{Design semantics and pre-answers}\label{sec:preanswers}

\paragraph{Why not jointly optimize mode and time?} Because the mode is a product-requirement property, not a scheduler preference. The mode follows from the ordered (resolution requirement, scene size) pair by the coarsest-sufficient library table; making it a decision gene would let the optimizer silently upgrade or downgrade the product the customer requires. The sliding factor is likewise a system parameter. The evidence this paper can offer for the joint formulation being unnecessary here is narrower than that argument needs, and stating it precisely matters: the coverage response is a construction response (Section~\ref{sec:faces}), so in this scenario set a unit's outcome is set before the search runs, and a mode gene would add decision variables to a search whose measured contribution is two units out of 260; whether a mode gene would change the served set under a different demand mix is untested. Joint mode-and-time allocation remains future work for regimes where that activation exists.

\paragraph{The reading is single-budget} The curve of Section~\ref{sec:results} is read at one registered budget, and a future budget extension can move it: nothing in the registered design forbids a re-run at a larger budget, and if a larger budget changed the served sets, the registered criteria of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} would be re-measured rather than assumed. In practice the budget was fixed by a registered decision --- raising it was judged to buy a different search rather than a different construction, since the construction face carries the response (Section~\ref{sec:faces}) --- so the extension is available in the design but priced out by policy; every reading inherits this single-budget convention.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper put the required resolution into the scheduling constraint set and measured what tightening it costs. On the platform leg, the imaging mode is resolved from the product requirement and its parameters follow the requirement per task, so the delivered resolution equals the requirement at the selected start by construction; the dwell is then an endogenous consequence of the mode, the requirement and the selected start, and the maneuver-coupled feasibility chain prices every schedule in physically meaningful time --- a lower-bound pricing under a registered margin policy, not an exact attitude measure (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). The quality index states a platform capability and supplies a reported reading --- a fulfillment ratio on the library convention --- while coverage is the only objective, and a unit's front is a tie-set on it.

The result is a coverage--demand curve over a frozen nine-point grid, read under a fixed mode assignment. Tightening is where the coverage moves: the 100-drawn / 99--100-admitted family drops 26.0 percentage points from the as-placed requirement to $s=0.9$ and 46.6 points to the tightest feasible point --- of which the stripmap floor accounts for 20.0 and 44.8 points (the floor-exclusion bound of Table~\ref{tab:modes}) --- the all-stripmap control drops 46.0 points at $s=0.9$ and has no feasible individual at all at the tightest point --- an infeasibility record, not a coverage of zero --- and the 50-drawn / 46--50-admitted family holds within 0.27 points of its as-placed coverage at every tighter point. Loosening recovers less: 4.15 percentage points for the 100-task family against its own saturation and 2.36 points for the 50-task family (3.98 and 0.93 paired per seed, respectively). The fixed-dwell counterfactual quantifies what the endogenous dwell buys: planned with the library dwell, the dense families violate the transfer constraint in 27--42\% of consecutive pairs and, after repair, land 7--30 points short, the span depending on the repair rule (Section~\ref{sec:counterfactual}). Two of the three frozen shape criteria fail on the 50-task family, and both failures are reported as this scenario set's answer; the tight-arm penalty recorded under the retired v4 model generation is withdrawn rather than quietly carried forward. The anchor already attains the best-found coverage in 258 of 260 units --- which is what makes the response a construction result rather than a search finding. The construction, not the search, carries the response.

The claims stay inside those bounds. They are requirement sensitivity under a frozen mode assignment, at one registered budget, with the capacity law stated in convention and without a number. The sensor-layer question of resolution against swath, the joint mode-and-time formulation, the recomputation of the serial bound under this scenario set, and an operational-cost evaluation are future work rather than results. Two continuations follow directly from the counterfactual: onboard replanning against realised dwells, where the fixed-dwell failure mode bites hardest, and constellation-level tier management, where the requirement tiers this paper tightens are priced products.
