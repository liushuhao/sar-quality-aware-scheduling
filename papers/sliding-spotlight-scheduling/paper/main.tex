\begin{abstract}
Agile Earth-observation satellites that steer a single antenna across stripmap, sliding-spotlight, and spotlight modes are usually scheduled under an exogenous, fixed imaging duration. For a sliding-spotlight platform the assumption is false in a quantifiable way: the dwell is produced by the mode continuum and the selected start, and it enters the inter-task transfer constraint that decides what a pass can carry. This paper makes the dwell endogenous, carries a per-mode image-quality index as a second objective, and treats the steering-coupled ambiguity criterion as a first-class admission constraint. The study is preregistered: a budget-ladder calibration separates search-budget artifacts from scenario properties, every frontier reading carries a NOT-PLATEAUED honesty clause, and the capacity law is stated with a per-pass caliber but no number, because its serial bound is not recomputed in the present scenario world. On 90 units across three budget tiers (ten seeds per family), no unit promotes (maximum spread 3.9722 dB against the frozen 6.0 dB threshold) while both treatment families are structurally alive at the model level (8.74 and 10.91 dB; the control reads 3.9998 dB); the pooled quality-headroom test on the treatment stratum rejects decisively ($t=24.78$, $p\approx 3\times 10^{-16}$), and the base-budget front-collapse reading is retracted as a search artifact. The platform's contribution is that each of these claims is registered, budget-bounded, and falsifiable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Motivation}

Agile Earth-observation satellites steered by a single antenna can operate their synthetic aperture radar (SAR) in a continuum of imaging modes: stripmap, sliding spotlight, and spotlight are one physical mode family parameterised by where the beam's virtual rotation centre sits. Which point of that continuum a task receives is not a free stylistic choice --- it is fixed by the requested resolution, the scene, and the geometry of the pass --- and it determines how long the satellite must dwell on the scene. Scheduling therefore cannot treat the imaging duration as an exogenous constant, as the classical agile-scheduling literature largely does \citep{long2024deep,qin2024multisatellite,li2024earth}: in sliding-spotlight operation the dwell is produced by the imaging physics, it changes from task to task, and it feeds back into how many tasks a pass can carry and at what image quality.

This paper studies that coupling on a single satellite in a regime of single-pass dense task clusters under true orbit geometry. Scheduling decisions select which tasks to serve and when to start imaging within each task's visibility windows; the mode follows from the product order, and the dwell, the maneuver, and the image quality follow from the mode and the window. The systematic study of scenario density as an experimental axis belongs to the thesis companion rather than this paper.

Two risks motivate the methodology as much as the physics does. First, a claim that a frontier property (its richness, its collapse) is a property of the \emph{scenario} can silently be a property of the \emph{search budget}; our own calibration caught exactly such a reading, and the paper reports it as a falsified artifact (Section~\ref{sec:convergence}). Second, image-quality statements are only as good as their measurement calibers: several formally plausible formulations (an ambiguity sum that includes the principal image, a range resolution that varies per task, a squint-dependent routing resolution) are registered as not used, and the paper says so where it matters.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

This section positions the paper along three lines: sliding-spotlight SAR imaging (where the dwell physics lives), agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling (where the fixed-dwell assumption lives), and quality-aware scheduling (where image quality enters planning decisions).

\subsection{Sliding-spotlight and spotlight SAR imaging}

The sliding-spotlight family is standardly formulated as steering about a fixed virtual rotation centre: the TerraSAR-X extended chirp scaling processor is built on that geometry \citep{mittermayer2003sliding}, the baseband azimuth-scaling processor of Prats et al.\ handles both sliding spotlight and terrain observation by progressive scans (TOPS) with the rotation centre beyond the swath \citep{prats2010processing}, and Kim et al.\ derive the attitude-maneuver and saturation-avoidance time formulas for squint staring and sliding spotlight on the same geometry \citep{kim2021optimal}. Operational descriptions sometimes state the steering as a constant angular rate --- Zhang et al.'s Gaofen-3 (GF-3) account does so explicitly and describes it as a small-angle simplification of the same geometry \citep{zhang2018sliding} --- and processing work that pushes to high squint is likewise explicit that the linearised steering assumption breaks there \citep{guo2011extended,ren2025high}. This paper adopts the rotation-centre formulation throughout (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}); its scheduling windows reach squints where only the exact geometry is valid, which is why the two readings must not be mixed.

Two quantitative anchors from the imaging line matter to this study, and both are quoted as their own platforms' literature values. First, Kim et al.'s Table~2 defines a $\pm 12^{\circ}$ task envelope for its reference mission; the design envelopes registered in this paper --- $\pm 45^{\circ}$ for stripmap/spotlight and $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$ (a registered conservative value, a design choice) for sliding spotlight --- are separate fields and are never merged with the literature task domain \citep{kim2021optimal}. Second, Song et al.\ report for their sparse-imaging platform that the azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio is anti-correlated with the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) and degrades as the scan angle grows (by about 8.6~dB beyond $1^{\circ}$ of scan for conventional sliding spotlight, and about 15.2~dB for the sparse variant), alongside a range-ambiguity reduction of about 10.7~dB for the sparse mode \citep{song2025spaceborne}; as Section~\ref{sec:direction} shows, the direction of the ambiguity-versus-squint dependence in our model is the opposite of that literature anchor, and the contrast is a result of this paper rather than a contradiction to be smoothed over. The standard ambiguity and resolution relations otherwise follow the textbook treatment \citep{cumming2005digital}.

The dwell time itself is treated in the imaging literature as a deterministic function of mode and geometry --- Kim et al.'s saturation-avoidance time chain is exactly that \citep{kim2021optimal} --- but it stops at the imaging problem boundary: the scheduling layer above it is not part of that literature's concern.

\subsection{Agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling}

The agile-scheduling literature is broad and mature: hybrid genetic-simulated-annealing planners for moving-target observation \citep{qin2024multisatellite}, deep-reinforcement-learning planners for large mission-planning instances \citep{long2024deep,chen2024learning,zhang2025deep}, adaptive large-neighborhood search with learned guidance \citep{du2025satellite}, constraint-programming formulations for super-agile platforms \citep{caleiras2026a}, memetic and multi-chromosome cooperative planners \citep{wang2024memetic,wang2024research}, and profit models in which the observation value depends on the interval between revisits \citep{li2024earth}. Across these formulations one structural assumption is essentially universal: the imaging duration of a task is an exogenous constant of the task, known before the scheduler runs. The scheduler then packs fixed-length intervals into time lines, and whatever the objective --- profit, coverage, latency --- the duration never feeds back.

For an agile SAR platform in sliding-spotlight operation that assumption is false in a specific, quantifiable way: the dwell is produced by the mode physics (the tan-exact closure chain of Section~\ref{sec:dwell}), it varies per task with the assigned mode and the squint of the selected start, and it enters the inter-task transfer constraint that decides what else the pass can carry. This paper makes the dwell endogenous and studies what that does to the schedule.

\subsection{Quality-aware scheduling}

Putting image quality inside the planning loop, rather than after it, has far less company, and the nearest lines of work are worth distinguishing explicitly. Image-quality research on the SAR side is largely an assessment discipline --- fidelity metrics, quality checkers, and radiometric corrections applied to formed images \citep{cumming2005digital}. On the scheduling side, interval-varying-profit formulations make observation value a function of the scheduling policy rather than a fixed task constant \citep{li2024earth,peng2019agile}, and multi-objective SAR mission and constellation studies weight image-related factors explicitly alongside coverage and priority \citep{kim2020optimal,kim2019multi,chang2021integrated,wang2024memetic,he2018a}. What those constructions do not carry is the quantity this paper optimises: a per-mode image-quality index derived from the platform's imaging physics --- dwell from the mode continuum, ambiguity from the steering geometry --- entering the optimisation as a second objective aggregated over the served set, with the steering-coupling loop carried as a first-class admission constraint. The closest construction to this paper's is the coarsest-sufficient mode mapping that turns a required resolution into a mode assignment; what is missing from that construction is the second objective: the quality actually delivered by the selected schedule, aggregated over the served set, competing against the served priority in the optimisation itself.

\subsection{Positioning}

The three lines meet at a gap that this paper occupies by construction: treating the steering-coupling loop as a first-class constraint and carrying a per-mode SAR metric as an independent objective dimension simultaneously has no prior work in the literature surveyed above. The imaging line supplies the dwell and ambiguity physics but not the schedule; the scheduling line supplies the packing machinery but holds the duration constant; the quality-aware line supplies requirement-level mode selection but not a second objective. Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:results} build the combined problem on a registered, preregistered footing --- promotion verdicts judged against a frozen threshold of 6.0~dB, frontier readings taken only at the top budget tier with their honesty qualifier attached --- and report what the coupling changes.

\section{Problem Formulation and Scheduling Model}\label{sec:model}

This section formalises the observation geometry, the three-mode imaging library, the dwell-duration closures, the per-mode image-quality model, and the constraints and objectives of the scheduling problem.

\subsection{Observation geometry and imaging modes}\label{sec:geometry}

We adopt the X-band reference platform of Kim et al. \citep{kim2021optimal}: altitude $h=570$~km, inclination $45^{\circ}$, wavelength $\lambda=0.031$~m, azimuth beam width $\theta_a=0.36^{\circ}$, and a square scene of 5~km side length. The derived geometry is $D=\lambda/\theta_a=4.9338$~m (antenna azimuth length), aperture-centre slant range $R_0=775.4$~km, and inertial scene velocity $v_s=7578.05$~m/s. Each task is a scene; the scheduled quantity is \emph{when} to image it, not \emph{whether} to reconfigure the payload.

The three imaging modes --- stripmap (STR), sliding spotlight (SLS), and spotlight (SPT) --- are treated as a single-parameter continuum over the position of a fixed virtual rotation centre \citep{kim2021optimal,zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}: writing $k$ for the sliding factor with $A=1-k$, $k\to 0$ places the rotation centre at infinity (stripmap, parallel antenna axis), $0<k<1$ places it below the surface beyond the scene (sliding spotlight), and $k\to 1$ places it at the target (spotlight). The registered operating point of this study is $k=0.797317$, i.e.\ a rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance $r_0=197\,119$~m $\approx 197$~km. We stress the symbol discipline that the two distances are dimensionally identical but semantically distinct: $r_0\approx 197$~km is the rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance, whereas $R_0\approx 775.4$~km is the aperture-centre slant range; their ratio is $r_0/R_0=0.254207$, and the two are never interchangeable (Fig.~\ref{fig:modes}).

The sliding factor: under the rotation-centre semantics adopted here, $A=r_0/(R_0+r_0)$ \citep{zhang2018sliding}; the form $A=1-R_0 k_\theta/v_g$, with $k_\theta$ the azimuth steering rate and $v_g$ the ground speed (Song Eq.~(1)) \citep{song2025spaceborne}, is a restatement within the same rotation-centre model --- for the steering rate that places the rotation centre at $r_0$ --- not an independent literature identity. The per-mode azimuth resolutions at zero squint are $\rho_{a,\mathrm{STR}}=D/2=2.4669$~m, $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SLS}}=A\,D/2=0.5000$~m, and $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SPT}}=\lambda/(2\beta_{\mathrm{span}})=0.3000$~m with $\beta_{\mathrm{span}}=2.9603^{\circ}$ the spotlight sweep span (library design constant).

Under the rotation-centre semantics the spectral compression factor is a constant at every squint. Presenting the Doppler rates in the aperture-range form,
\[
K_a=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda R_0},\qquad
K_c=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda\,(R_0+r_0)},\qquad
a_{\mathrm{res}}=1-\frac{K_c}{K_a}\equiv A,
\]
the $\cos^3$ factors cancel at beam-centre crossing, so $a_{\mathrm{res}}$ does not degrade with squint; the sliding-spotlight design azimuth resolution is the constant $A\,D_a/2$ \citep{zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}.

The registered squint domains are separate registered fields, listed apart from the literature task envelope of Kim et al.\ ($\pm 12^{\circ}$, their Table~2 \citep{kim2021optimal}): STR and SPT carry a design domain of $\pm 45^{\circ}$, and SLS carries a registered conservative envelope of $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$. The SLS cap is a \emph{registered conservative value (design choice)} --- antenna steering margin plus frozen-data continuity --- and not a physical boundary: under the rotation-centre semantics $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A>0$ at every squint, so high-squint reachability is limited by the registered cap rather than by mode physics. Two extrapolation disclosures attach to the $45^{\circ}$ design domain: the azimuth-resolution projection correction (resolution-ellipse rotation) is outside the closed form, and the $45^{\circ}$ dwell readings of STR/SPT are extrapolated, because the planar tan-difference closure omits Earth curvature against the full-curvature $R_0$ (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).

\subsection{Dwell duration and visibility windows}\label{sec:dwell}

The dwell of a task is the saturation-avoidance time (SAT) at scene level: the time the beam needs to sweep the full square scene at the task's squint, where $W_a$ denotes the scene (square) size --- 5~km on the reference platform --- entering as a scene-length traversal term. The production closures are the tan-exact Kim Eq.~(22)/(24) chain \citep{kim2021optimal}. For STR the scene dwell is the exact tan difference of the beam-edge crossing angles plus the scene traversal $W_a/v_s$; for SLS it is the symmetric construction of Eq.~(22)/(24) with the sliding factor in the denominator; for SPT it is the exact Eq.~(10)--(11) tan-difference with the library sweep span. A planar closed form with the $\sec^2\bar\psi$ squint correction, $T_{\mathrm{SLS}}=(R_0\theta_a+W_a)/(A\,v_s)\cdot\sec^2\bar\psi$ (scene size inside the correction), survives as the test-time oracle: at the Kim reference squint of $12^{\circ}$ it deviates from the production chain by $+0.4185\%$, and the deviation grows to $+2.1\%$ at the SLS cap and $+5.3\%$ at $45^{\circ}$ --- which is what triggered the kill-test switch from the planar form to the tan-exact chain in the first place. A frozen window-tail pad of 1\% of the dwell is carried as a conservative margin on the production path; it is not claimed to bound the production closure's own squint error, which is not separately quantified here. Two provenance notes complete the closure. First, the Kim Eq.~(22)/(24) chain is a zero-Doppler-clamped (broadside) attitude construction; applying it at nonzero squint is a registered approximation, and the growth of the planar oracle's deviation with squint, quantified above, bounds the oracle's linearisation error rather than the production chain's --- it is evidence that the broadside-derived closure carries a modelling gap rather than numerical noise. Second, the resulting dwell feeds the C2 inter-task transfer requirement that determines pass capacity, which is why the deviation pattern and the frozen window-tail pad are carried with the constraint semantics (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}) rather than with the numerics.

The zero-squint anchors close an identity: $\theta_a/A=\lambda/(2\rho_{a})$ holds at the library design point ($0.006283185307/0.202683397006=0.031000000000$~rad $=\lambda/(2\times 0.5\,\mathrm{m})$) with a full-precision residual of $3.5\times 10^{-18}$. The surviving guard of this identity is the pytest fixture \texttt{test\_duration\_models.py::test\_identity\_theta\_a\_over\_A\_equals\_lambda\_over\_2rho}.

Each task carries one or more visibility windows produced by ground-track pass enumeration over the scenario span; a window fixes the closest-approach epoch $t_{\mathrm{ca}}$, the per-window slant range, and the earliest/latest feasible imaging start. The decision variable $\tau\in[0,1]$ of a selected task is mapped onto the feasible start interval of its window (Section~\ref{sec:solver}). The squint at the selected start, $\bar\psi$, is measured at the window's geometry.

One scheduling-level geometric quantity needs a definition here because it enters the inter-task constraint: the maneuver angle between consecutive tasks $i,j$, $d_\psi=|\psi_i-\psi_j|$, is the difference of their start squints. This is a planar-caliber \emph{lower bound} on the true attitude excursion: it omits the sweep \emph{during imaging} (the antenna keeps steering at $0.4233$--$0.6686\ ^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}$, i.e.\ $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ over a $4$~s dwell) and the start-squint difference understates the off-vertical attitude compression by 15--25\%. The full disclosure of the two-sided understatement and the margin-pad argument is given in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}.

\subsection{Image quality model}\label{sec:quality}

The per-task quality index is an inverse-pixel-area form with a range-normalised (non-normalised, $m^{-2}$ caliber) readout,
\[
f_{Q,i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}\,\rho_{a}(m_i)}\;g_T(m_i)\,\Big(\frac{R_0}{R}\Big)^{3},
\]
where $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is the ground-range resolution, $\rho_a(m_i)$ the zero-squint azimuth resolution of the assigned mode, and $g_T$ the transmit-path antenna gain term. The $(R_0/R)^3$ normalisation makes the gain term dimensionally explicit at reference range; at $R=R_0$ the identity chain collapses to $f_Q=(g_T/\rho_a)\cdot(1/\rho_{\mathrm{rg}})$ \citep{song2025spaceborne}. A scope note on the name: the index is dominated by its azimuth-resolution term, and no radiometric quantity --- noise-equivalent sigma zero, signal-to-noise ratio, or radiometric resolution --- enters the objective; the ambiguity ratio enters separately as an admission constraint, not as a traded objective. ``Image quality'' in this paper therefore denotes spatial-resolution quality under a gain-shaped multiplier.

Three caliber statements are frozen with this definition. First, $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of $0.5$~m (registered at $45^{\circ}$ incidence, scene-centre semantics); it is not recomputed per task, and the magnitude narrative carries a limitation (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). Second, $g_T$ is \emph{self-derived} and \emph{internally verified}: no external literature source carries the noise-equivalent sigma zero (NESZ) equation set for this mode family, so the verification route is the internal identity chain above (and its pytest guard), and the absence of external cross-validation is disclosed. Third, the squint-dependent achieved resolution $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}(m,\bar\psi)=\rho_a(m,0)/\cos\bar\psi$ is registered for reporting; the router consumes the zero-squint constant, and the only bandwidth consumer of $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}$ is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$ --- there is no consumer of the achieved resolution in mode routing. The projection relation behind the anchor is $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}=c/(2\,B_{\mathrm{rg}}\sin\eta_i)$ with the range bandwidth $B_{\mathrm{rg}}$ frozen at the design incidence; the planar evaluation layer holds the incidence at that design value, which is why $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ enters the objective as a constant, with the incidence-band variation disclosed separately in Section~\ref{sec:caveats}.

The azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio (AASR) is defined with the ambiguity sum \emph{excluding} the \emph{zeroth} (principal-image) term,
\[
\mathrm{AASR}=\frac{\sum_{m\neq 0}\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G\big(f_a+m\cdot\mathrm{PRF}\big)\big|^{2}\,df_a}{\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G(f_a)\big|^{2}\,df_a},
\]
following the standard convention \citep{cumming2005digital}: keeping $m=0$ would place the principal image in its own numerator and force $\mathrm{AASR}\ge 1$ identically. The frequency-axis rescaling by $(1-K_c/K_a)\approx(1-k_\theta R_0/v_g)$ --- a flat-ground bridge between the $K_c$ and $v_g$ forms, per the frozen formula registration --- under rotation-centre steering is what couples the steering geometry into the ambiguity integral \citep{song2025spaceborne}. The displayed integral is Song's general form, with the absolute-centre shift $f_{ac}$ carried in the integration limits; the implementation integrates the same sums about the DC-relative coordinate, where that shift cancels and the absolute centre drops out (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}). A footnote on the spotlight special case: Song et al.'s spotlight AASR \citep{song2025spaceborne} is the degenerate integral of the same form in which neither numerator nor denominator contains $f_a$ after the rescaling, which is why the SPT ambiguity in our model is squint-invariant. Integration caliber: the ambiguity sum runs over the azimuth-frequency axis alone --- the two-way pattern enters as a function of azimuth frequency, with no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so far-ambiguous-pulse attenuation from the elevation plane is not modeled; the principal effect of that simplification falls on the absolute ambiguity level, and it is registered with the model caveats (Section~\ref{sec:caveats}).

Admission uses a \emph{discrete} worst-case readout, \emph{not continuous} evaluation: the AASR of a task is evaluated at $11$ equally spaced \emph{in-window} samples and the maximum must not exceed the registered $-13$~dB admission threshold at generation time. Feasibility statements in this paper inherit that caliber --- a selection is feasible with respect to the generator's 11-point admission rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own.

\subsection{Constraints and objectives}\label{sec:constraints}

The decision vector of a scenario with $N$ tasks is $x=(s,\tau)\in\{0,1\}^{N}\times[0,1]^{N}$: a selection bit per task and a start-time fraction per selected task. The imaging mode $m_i$ is \emph{not} a decision gene: the pair (required resolution, scene size) is mapped to a mode by the coarsest-sufficient library table, a \emph{system design input with product order semantics} --- the platform is told what product a customer ordered --- and the sliding factor $k$ is likewise a system parameter. Both are \emph{exogenous} to the scheduler; joint mode-and-time allocation is future work.

Four constraint families apply.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1 (geometry, generation side).} A selected start must fall inside a visibility window of the task whose node satisfies the serviceability evidence: \emph{every} node in the dense domain carries \emph{at least one} in-band transit over the span ($25/25$ nodes over 72~h, per-node reading, recorded in the generation ledger).
\item \textbf{C2 (inter-task transfer).} For consecutive tasks in start-time order, the gap must cover $(1+0.25)\,T_{\mathrm{dwell},i}+t_{\mathrm{man}}(d_\psi,\omega_{\mathrm{start}},\omega_{\mathrm{end}})+t_{\mathrm{settle}}$: the $25\%$ pad multiplies the \emph{dwell} (Kim's own margin semantics, stated in four places in \citealt{kim2021optimal}), never the maneuver, and the settle term is an unpadded measured constant. The conflict predicate uses the bare dwell: pads are scheduling margin, not physical occupancy.
\item \textbf{C3 (energy/duty).} Per-task duty is normalised against the mode-library envelope reference $T_{\mathrm{env,max}}=10.5830$~s (the largest feasible scene dwell at the envelope-edge squint, attained by SPT) with mode weights STR/SLS/SPT $=1.0/1.15/1.3$.
\item \textbf{C4 (solid-state memory).} Per-echo bytes follow $\mathrm{PRF}\cdot T\cdot N_r\cdot b_q\cdot 2/8$ with $N_r=10\,000$ range samples and $b_q=3$ bits per component (TerraSAR-X-style block-adaptive quantisation), normalised against the same per-window reference as C3 (each measured window's own slant range, the registered v3 caliber) under the single production PRF of $3630$~Hz.
\end{itemize}

Two structural facts about C3/C4 are disclosed as design semantics rather than discovered: both budgets are \emph{structurally} \emph{never binding} inside the squint envelope (their normalised values cannot exceed 1.0 there, so the binding channels are C2 and interval conflicts), and the task priorities that drive the first objective are drawn uniformly at random as $\mathrm{randint}(1,10)$, i.e.\ they carry \emph{no physical} provenance --- they are demand-side bookkeeping, not measured value.

The bi-objective problem maximises the served priority fraction $f_1=\sum_{i\in S}p_i/\sum_{i}p_i$ and maximises the mean delivered quality index of the selected set, $\max f_Q=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}f_{Q,i}$ (the non-normalised quality aggregate, caliber as in Section~\ref{sec:quality}), over selections $S$ that satisfy C1--C4; the search implements the two axes as $(-f_1^*,-f_Q)$ minimisation, so both axes are maximised, and finer imaging yields the larger quality index. The search method that produces the front is described next.

\section{Solving Approach}\label{sec:solver}

\subsection{Encoding and evaluation}

The solver is a dual-objective evolutionary search over the chromosome of Section~\ref{sec:constraints}: a selection bit and a start fraction $\tau\in[0,1]$ per task --- no mode gene, since the mode is fixed by the product order --- so the densest real-window family (R3H) encodes 100 drawn tasks (99 served by the generation ledger) over 753 enumerated windows as 200 decision variables. Decoding projects $\tau$ onto the union of feasible window intervals of the admitted windows: a served task is feasible with respect to the generator's discrete 11-point admission rule (Section~\ref{sec:scenarios}), and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own --- feasibility statements in the results are relative to that generation-side caliber, not to an independent physical guarantee (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}).

\subsection{Anchor-seeded initialization}

The initial population contains the greedy anchor: a bounded single-pass re-timing repair of the all-selected schedule. The seed is what makes the $f_1$ normalisation meaningful --- every front carries a solution anchored at the served-priority reference --- and its injection into the population is checked by a dedicated cross-check that once exposed spurious violations from time-inverted index pairs, which is also why the evaluation loop sorts by start time before charging the C2 chain.

\subsection{Search operators and selection}

Variation uses standard binary crossover on the selection bits and simulated binary crossover with polynomial mutation on the start fractions; environmental selection is the standard non-dominated sorting with crowding distance \citep{deb2002a}. The search runs under a fixed random seed per unit, single-threaded, with manifest task-list order everywhere (no set or dictionary iteration order may influence a run), so every unit is bit-reproducible.

\subsection{The measure normalisation of $\tau$ and its implicit bias}

One parameterisation choice is disclosed as intentional. A start fraction $\tau$ indexes position within the \emph{feasible measure} of a task's window union, not within wall-clock time: decoding is the measure projection of Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}. Because the projection is measure-normalised, a uniform grid on $\tau$ samples wider windows more finely --- the parameterisation carries an implicit weighting toward wider windows. This bias is registered as a deliberate design decision: the main specification does not cover it, and the registration lives in the project's amendment record rather than in the main specification. No selection mechanism in the loop undoes the weighting; it is part of the search design, and every reading in Section~\ref{sec:results} inherits it.

\section{Experimental Design and Setup}\label{sec:testdesign}

This section describes the scenario world, the family and caliber system, and the preregistered test design.

\subsection{Scenario construction}\label{sec:scenarios}

The scenario world is built from \emph{real ground-track transit enumeration}, not from an arbitrary orbit phase point: visibility windows come from enumerating true ground-track passes over the scenario span, with a 15~s scan step, gap merging at 300~s (a registered band parameter, invariant across the registered 150/600/1200~s sensitivity band, not a physical lower bound), and 33-point edge refinement. The registry's own ledger records why: under the earlier phase-point pass rule only 0 of 8 clusters were serviceable after the incidence fix, against 6 of 8 under true enumeration. The demand mix itself --- resolution requirements and scene sizes --- is a registered synthetic template parameter, not a measured campaign profile; the priorities that drive the first objective are uniform random integers with no physical provenance (Section~\ref{sec:constraints}), so the mode composition of the served set reflects the template's requirement distribution.

Orbit propagation is two-body only --- no $J_2$ or atmospheric terms --- so the registered repeat figure is the \emph{simulated} ground-track repeat of 14.97 orbits $\approx$ 23.93~h (one sidereal day over the two-body period); the figure that includes $J_2$ nodal drift describes the physical orbit and is deliberately not the one this simulator produces. The surface normal used in incidence computations is an \emph{approximate} normal: its maximum deviation from the true ellipsoidal normal is $0.125^{\circ}$ (at ${\approx}30^{\circ}$ latitude; $0.109^{\circ}$ at the dense-domain centre), with no effect on band-boundary judgements at the registered margins. The dense domain is a 1000~km square centred at 20°N/0°E, a $5\times 5$ node grid at 250~km spacing with per-cell target jitter, a 3-day span in coordinated universal time (UTC). The serviceability criterion is per-node: every node carries at least one in-band transit within the span, measured 25/25; the literal best-incidence reading of the dense-domain constraint fails at the centre node (near-nadir passes exist there but yield no in-band window), which is why the operative criterion is the per-node one. The evidence lives in the generation-time guards and the manifest ledger.

The AASR admission criterion is evaluated per family, and the two families differ by design --- a discrepancy we disclose rather than harmonise post hoc. Synthetic M-family windows are admitted by a window-edge single-point readout (the AASR at the window edge squint only), whereas the real-window R families sample the AASR at 11 evenly spaced in-window instants ($\mathrm{lo}+(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})\,j/10$, $j=0,\dots,10$) and admit the window only if the worst of the 11 samples meets the threshold. A sample outside the physical sliding domain contributes an exclusion, so it can never mask an unserviceable window. The window-edge single-point rule \emph{underestimates} the in-window worst case by about 4~dB, so the two admission rules are stated per family wherever admission is discussed; the R-family rule is the discrete in-window worst-case caliber that all feasibility statements in this paper inherit (Section~\ref{sec:quality}). This sampling is \emph{discrete}, \emph{not continuous} evaluation: a window admitted under it is feasible with respect to the 11-point rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own.

\subsection{Hypotheses, power, and preregistration}\label{sec:hypotheses}

The quality-headroom test is a one-sample, one-sided $t$-test at $\alpha=0.05$ on the pooled primary stratum (registered as \texttt{ttest\_1samp} against a null mean of zero, pooled $n=20$; 10 seeds per family), on the registered estimand (the quality headroom of the front over its anchor at the top budget). The effect size is $\delta=6.0$ in R-family $f_q$ units ($m^{-2}$): the numeral inherits the magnitude of the frozen 6.0~dB promotion threshold as a \emph{numerical inheritance} and is dimensionally \emph{not} equivalent to it (dB vs $m^{-2}$); it was registered \emph{after calibration}, and the registration records exactly that --- the honest description is ``registered after calibration, value inherited from the frozen threshold'', never ``pre-registered''. The estimand itself is a within-unit difference: $dq_{all}=\max\{f_q(p): p \mathrm{\ on\ the\ front}\}-f_q(\mathrm{the\ anchor\ point\ on\ the\ same\ front})$; per-family $f_q$ levels are never pooled into one span (Section~\ref{sec:families}), while the registered primary test pools these within-unit differences, whose scale is comparable across the two pooled families.

The power design is registered with its asymmetry disclosed; the figures below are post-hoc observed powers, recomputed from the same ten units per family that the test reads, so the 0.92/0.29 asymmetry is a property of the measured dispersion, not an a-priori design guarantee. Recomputed from the promoted artifacts at the top budget, the standard deviation of the estimand is 5.67 (R2F) and 16.14 (R3H), giving minimum detectable effects at 80\% power of 4.87 and 13.87 respectively. At the registered $\delta$, the R2F power is 0.92, and the R3H power is 0.29; reaching 80\% power at $\delta$ would require R2F to run 7 seeds (already met) and R3H to run 54 seeds --- a \emph{cost anchor}, not a plan: the budget decision registered before the run declines to escalate, and the consequence for reporting is mandatory and asymmetric. R2F may claim 0.92 power at the registered $\delta$; R3H must disclose the 0.29 figure and report its result as not reaching significance at this $n$. Two registered elements of the analysis plan travel with the test and are reported in Section~\ref{sec:primary}: a sign-flip permutation robustness gate ($10^{5}$ flips; if the $t$ and permutation $p$ disagree across $\alpha$, the result is reported INCONCLUSIVE), and per-family Holm-adjusted secondary tests for locating only. Fig.~\ref{fig:power} shows the registered power curve with the operating point marked.

\section{Results and Analysis}\label{sec:results}

All readings in this section are taken from the highest tier of the promoted artifacts (160/240 population/generations) and carry the NOT-PLATEAUED qualifier where frontier richness is stated; the 40/60 base tier is retained only as an under-converged baseline and is never read as a result.

\subsection{Primary stratum results}\label{sec:primary}

The primary stratum for the quality-headroom test is the R2F+R3H pooled stratum, a registered adjudication recorded before the quality-headroom analysis was run; R1C is demoted to a single-orbit control and is read descriptively only, its served-fraction saturation being anchor-construction-carried (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}). Two registered facts motivate the demotion: the R1C quality axis operates on a different scale from the pooled families (the per-family domains of Section~\ref{sec:families} are separated by up to two orders of magnitude), and the power design covers R2F/R3H only. The pooled test itself rejects: recomputed from the 20 promoted top-tier units (10 R2F and 10 R3H), the pooled quality-headroom estimand $dq_{all}$ of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} reads a mean of 66.872~$m^{-2}$ (sd~12.071). The one-sided $t$-test against a null mean of zero gives $t=24.78$ with $p\approx 3\times 10^{-16}$, and the registered sign-flip permutation gate agrees ($10^{5}$ flips, no exceedance, add-one $p\approx 1\times 10^{-5}$); the result is therefore not INCONCLUSIVE. The registered pooled design's own detection limit at 80\% power is the minimum detectable effect (MDE$_{80}$) of 6.991~$m^{-2}$ (registered with the stratum adjudication), slightly above the registered $\delta=6.0$: the pooled test's power at $\delta$ therefore sits below the 80\% convention, and the decisive rejection reported above reflects an observed effect about an order of magnitude larger than $\delta$, not a design with power to spare. The registered quality-headroom prediction (prediction P2 of the preregistration) lands on its positive branch: the search confers measurable quality headroom over the greedy anchor at matched front, rejecting at $\alpha=0.05$. Two qualifiers travel with this result, as registered. First, the reading is taken at the top budget of a ladder whose fronts are still rising (NOT-PLATEAUED, Section~\ref{sec:convergence}): the magnitude of the headroom is budget-dependent, and this paper claims the headroom's existence and its registration discipline, not a converged magnitude. Second, the coverage-parity statement travels with this result in every branch (Section~\ref{sec:coverage}). Both the headline and every per-family reading in this section inherit the frozen $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ caliber: the incidence-band variation bound stated in Section~\ref{sec:caveats} applies to these magnitudes, and they are not corrected for it. The headroom is a \emph{within-run} property of the search relative to its own anchor, not a performance measure against an independent method; no comparative baseline is reported (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). The per-family readouts remain descriptive and are governed by the registered asymmetric reporting rule below.

The asymmetry is reported as registered. At the top budget the R2F power is 0.92 at the registered $\delta$, so R2F may claim the test; the R3H power is 0.29, and R3H's outcome is therefore reported as not reaching significance at $n=10$ --- the honest reading of a design whose seed anchor (R3H would need 54 seeds for 80\% power) was registered as a cost anchor, not a plan. What the test does \emph{not} constrain is the structural axis: ALIVE and DEAD verdicts on the model-level bound below are structural statements, and a family can be structurally ALIVE while failing to reject at this $n$. The pooled primary result reported above and this per-family rule operate at different scopes: the registered primary test is the pooled one, and the asymmetric rule governs per-family claims.

\subsection{Promotion, two axes}\label{sec:twoaxes}

The promotion verdict is stated on two separated axes, and the separation is the result.

\paragraph{Structural axis (model-level).} The real-window structural spread $R_{fam}$ --- the model-level AASR range attainable across each family's windows, recomputed under the rotation-centre (ticket-40) caliber --- is 3.9998~dB for R1C and 8.74/10.91~dB for R2F/R3H (Fig.~\ref{fig:promotion}a). R1C is therefore STRUCTURAL DEAD: its base-seed reading and its reading at every one of the ten seeds sit at or below 3.9998~dB (the weakest seed reads 3.9968~dB), all under the 6.0~dB threshold, so the verdict does not depend on which seed is read; its greedy anchor saturates the served fraction at 1.0 in every unit, its front is a pure quality trade-off surface, and that is exactly why it serves as the control. R2F and R3H are descriptively ALIVE at the model-level structural bound. Three disclosures attach. First, ``ALIVE'' is a model-level statement about what the scenario admits; it does not mean promotion fires, does not mean any readout has converged, and licenses no physical claim. Second, the knife-edge margin often quoted near promotion verdicts --- 0.499~dB, i.e.\ a 5.501~dB reading one half-dB under the line --- belongs to the synthetic P8WH family of the earlier calibration; R1C's own margin is 2.000~dB, and the two numbers are never interchangeable. Third, stability across calibers: the R2F and R3H structural readings moved by about one dB when the ambiguity semantics switched to the rotation-center caliber (the registered pre-ticket-40 readings were 9.75 and 11.99~dB), while R1C --- which carries no SLS task --- did not move at all. The verdicts are insensitive to that switch, because even the lowest registered per-seed aggregate for R2F (7.20~dB) stays above the threshold; the ALIVE/DEAD labels reported here therefore do not hinge on which admissible steering semantics is chosen.

\paragraph{Reading axis.} Over all 90 units (3 families $\times$ 3 budget tiers $\times$ 10 seeds) the promotion decision is no\_promotion everywhere, with a maximum spread of 3.9722~dB against the frozen 6.0~dB threshold (Fig.~\ref{fig:promotion}b). The no-promotion verdict is robust across the 16$\times$ budget ladder (4$\times$ in population and 4$\times$ in generations, i.e.\ 16$\times$ in evaluations) and may be cited as a structural reading with its budget evidence attached. The axes must not be merged: structural ALIVE is not a prediction that promotion triggers, and the 90/90 reading-axis outcome does not contradict the structural verdict.

\subsection{Convergence verdicts and budget tiers}\label{sec:convergence}

Convergence was calibrated under a preregistered rule frozen before the two additional budget tiers were run. Both treatment families carry the SEARCH-ARTIFACT verdict, and both disclosures are of falsified readings, not assertions.

For R3H the base-budget picture --- 6 of 10 seeds ending at a single-point front (median~1) --- inverts completely under the ladder. At the 160/240 top budget the R3H median front size is 140, and the median distinct-fq count is 99. The apparent collapse was judged a search artifact by the preregistered rule, and the verdict names the mechanism: running a $40\times 60$ budget against 200 decision variables. Any physical-collapse narrative built on the base budget is withdrawn, in both directions; for R2F the same rule fires more mildly: the ladder takes the median front size from 19.5 to 84.5 and the median distinct-fq count from 14.5 to 64.5 (a 4.4$\times$ rise), so the base-budget reading there was likewise an artifact of budget, not a property of the scenario (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Scope and negative claims}\label{sec:scope}

Four scope statements are registered before anything is claimed. First, the capacity law carries a per-pass caliber: the registered serial bound is defined per pass, not per orbit, and the closure (the served fraction of the bound) is a containment reading --- it says the schedule fits inside the bound, not that the bound validates the scheduler. The minimum-over-pairs transfer time that enters it is a legal lower bound on the true transfer, never an average or a worst case. Under the present real-window scenario world the serial bound has not been recomputed, so this paper reports no capacity number in any form; the law, its caliber, and its discipline are the contribution, and the recomputation is future work. Second, the trade-off this study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum. The antenna-design question of resolution against swath is a sensor-layer consequence that requires a width-versus-factor model this simulation does not have; it is reported as a limitation and future work, not as a demonstrated result. Third, the verification methodology's own reach is budget-bounded (Section~\ref{sec:preanswers}). Fourth, the operational layer: the model covers single-pass scheduling decisions only --- no dynamic task arrival, no downlink or ground-station modeling, no cloud-cover attrition, and no replanning loop --- and no wall-clock runtime figure is registered for the promoted runs, so the platform is offered as a falsifiable scheduling substrate, not as an operationally timed planner; operational-cost evaluation is future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Azimuth ambiguity versus the literature: a direction contrast}\label{sec:direction}

The registered correction behind this section is blunt: in this model the ambiguity-versus-squint direction is the \emph{opposite} of the literature anchor it would most naturally be cited against. Under the production PRF and the registered calibration grid, the sliding-spotlight ambiguity ratio improves monotonically with squint: from $-21.35$~dB at zero squint to $-23.32$~dB at the $26^{\circ}$ calibration-grid edge (the registered SLS cap is $26.9^{\circ}$). The stripmap ratio likewise improves ($-21.35$ to $-23.32$~dB over the same registered $0$--$26^{\circ}$ calibration span), and the spotlight ratio is squint-invariant at $-7.05$~dB, exactly as the degenerate-integral structure of Section~\ref{sec:quality} predicts. Song et al.'s platform, by contrast, degrades with scan angle (the anchors quoted in Section~\ref{sec:related}) \citep{song2025spaceborne}; those values are cited as that platform's literature readings only. The contrast also carries its model-dependence: it is a consequence of adopting the rotation-centre semantics (under which the compression factor is squint-independent) together with the registered omission of the absolute Doppler centre, and no experiment in this paper discriminates the two steering semantics; the literature-side mechanism --- a degrading compression factor competing with bandwidth narrowing --- is not modeled here, so the comparison establishes a difference of registered model behavior, not a measurement that refutes the anchor.

The mechanism is the Doppler bandwidth. In this model the spectral compression factor is the design constant $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A$ at every squint (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}), so the \emph{only} squint-dependent channel into the ambiguity integral is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$: squint narrows $B_D$, a narrower band aliases less of the ambiguity power, and the ratio improves. The registered direction contrast therefore differs by mechanism, not merely by sign: it is what remains once the pseudo-boundary of the linearised steering law --- under which the compression factor itself would collapse with squint --- is retired as the extrapolation artifact it is (Section~\ref{sec:geometry}). We flag the contrast rather than reconcile it: the two statements are made under different steering semantics, and the comparison is only legitimate with that stated. A caliber note on the spotlight reading: $-7.05$~dB is the library-edge constant evaluated at the platform-level scene extent (5~km, worst edge), whereas admission evaluates each task at its own scene extent through the 11-point in-window rule of Section~\ref{sec:quality} --- the spotlight aliasing term scales with scene size over range. The two figures are therefore different calibers of the same model, not a contradiction; the admission readings are the ones every feasibility statement inherits.

\subsection{Model caveats}\label{sec:caveats}

\paragraph{f$_{ac}$ absolute position is omitted.} The ambiguity folding is integrated about the direct-current (DC)-relative coordinate, so the absolute Doppler centre $f_{ac}$ never enters the model; only the bandwidth and the compression factor depend on squint. The measured magnitude of the omission at the design-domain edge (worst side, $45^{\circ}$, an extrapolated-domain reading) is 345.7~kHz --- 95.2 times the production PRF of 3630~Hz, and 300.6 times the 1150~Hz registration key --- so the folded ambiguity structure sits far outside the band and the omission is defensible; it is disclosed regardless, and pushing the platform to its full $45^{\circ}$ design domain must carry this number with it. A second simplification of the same family: the antenna pattern carries azimuth-frequency dependence only --- there is no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so the absolute ambiguity level is an azimuth-integration reading; no verdict in this paper rests on the unmodeled elevation-plane attenuation, and the direction contrast of Section~\ref{sec:direction} is carried by the bandwidth channel alone.

\paragraph{The C2 maneuver angle is a two-sided lower bound.} The inter-task requirement of Section~\ref{sec:dwell} forms the maneuver angle from the difference of the tasks' start squints. This is a lower bound on the true attitude excursion from two independent sides: it omits the beam sweep during each dwell (the per-task sweep rate is $0.6686^{\circ}$/s at $30^{\circ}$ incidence, $0.5599^{\circ}$/s at $45^{\circ}$, and $0.4233^{\circ}$/s at $60^{\circ}$, i.e.\ a $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ excursion across a 4~s dwell that the start-point difference never sees), and it understates the off-vertical attitude compression by 15--25\%. The 25\% inter-task pad is therefore a \emph{margin argument}, not a correctness argument: it buys slack against a bound that is systematically low, and the paper never describes the bound as an exact measure of the attitude change. One cross-reference closes this caveat: the per-task sweep rates quoted here are the antenna steering rates of Section~\ref{sec:dwell}'s definition, and the antenna's own sweep during a dwell is the quantity the $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ excursion describes --- the dwell that feeds C2 comes from the broadside-derived closure whose registered approximation status Section~\ref{sec:dwell} records.

\paragraph{g$_T$ has no external cross-validation.} The transmit-path gain term is self-derived and internally verified; the registered search for an external source found no noise-equivalent equation set in the Song platform paper (zero hits across its full text) and the textbook is not in the library with resolvable pages \citep{song2025spaceborne,cumming2005digital}. The verification route is therefore the internal identity chain of Section~\ref{sec:quality} and its pytest guard, and the absence of external cross-validation stands as a limitation in its own right. Consistent with the objective-level scope note of Section~\ref{sec:quality}, no radiometric quantity enters the traded objective; the gain term shapes the resolution index but does not import a noise model.

\paragraph{The range-resolution anchor is a design constant.} $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of 0.5~m, registered at $45^{\circ}$ incidence with scene-centre semantics. Under the real-window incidence band (30--60$^{\circ}$) a physical range resolution would vary by roughly $-18\%$/$+41\%$ across the band (the $\sin\eta_i$ projection of the frozen design-incidence bandwidth: $-18\%$ at $60^{\circ}$, $+41\%$ at $30^{\circ}$, each relative to the $45^{\circ}$ anchor); the constant is a modeling choice that keeps the second objective comparable across windows, and the magnitude narrative of any quality reading inherits this caveat.

\paragraph{Per-mode calibers are not economically comparable.} The spotlight admission in the mode library is defined per point target while the sliding-spotlight scenes are scene-level, and the scheduler consumes the scene-level dwell; the two calibers are registered as asymmetric. Any statement that trades dwell economy across modes --- a spotlight dwell against a sliding-spotlight dwell as if they purchased the same thing --- is outside what the model supports, and none is made.

\subsection{Design semantics and pre-answers}\label{sec:preanswers}

\paragraph{Why not jointly optimize mode and time?} Because the mode is a product-order property, not a scheduler preference. The mode follows from the ordered (resolution requirement, scene size) pair by the coarsest-sufficient library table; making it a decision gene would let the optimizer silently upgrade or downgrade the product the customer ordered. The sliding factor is likewise a system parameter. The registered evidence is narrower than that argument needs, and stating it precisely matters: the reading-axis verdict is no\_promotion across all 90 units under the current demand mix, so a mode decision gene has no registered activation scenario in this scenario world; whether a mode gene would change the served set under a different demand mix is untested, and the model-level ALIVE verdicts for R2F/R3H leave that open. Within the registered world, the joint version would add decision variables without a registered mechanism to matter; joint mode-and-time allocation remains future work for regimes where that activation exists.

\paragraph{The verification criterion is budget-bounded.} The convergence calibration of Section~\ref{sec:convergence} is falsifiable in principle: its judgment rule was frozen before the ladder ran, and any future budget extension can overturn the NOT-PLATEAUED state by satisfying the plateau rule. In practice the ladder is capped by a registered budget decision --- chasing budget was judged to buy frontier richness, not verdicts, since the reading-axis verdict is already robust across the 16$\times$ ladder (no\_promotion over all 90 units) --- so the falsification route exists on paper and is priced out by policy. That asymmetry is disclosed rather than hidden, and it bounds every frontier statement in this paper.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper made the imaging physics of a three-mode agile SAR platform --- mode, dwell, and image quality --- into the decision substance of satellite scheduling, and it made the resulting claims auditable. On the platform leg, the sliding-spotlight dwell is an endogenous consequence of the mode continuum and the selected start, the quality index of the served set is a first-class objective, and the maneuver-coupled feasibility chain prices every schedule in physically meaningful time. On the co-headline legs, the per-pass capacity law supplies the registered containment discipline for what a pass can carry, and the convergence-verification methodology separates what the scenario owns from what the search budget owns --- including the falsified base-budget reading that the preregistered ladder caught and retired.

The registered verdicts are deliberately modest. The structural axis says the real-window treatment families admit a spread above the threshold at the model level, and the control family does not; the reading axis says none of the 90 units promotes, robustly across the budget ladder; the quality-headroom test on the pooled stratum is powered for one family and honestly under-powered for the other. Every frontier reading carries its honesty qualifier, because the plateau rule was never satisfied.

Future work follows the registered deferrals: recomputing the serial bound under the real-window scenario world so that the capacity law can carry numbers again; the sensor-layer design question of resolution against swath, which needs a width-versus-factor model this simulation does not have; joint mode-and-time allocation for regimes where a mode decision gene would actually activate; and the density-axis companion study, which scales the same platform to the regime where the binding constraints trade places. To the registered deferrals this revision adds one: an operational-cost evaluation of the platform --- wall-clock cost at the registered scales and comparison against operationally recognisable baselines --- which requires measurements outside the promoted artifacts and is therefore deferred rather than asserted here.
