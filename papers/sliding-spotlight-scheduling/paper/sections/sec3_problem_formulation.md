# 3. Problem Formulation and Scheduling Model

This section formalises the observation geometry, the three-mode imaging library, the dwell-duration closures, the per-mode image-quality model, and the constraints and objectives of the scheduling problem.

## 3.1 Observation geometry and imaging modes

We adopt the X-band reference platform of Kim et al. \cite{kim2021optimal}: altitude $h=570$ km, inclination $45^{\circ}$, wavelength $\lambda=0.031$ m, azimuth beam width $\theta_a=0.36^{\circ}$, and a square scene of 5 km side length. The derived geometry is $D=\lambda/\theta_a=4.9338$ m (antenna azimuth length), aperture-centre slant range $R_0=775.4$ km, and inertial scene velocity $v_s=7578.05$ m/s. This is the platform inertial velocity, registered as the scene-traversal velocity of this scenario set rather than the beam-footprint ground velocity; it enters every dwell closure and the Doppler bandwidth as the single frozen value, so cross-mode and cross-squint dwell ratios are invariant to the choice, while absolute dwell durations and every reading that mixes dwells with velocity-independent constants (the coverage readings) or evaluates the ambiguity ratio at a fixed PRF carries the convention. Each task is a scene; the scheduled quantity is *when* to image it, not *whether* to reconfigure the payload.

The three imaging modes --- stripmap (STR), sliding spotlight (SLS), and spotlight (SPT) --- are treated as a single-parameter continuum over the position of a fixed virtual rotation centre \cite{kim2021optimal,zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}: writing $k$ for the rotation-centre parameter and $A=1-k$ for the sliding factor, $k\to 0$ places the rotation centre at infinity (stripmap, parallel antenna axis), $0<k<1$ places it below the surface beyond the scene (sliding spotlight), and $k\to 1$ places it at the target (spotlight). The registered operating point of this study is $k=0.797317$, i.e. a rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance $r_0=197119$ m $\approx 197$ km. We stress the symbol discipline that the two distances are dimensionally identical but semantically distinct: $r_0\approx 197$ km is the rotation-centre-to-scene-centre distance, whereas $R_0\approx 775.4$ km is the aperture-centre slant range; their ratio is $r_0/R_0=0.254207$, and the two are never interchangeable (Fig. 1).

The sliding factor: under the rotation-centre semantics adopted here, $A=r_0/(R_0+r_0)$ \cite{zhang2018sliding}; the form $A=1-R_0 k_\theta/v_g$, with $k_\theta$ the azimuth steering rate and $v_g$ the ground speed (Song Eq.(1)) \cite{song2025spaceborne}, is a restatement within the same rotation-centre model --- for the steering rate that places the rotation centre at $r_0$ --- not an independent literature identity. The per-mode azimuth resolutions at zero squint are $\rho_{a,\mathrm{STR}}=D/2=2.4669$ m, $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SLS}}=A\,D/2=0.5000$ m, and $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SPT}}=\lambda/(2\beta_{\mathrm{span}})=0.3000$ m with $\beta_{\mathrm{span}}=2.9603^{\circ}$ the spotlight sweep span (library design constant).

Under the rotation-centre semantics the spectral compression factor is a constant at every squint. Presenting the Doppler rates in the aperture-range form,
$$K_a=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda R_0},\qquad
K_c=\frac{2v_s^{2}\cos^{3}\bar\psi}{\lambda\,(R_0+r_0)},\qquad
a_{\mathrm{res}}=1-\frac{K_c}{K_a}\equiv A,$$
the $\cos^3$ factors cancel at beam-centre crossing, so the compression factor $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A$ is squint-invariant --- it is the achieved resolution that still carries the $1/\cos\bar\psi$ projection of Section 2.3, which the C5 parameter laws compensate --- and the sliding-spotlight zero-squint design resolution is the constant $A\,D/2$ \cite{zhang2018sliding,song2025spaceborne}.

The registered squint domains are separate registered fields, listed apart from the literature task envelope of Kim et al. ($\pm 12^{\circ}$, their Table 2 \cite{kim2021optimal}): STR and SPT carry a design domain of $\pm 45^{\circ}$ --- for SPT the reachable start domain is narrower, because the formation-point clip $|\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}|+\beta_{\mathrm{task}}/2\le 45^{\circ}$ bounds starts to about $43^{\circ}$ at the finest order --- and SLS carries a registered conservative envelope of $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$. The SLS cap is a *conservative design choice* --- antenna steering margin plus frozen-data continuity --- and not a physical boundary, with no analytic derivation claimed (the cap sits $0.14^{\circ}$ above a retired linearisation boundary of $26.7567^{\circ}$; the proximity is a numerical coincidence, not a physical relation): under the rotation-centre semantics $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A>0$ at every squint, so high-squint reachability is limited by the registered cap rather than by mode physics. Two extrapolation disclosures attach to the $45^{\circ}$ design domain: the azimuth-resolution projection correction (resolution-ellipse rotation) is outside the closed form, and the $45^{\circ}$ dwell readings of STR/SPT are extrapolated, because the planar tan-difference closure omits Earth curvature against the full-curvature $R_0$ (Section 7.3).

## 3.2 Dwell duration and visibility windows

The dwell of a task is the synthetic-aperture time (SAT) at scene level: the time the beam needs to sweep the full square scene at the task's squint, where $W_a$ denotes the scene (square) size --- 5 km on the reference platform --- entering as a scene-length traversal term. The production closures follow the reference geometry's scene-level sweep-span construction, evaluated tan-exactly about the centre squint (the symmetric construction quantified against the exact chain below) \cite{kim2021optimal}: for STR the scene dwell is the exact tan difference of the beam-edge crossing angles plus the scene traversal $W_a/v_s$; for SLS it is the symmetric construction of Kim et al.'s Eq.(22)/(24) --- $R_0\big[\tan(\theta_{s0}+\theta_a/2)-\tan(\theta_{s1}-\theta_a/2)\big]+W_a$ over $v_s$, where the broadened sweep $\alpha=\theta_a+(R_0\theta_a+W_a)/r_0$ carries the task requirement through $r_0=R_0\theta_a/(\lambda/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})-\theta_a)$ (Eq.(16),(18)), equivalently through $R_0+r_0=R_0/(1-A_{\mathrm{task}})$ with the task-level sliding factor $A_{\mathrm{task}}=2\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/D$ (the planar oracle below instead carries $A_{\mathrm{task}}$ directly in the denominator); for SPT it is the exact Eq.(10)-(11) tan-difference with the task-level sweep span $\beta_{\mathrm{task}}=\lambda\sec\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$. The SLS and SPT closures are evaluated at these task-level parameters derived from the requirement (constraint C5, Section 3.4), so the delivered resolution equals the required resolution by construction and a tighter requirement spends more dwell; STR keeps its library-floor values, and the mode assignment itself is frozen under the demand scan (grandfathered, Section 3.5). The production construction itself is a further approximation to the exact rotating-centre chain of the same source (the Eq.(20)--(23) quadratic for the end squint, implemented and used here as an internal identity check): evaluated over the registered SLS envelope and the fine-tier requirement range, the symmetric construction deviates from the exact chain by at most $1.9\%$ ($+1.87\%$ at the envelope corner, $+0.31\%$ at the reference squint of $12^{\circ}$ with the sign depending on the requirement), and the registered scene term $W_a$ enters unscaled while the planar oracle applies the squint correction to it --- both semantics are carried with the closure. A planar closed form with the $\sec^2\bar\psi$ squint correction, $T_{\mathrm{SLS}}=(R_0\theta_a+W_a)/(A\,v_s)\cdot\sec^2\bar\psi$ (scene size inside the correction), survives as the test-time oracle: at the Kim reference squint of $12^{\circ}$ it deviates from the production chain by $+0.4185\%$, and the deviation grows to $+2.1\%$ at the SLS cap and $+5.3\%$ at $45^{\circ}$ (an extrapolated-domain reading, Section 3.1), which is what triggered the kill-test switch from the planar form to the tan-exact chain in the first place. A frozen window-tail pad of 1% of the dwell is carried as a conservative margin on the production path; it is not claimed to bound the production closure's own squint error, which is not separately quantified here. Two provenance notes complete the closure. First, the Kim Eq.(22)/(24) chain is a zero-Doppler-clamped (broadside) attitude construction; applying it at nonzero squint is a registered approximation, and the growth of the planar oracle's deviation with squint, quantified above, bounds the oracle's linearisation error rather than the production chain's --- it is evidence that the broadside-derived closure carries a modelling gap rather than numerical noise. Second, the resulting dwell feeds the inter-task transfer requirement (constraint C2, defined in Section 3.4) that determines pass capacity, which is why the deviation pattern and the frozen window-tail pad are carried with the constraint semantics (Section 7.3) rather than with the numerics. Figure 2 draws the resulting law: flat for stripmap, steepening for the two fine modes, with the sliding branch degenerating onto the stripmap transit at the floor.

Each task carries one or more visibility windows produced by ground-track pass enumeration over the scenario span; a window fixes the closest-approach epoch $t_{\mathrm{ca}}$, the per-window slant range, and the earliest/latest feasible imaging start. The decision variable $\tau\in[0,1]$ of a selected task is mapped onto the feasible start interval of its window (Section 4). The squint at the selected start, $\bar\psi$, is measured at the window's geometry.

One scheduling-level geometric quantity needs a definition here because it enters the inter-task constraint: the maneuver angle between consecutive tasks $i,j$, $d_\psi=|\psi_i-\psi_j|$, is the difference of their start squints. This is a planar-convention *lower bound* on the true attitude excursion: it omits the sweep *during imaging* (the antenna keeps steering at $0.4233$--$0.6686^{\circ}/\mathrm{s}$, i.e. $1.7$--$2.7^{\circ}$ over a $4$ s dwell) and the start-squint difference understates the off-vertical attitude compression by 15--25%. The full disclosure of the two-sided understatement and the margin-pad argument is given in Section 7.3.

## 3.3 Image quality model

The per-task quality index is an inverse-pixel-area form with a range-normalised (non-normalised, $m^{-2}$ convention) readout, $$f_{q,i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}\,\rho_{a}(m_i)}\;g_T(m_i)\,\Big(\frac{R_0}{R}\Big)^{3},$$ where $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is the ground-range resolution, $\rho_a(m_i)$ the zero-squint azimuth resolution of the assigned mode, and $g_T$ the dwell-gain ratio --- the mode's point-target integration time over the stripmap point-target integration time (exactly $1/A$ for sliding spotlight); no antenna gain enters it. The $(R_0/R)^3$ factor carries the range dependence of the radiometric term, and its derivation runs through the integration time, not the cell: two-way spreading falls as $R^{-4}$ while the coherently integrated energy grows with the integration time $T\propto R/\rho_a$ at fixed resolution, so a noise-equivalent backscatter referenced to a fixed-resolution cell scales as $R^{3}$; in this model $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ and $\rho_a$ are range-independent constants, so the factor is carried as a *relative* normalisation without calibration, at $R=R_0$ the identity chain collapses to $f_q=(g_T/\rho_a)\cdot(1/\rho_{\mathrm{rg}})$ \cite{song2025spaceborne}. A scope note on the name: the index is dominated by its azimuth-resolution term, and no *calibrated* radiometric quantity --- noise-equivalent sigma zero, signal-to-noise ratio, or radiometric resolution --- enters the index: the only radiometric-shaped factor is the uncalibrated relative range dependence above, and it is evaluated at each window's own slant range, so the index of a task depends on which of its windows is scheduled (Section 7.3); the ambiguity ratio enters separately as an admission constraint, not as a traded objective. "Image quality" in this paper therefore denotes spatial-resolution quality under a gain-shaped multiplier.

The index has two roles in this paper: it states a platform capability, and it supplies the numerator of a reported reading. As a capability statement, at the zero-squint library convention the per-mode constants are $0.8107$ (STR), $19.7352$ (SLS) and $54.8322$ (SPT) m$^{-2}$: magnitude ratios of $24.3\times$ (SLS over STR), $67.6\times$ (SPT over STR) and $2.8\times$ (SPT over SLS). Under the registered fixture two of these constants also admit a direct recomputation form: the stripmap and sliding-spotlight dwell gains carry the exact product identity $g_T(m)\,\rho_a(m)=D/2$, so with the held $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}=0.5$ m they are equivalently $D/\rho_a^{2}$; the spotlight gain is the tan-exact point-dwell ratio rather than the tier ratio, so its constant sits a fraction of a percent above that form. Because the squint factor cancels inside the gain term (exactly for STR and SLS, and to within $0.07\%$ for SPT across the full envelope), these constants --- not squint-dependent readings --- are what separates the modes on this axis, and they are properties of the mode library rather than results of a schedule. One construction choice of the index is stated with them: the explicit $1/\rho_a$ factor sits on top of a gain term that itself scales as $1/\rho_a$ (their product is the registered constant $D/2$), so the index deliberately weights azimuth resolution twice, and the magnitude ratios quoted above ($24.3\times$, $67.6\times$, $2.8\times$) are properties of this chosen index rather than radiometric capability statements.

The reported reading is a fulfillment ratio (the per-family readings of Section 6.8). For a served task it is the $f_{q}$ at the realised window and the selected start's squint, divided by the best $f_{q}$ the task could reach over all of its windows at zero squint, so the ratio is at most one (to within the 0.07% squint variation of the spotlight gain). It is computed on the library convention, averaged over all tie-set members rather than read off the anchor solution, and reported as a reading with no optimization role. Its label travels with every number it states: *[library convention $f_q$ | per-task $f_q$ at the realised window and squint over the zero-squint optimum over all windows | mean over all tie-set members | reported reading, not an optimization target]*.

Three convention statements are frozen with this definition. First, $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of $0.5$ m (registered at $45^{\circ}$ incidence, scene-centre semantics); it is not recomputed per task, and the magnitude narrative carries a limitation (Section 7.3). Second, $g_T$ is *self-derived* and *internally verified*: no external literature source carries the noise-equivalent sigma zero (NESZ) equation set for this mode family, so the verification route is the internal identity chain above (and its regression guard), and the absence of external cross-validation is disclosed. Third, the squint-dependent achieved resolution $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}(m,\bar\psi)=\rho_a(m,0)/\cos\bar\psi$ is registered for reporting; the router consumes the zero-squint constant, and the only bandwidth consumer of $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}$ is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$ --- there is no consumer of the achieved resolution in mode routing. The C5 parameter laws are its inverse: setting $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}=\rho_{\mathrm{req}}$ in $\rho_{a,\mathrm{ach}}=A\,D/(2\cos\bar\psi)$ solves to $A_{\mathrm{task}}=2\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\bar\psi/D$, and in $\lambda/(2\beta\cos\bar\psi)$ to $\beta_{\mathrm{task}}=\lambda\sec\bar\psi/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$ --- the secant is load-bearing for the delivery identity and the STR start clip, and a reporting caliber only for the quality index. The projection relation behind the anchor is $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}=c/(2\,B_{\mathrm{rg}}\sin\eta_i)$ with the range bandwidth $B_{\mathrm{rg}}$ frozen at the design incidence; the planar evaluation layer holds the incidence at that design value, which is why $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ enters the objective as a constant, with the incidence-band variation disclosed separately in Section 7.3.

The azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio (AASR) is defined with the ambiguity sum *excluding* the *zeroth* (principal-image) term,
$$\mathrm{AASR}=\frac{\sum_{m\neq 0}\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G\big(f_a+m\cdot\mathrm{PRF}\big)\big|^{2}\,df_a}{\int_{-B_D/2+f_{ac}}^{B_D/2+f_{ac}}\big|G(f_a)\big|^{2}\,df_a},$$
following the standard convention \cite{cumming2005digital}: keeping $m=0$ would place the principal image in its own numerator and force $\mathrm{AASR}\ge 1$ identically. The frequency-axis rescaling by $(1-K_c/K_a)\approx(1-k_\theta R_0/v_g)$ --- a flat-ground bridge between the $K_c$ and $v_g$ forms, per the frozen formula registration --- under rotation-centre steering is what couples the steering geometry into the ambiguity integral \cite{song2025spaceborne}. The displayed integral is Song's general form, with the absolute-centre shift $f_{ac}$ carried in the integration limits; the implementation integrates the same sums about the DC-relative coordinate, where that shift cancels and the absolute centre drops out (Section 7.3). A footnote on the spotlight special case: Song et al.'s spotlight AASR \cite{song2025spaceborne} is the degenerate integral of the same form in which neither numerator nor denominator contains $f_a$ after the rescaling, which is why the SPT ambiguity in our model is squint-invariant. Integration convention: the ambiguity sum runs over the azimuth-frequency axis alone --- the two-way pattern enters as a function of azimuth frequency, with no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so far-ambiguous-pulse attenuation from the elevation plane is not modeled; the principal effect of that simplification falls on the absolute ambiguity level, and it is registered with the model caveats (Section 7.3).

Admission uses a *discrete* worst-case readout, *not continuous* evaluation: the AASR of a task is evaluated at $11$ equally spaced *in-window* samples and the maximum must not exceed the registered $-13$ dB admission threshold at generation time. Feasibility statements in this paper inherit that convention --- a selection is feasible with respect to the generator's 11-point admission rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own.

## 3.4 Constraints and objectives

The decision vector of a scenario with $N$ tasks is $x=(s,\tau)\in\{0,1\}^{N}\times[0,1]^{N}$: a selection bit per task and a start-time fraction per selected task. The imaging mode $m_i$ is *not* a decision gene: the pair (required resolution, scene size) is mapped to a mode by the library table, a *system design input with product requirement semantics* --- the platform is told what product a customer requires --- and the mode assignment is frozen for the data of this paper (Section 3.5). What the scheduler does inherit from the requirement is the mode's parameterisation: for sliding spotlight and spotlight the design parameters are functions of $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}$ (constraint C5), so the requirement reaches the dwell through the mode rather than through a decision variable. Joint mode-and-time allocation is future work.

Five constraint families apply.

- **C1 (geometry, generation side).** A selected start must fall inside a visibility window of the task whose node satisfies the serviceability evidence: *every* node in the dense domain carries *at least one* in-band transit over the span ($25/25$ nodes over 72 h, per-node reading taken on the registered domain construction).
- **C2 (inter-task transfer).** For consecutive tasks in start-time order, the gap must cover $(1+0.25)\,T_{\mathrm{dwell},i}+t_{\mathrm{man}}(d_\psi,\omega_{\mathrm{start}},\omega_{\mathrm{end}})+t_{\mathrm{settle}}$: the $25\%$ pad multiplies the *dwell* (Kim's own margin semantics, stated in four places in \cite{kim2021optimal}), never the maneuver, and the settle term is an unpadded measured constant ($t_{\mathrm{settle}}=2.0$ s). Production evaluates $t_{\mathrm{man}}$ as a registered hybrid: the analytic model stands alone only where it reaches at least half of the measured calibration anchor ($1.84$ s, Kim scenario 1); below that trigger the per-transition maneuver bound falls back to the worst measured required maneuver time ($4.07$ s, pure maneuver, Kim scenario 2) --- a conservative substitution; the calibration under-prediction it guards is quantified with the transfer-time composition in Section 7.3. The conflict predicate uses the bare dwell: pads are scheduling margin, not physical occupancy.
- **C3 (energy/duty).** Per-task duty is normalised against the per-task envelope reference --- the largest task-law scene dwell admissible for the task's own order at the measured window's slant range, over the modes that can serve it (library-calibre anchor of this reference: $T_{\mathrm{env,max}}=10.5830$ s at the $45^{\circ}$ edge, attained by SPT, a planar-closure reading inheriting the Earth-curvature extrapolation of Section 3.1) --- with mode weights STR/SLS/SPT $=1.0/1.15/1.3$; the normalisation denominator is the largest weight times that per-task reference (the C4 reference below omits this weight factor).
- **C4 (solid-state memory).** Per-echo bytes follow $\mathrm{PRF}\cdot T\cdot N_r\cdot b_q\cdot 2/8$ with $N_r=10{,}000$ range samples and $b_q=3$ bits per component (TerraSAR-X-style block-adaptive quantisation), normalised against the same per-window reference as C3 (each measured window's own slant range, the convention registered for this scenario set) under the single production PRF of $3630$ Hz.
- **C5 (required resolution, evaluation side).** Every selected task must be imaged at its required azimuth resolution $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}$. For sliding spotlight and spotlight the mode parameters are derived from the requirement at the selected start's squint $\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}$ --- $A_{\mathrm{task}}=2\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/D$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{task}}=\lambda\sec\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/(2\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$ --- so the delivered resolution equals the requirement by construction; the identity is re-verified at every dwell evaluation against a $10^{-12}$ tolerance, and a violation raises instead of being scored. The claim is pointwise at that start: inside the imaging interval the delivered resolution follows $\rho_{\mathrm{req}}\cos\psi_{\mathrm{sel}}/\cos\psi(t)$, so this paper does not claim a constant delivered resolution across a whole imaging pass. The stripmap mode (STR) has no task-level parameter: its delivered resolution is the squint-dependent floor value $(D/2)/\cos\bar\psi$ --- at or finer than the requirement --- so its admissible start domain is clipped to $|\bar\psi|\le\arccos((D/2)/\rho_{\mathrm{req}})$, a band that tightens continuously as the requirement approaches the floor from above and is empty for any requirement finer than the floor: there the task has no admissible mode, and the response is a step at the floor rather than a continuous degradation.

Two structural facts about C3/C4 are disclosed as design semantics rather than discovered: both budgets are *structurally* *never binding* inside the squint envelope (their normalised values cannot exceed 1.0 there, so the binding channels are C2 and interval conflicts), and the task priorities that drive the objective are drawn uniformly at random as $\mathrm{randint}(1,10)$, i.e. they carry *no physical* provenance --- they are demand-side bookkeeping, not measured value. The non-binding property is itself the product of a dated design decision, not an accident: the normalisation reference was redefined on 2026-10-20, on the day the sweep ran, from the mode-library constant dwell to the per-task envelope-maximum task-law dwell, after the task-law dwells were measured (dated design record) to exceed the library reference by up to $1.2008\times$ on the 50-task family (theoretical worst $1.274\times$) and had produced false violations; the redefinition removes those violations by construction, and both the date and the rationale are part of the record. Across the five constraint families, the set that actually binds in this scenario set has exactly two members: the C2 transfer chain and the stripmap floor. C1 filters at generation time, C3/C4 are structurally slack inside the envelope, and C5 is a raise-guarded identity for the two task-law modes and a start-domain clip for STR, rather than a scored constraint; the inventory above is the registered model, not a claim that five mechanisms bind.

The problem maximises the served priority fraction $f_1=\sum_{i\in S}p_i/\sum_{i}p_i$ over the selections $S$ that satisfy C1--C5. Coverage is the only objective. Under this scenario set's uniform random priorities the metric is close to inert: what the schedule drops under tightening is, to within about two points, a proportional slice of the task set rather than a priority-selected one, so $f_1$ behaves as the served-task fraction. The search optimises one criterion, coverage; a front is a tie-set on coverage, and the quality index of Section 3.3 appears only as the fulfillment reading. The search method is described next.

A reader who meets this model through earlier versions of this line will expect a quality aggregate to be traded against served priority, so the change is stated explicitly. This is not a re-parameterisation of the same problem: the search returns a tie-set instead of a front, and the quality index moves from a decision criterion to a measured property of whatever the scheduler serves. The change is registered as an amendment to the project's preregistration, which vacates the quality-headroom estimand and registers the coverage--demand curve of Section 3.5 as the main result form.

## 3.5 The demand-tightening axis

The experimental axis of this paper is external to the scheduler. A scalar $s$ multiplies every task's required resolution, $\rho_{\mathrm{req},i}(s)=s\,\rho_{i}^{\mathrm{req}}$, with $s=1$ reproducing the requirement as placed and $s<1$ a tighter requirement; nine values are frozen, $s\in\{0.8,0.9,1.0,1.1,1.2,1.3,1.4,1.5,2.0\}$. Scaling touches the requirement only: the scenario population --- geometry, task set, and visibility windows --- is not regenerated, so every point of the grid is one scenario set seen through a different requirement.

The axis is swept under a frozen mode assignment. Which mode a task receives is not re-derived as the requirement tightens: the assignment is the one the scenario set was generated with, inherited unchanged (a dated comparability decision in the project's design record). The reason is comparability --- re-cutting the mode mix at every grid point would confound the coverage response with a routing change --- and it fixes the scope of every conclusion that follows. The results are requirement sensitivity under a fixed mode assignment, not a general statement about how coverage trades against resolution when a router is free to move tasks between modes; the experiment that would answer the general question is a different one and is not run here.

The mode assignment matters most on the stripmap family, because that is where the floor sits. A family whose tasks are all stripmap cannot respond to a tighter requirement continuously: the delivered resolution is the antenna floor, so the family either serves at the floor or finds no admissible mode. The all-stripmap control family R1C meets that floor inside the frozen grid --- at $s=0.8$ the family has no feasible individual in 10 of 10 seeds --- and that outcome is an infeasibility record, not a measured coverage of zero. The two statements are kept apart everywhere in this paper: an infeasible grid point is not a point on the curve, and it is never plotted, averaged, or quoted as one.

Three different mode rules are in play in this project, and a reader should not mistake one for another. (i) The admission path routes a requirement to the coarsest library tier whose zero-squint resolution meets it (the coarsest-sufficient tier routing); that rule consults the requirement and the library alone, one tier per task, with no scene-size test. (ii) The registered selection policy adds the feasibility test ($A_{\mathrm{task}}\in(0,1)$, the mode envelopes, the spotlight footprint bound, the ambiguity gate) and a two-key rule on scene size --- spotlight inside the footprint, sliding spotlight beyond it, stripmap at the coarse end --- and is the policy registered for narrative and for future families (the same dated design record). (iii) The data of this paper re-run neither rule as $s$ moves: they carry rule (i) evaluated once at the as-placed requirement and then frozen --- the grandfathered assignment described above. Where this paper says that the mode follows the requirement, it means that the parameters of the *assigned* mode follow the requirement, not that the assignment itself changes.

Coverage is measured on this axis as a reading: for each family and each grid point, the median over ten seeds of the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, at a single search budget. Three shape criteria are frozen against the curve --- monotone non-decreasing medians across the grid, a saturation arm ($s=2.0$ at least $s=1.5$), and a tight arm ($s=0.8$ below $s=1.0$) --- and they are inherited unchanged from their earlier registration rather than tuned to this scenario set's data. Section 6 reports them as measured, failures included. Coverage admits no pass/fail verdict here: every unit's front is a tie-set carrying one coverage value, so there is nothing for a verdict to decide, and the curve is reported as a reading.
