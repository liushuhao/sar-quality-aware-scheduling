# 7. Discussion and Limitations

## 7.1 Scope and negative claims

Three scope statements are registered before anything is claimed. First, the capacity law carries a per-pass caliber: the registered serial bound is defined per pass, not per orbit, and the closure (the served fraction of the bound) is a containment reading --- it says the schedule fits inside the bound, not that the bound validates the scheduler --- and the minimum-over-pairs transfer time that enters it is a legal lower bound on the true transfer, never an average or a worst case. Under the present real-window scenario world the serial bound has not been recomputed, so this paper reports no capacity number in any form; the law, its caliber, and its discipline are the contribution, and the recomputation is future work. Second, the trade-off this study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum. The antenna-design question of resolution against swath is a sensor-layer consequence that requires a width-versus-factor model this simulation does not have; it is reported as a limitation and future work, not as a demonstrated result. Third, the verification methodology's own reach is budget-bounded (Section 7.4).

## 7.2 Azimuth ambiguity versus the literature: a direction contrast

The registered correction behind this section is blunt: in this model the ambiguity-versus-squint direction is the *opposite* of the literature anchor it would most naturally be cited against. Under the production PRF and the registered calibration grid, the sliding-spotlight ambiguity ratio improves monotonically with squint --- from −21.35 dB at zero squint to −23.32 dB at the 26° envelope edge --- the stripmap ratio likewise improves (−21.35 to −25.40 dB at its 45° design edge, an extrapolated-domain reading), and the spotlight ratio is squint-invariant at −7.05 dB, exactly as the degenerate-integral structure of Section 3.3 predicts. Song et al.'s platform, by contrast, degrades with scan angle (the anchors quoted in Section 2.1) \cite{song2025spaceborne}; those values are cited as that platform's literature readings only.

The mechanism is the Doppler bandwidth. In this model the spectral compression factor is the design constant $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A$ at every squint (Section 3.1), so the *only* squint-dependent channel into the ambiguity integral is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$: squint narrows $B_D$, a narrower band aliases less of the ambiguity power, and the ratio improves. The registered direction contrast therefore differs by mechanism, not merely by sign: it is what remains once the pseudo-boundary of the linearised steering law --- under which the compression factor itself would collapse with squint --- is retired as the extrapolation artifact it is (Section 3.1). We flag the contrast rather than reconcile it: the two statements are made under different steering semantics, and the comparison is only legitimate with that stated.

## 7.3 Model caveats

**f_ac absolute position is omitted.** The ambiguity folding is integrated about the direct-current (DC)-relative coordinate, so the absolute Doppler centre $f_{ac}$ never enters the model; only the bandwidth and the compression factor depend on squint. The measured magnitude of the omission at the design-domain edge (worst side, 45°, an extrapolated-domain reading) is 345.7 kHz --- 95.2 times the production PRF of 3630 Hz, and 300.6 times the 1150 Hz registration key --- so the folded ambiguity structure sits far outside the band and the omission is defensible; it is disclosed regardless, and pushing the platform to its full 45° design domain must carry this number with it.

**The C2 maneuver angle is a two-sided lower bound.** The inter-task requirement of Section 3.2 forms the maneuver angle from the difference of the tasks' start squints. This is a lower bound on the true attitude excursion from two independent sides: it omits the beam sweep during each dwell (the per-task sweep rate is 0.6686°/s at 30° incidence, 0.5599°/s at 45°, and 0.4233°/s at 60°, i.e. a 1.7--2.7° excursion across a 4 s dwell that the start-point difference never sees), and it understates the off-vertical attitude compression by 15-25%. The 25% inter-task pad is therefore a *margin argument*, not a correctness argument: it buys slack against a bound that is systematically low, and the paper never describes the bound as an exact measure of the attitude change.

**g_T has no external cross-validation.** The transmit-path gain term is self-derived and internally verified; the registered search for an external source found no noise-equivalent equation set in the Song platform paper (zero hits across its full text) and the textbook is not in the library with resolvable pages \cite{song2025spaceborne,cumming2005digital}. The verification route is therefore the internal identity chain of Section 3.3 and its pytest guard, and the absence of external cross-validation stands as a limitation in its own right.

**The range-resolution anchor is a design constant.** $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of 0.5 m, registered at 45° incidence with scene-centre semantics. Under the real-window incidence band (30--60°) a physical range resolution would vary by roughly −13%/+41% across the band; the constant is a modeling choice that keeps the second objective comparable across windows, and the magnitude narrative of any quality reading inherits this caveat.

**Per-mode calibers are not economically comparable.** The spotlight admission in the mode library is defined per point target while the sliding-spotlight scenes are scene-level, and the scheduler consumes the scene-level dwell; the two calibers are registered as asymmetric. Any statement that trades dwell economy across modes --- a spotlight dwell against a sliding-spotlight dwell as if they purchased the same thing --- is outside what the model supports, and none is made.

## 7.4 Design semantics and pre-answers

**Why not jointly optimize mode and time?** Because the mode is a product-order property, not a scheduler preference. The mode follows from the ordered (resolution requirement, scene size) pair by the coarsest-sufficient library table; making it a decision gene would let the optimizer silently upgrade or downgrade the product the customer ordered. The sliding factor is likewise a system parameter. The registered probe evidence is that promotion is dead at all densities under the current regime --- there is no activation scenario in which a mode decision gene would change the served set --- so the joint version would add decision variables without a mechanism to matter. Joint mode-and-time allocation remains future work for regimes where that activation exists.

**The verification criterion is budget-bounded.** The convergence calibration of Section 6.3 is falsifiable in principle: its judgment rule was frozen before the ladder ran, and any future budget extension can overturn the NOT-PLATEAUED state by satisfying the plateau rule. In practice the ladder is capped by a registered budget decision --- chasing budget was judged to buy frontier richness, not verdicts, since the reading-axis verdict is already robust across the 16× ladder (no_promotion over all 90 units) --- so the falsification route exists on paper and is priced out by policy. That asymmetry is disclosed rather than hidden, and it bounds every frontier statement in this paper.
