# 7. Discussion and Limitations

## 7.1 Scope and negative claims

Five scope statements are registered before anything is claimed. First, the capacity law carries a per-pass caliber: the registered serial bound is defined per pass, not per orbit, and the closure (the served fraction of the bound) is a containment reading --- it says the schedule fits inside the bound, not that the bound validates the scheduler. The minimum-over-pairs transfer time that enters it is a legal lower bound on the true transfer, never an average or a worst case. Under the present real-window scenario world the serial bound has not been recomputed, so this paper reports no capacity number in any form; the law, its caliber, and its discipline are the contribution, and the recomputation is future work.

Second, the main claim is bounded by its mode assignment. The results of Section 6 are order sensitivity under the frozen mode assignment of Section 3.5: the curve answers what tightening the order costs when the mode a task receives does not change. It does not answer how coverage trades against resolution when a router is free to move tasks between modes, and that experiment is not run here. Third, the curve is read at one registered search budget (Section 5.3), so it is a reading of this world at that budget and not a statement about a converged frontier in either direction.

Fourth, the trade-off this study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum. The antenna-design question of resolution against swath is a sensor-layer consequence that requires a width-versus-factor model this simulation does not have; it is reported as a limitation and future work, not as a demonstrated result. Fifth, the operational layer: the model covers single-pass scheduling decisions only --- no dynamic task arrival, no downlink or ground-station modeling,, and no replanning loop --- and no wall-clock runtime figure is registered for the promoted runs, so the platform is offered as a falsifiable scheduling substrate, not as an operationally timed planner; operational-cost evaluation is future work (Section 8).

## 7.2 Azimuth ambiguity versus the literature: a direction contrast

The registered correction behind this section is blunt: in this model the ambiguity-versus-squint direction is the *opposite* of the literature anchor it would most naturally be cited against. Under the production PRF and the registered calibration grid, the sliding-spotlight ambiguity ratio improves monotonically with squint --- from −21.35 dB at zero squint to −23.32 dB at the 26° calibration-grid edge (the registered SLS cap is 26.9°) --- the stripmap ratio likewise improves (−21.35 to −23.32 dB over the same registered 0–26° calibration span), and the spotlight ratio is squint-invariant at −7.05 dB, exactly as the degenerate-integral structure of Section 3.3 predicts. That the stripmap and sliding-spotlight calibration sequences read identical values is not a transcription error: with $\rho_{a,\mathrm{SLS}}=A\,D/2$ the sliding-spotlight Doppler bandwidth is $B_{D,\mathrm{SLS}}=v_s\cos\bar\psi/(A\,D/2)$, and once the spectrum is compressed by the sliding factor $A$ its bandwidth equals the stripmap bandwidth $2\,v_s\cos\bar\psi/D$ for every $A$, so under the same pattern $G$ and the same PRF the two sequences degenerate point by point. Song et al.'s platform, by contrast, degrades with scan angle (the anchors quoted in Section 2.1) \cite{song2025spaceborne}; those values are cited as that platform's literature readings only. The contrast also carries its model-dependence: it is a consequence of adopting the rotation-centre semantics (under which the compression factor is squint-independent) together with the registered omission of the absolute Doppler centre, and no experiment in this paper discriminates the two steering semantics; the literature-side mechanism --- a degrading compression factor competing with bandwidth narrowing --- is not modeled here, so the comparison establishes a difference of registered model behavior, not a measurement that refutes the anchor. A scope note on the PRF: the production PRF is a registered platform constant of this world, and no PRF-selection or band-widening criterion is exercised; the SLS and SPT processing bandwidths at zero squint, 15.2 and 25.3 kHz, are several times the PRF, and their aliasing structure enters the admission readings through the AASR model evaluated at the registered PRF rather than being avoided by a PRF choice.

The mechanism is the Doppler bandwidth. In this model the spectral compression factor is the design constant $a_{\mathrm{res}}\equiv A$ at every squint (Section 3.1), so the *only* squint-dependent channel into the ambiguity integral is the Doppler bandwidth $B_D=v_s\cos\bar\psi/\rho_a(m,0)$: squint narrows $B_D$, a narrower band aliases less of the ambiguity power, and the ratio improves. The registered direction contrast therefore differs by mechanism, not merely by sign: it is what remains once the pseudo-boundary of the linearised steering law --- under which the compression factor itself would collapse with squint --- is retired as the extrapolation artifact it is (Section 3.1). We flag the contrast rather than reconcile it: the two statements are made under different steering semantics, and the comparison is only legitimate with that stated. A caliber note on the spotlight reading: −7.05 dB is the library-edge constant evaluated at the platform-level scene extent (5 km, worst edge), whereas admission evaluates each task at its own scene extent through the 11-point in-window rule of Section 3.3 --- the spotlight aliasing term scales with scene size over range --- so the two figures are different calibers of the same model, not a contradiction; the admission readings are the ones every feasibility statement inherits.

## 7.3 Model caveats

**f$_{ac}$ absolute position is omitted.** The ambiguity folding is integrated about the direct-current (DC)-relative coordinate, so the absolute Doppler centre $f_{ac}$ never enters the model; only the bandwidth and the compression factor depend on squint. The measured magnitude of the omission at the design-domain edge (worst side, 45°, an extrapolated-domain reading) is 345.7 kHz --- 95.2 times the production PRF of 3630 Hz, and 300.6 times the 1150 Hz registration key --- so the folded ambiguity structure sits far outside the band and the omission is defensible; it is disclosed regardless, and pushing the platform to its full 45° design domain must carry this number with it. A second simplification of the same family: the antenna pattern carries azimuth-frequency dependence only --- there is no cross-range (elevation-plane) pattern factor --- so the absolute ambiguity level is an azimuth-integration reading; no verdict in this paper rests on the unmodeled elevation-plane attenuation, and the direction contrast of Section 7.2 is carried by the bandwidth channel alone.

**The C2 maneuver angle is a two-sided lower bound.** The inter-task requirement of Section 3.2 forms the maneuver angle from the difference of the tasks' start squints. This is a lower bound on the true attitude excursion from two independent sides: it omits the beam sweep during each dwell (the per-task sweep rate is 0.6686°/s at 30° incidence, 0.5599°/s at 45°, and 0.4233°/s at 60°, i.e. a 1.7--2.7° excursion across a 4 s dwell that the start-point difference never sees), and it understates the off-vertical attitude compression by 15-25%. The 25% inter-task pad is therefore a *margin argument*, not a correctness argument: it buys slack against a bound that is systematically low, and the paper never describes the bound as an exact measure of the attitude change. One cross-reference closes this caveat: the per-task sweep rates quoted here are the antenna steering rates of Section 3.2's definition, and the antenna's own sweep during a dwell is the quantity the 1.7--2.7° excursion describes --- the dwell that feeds C2 comes from the broadside-derived closure whose registered approximation status Section 3.2 records.

**g$_{T}$ has no external cross-validation.** The transmit-path gain term is self-derived and internally verified; the registered search for an external source found no noise-equivalent equation set in the Song platform paper (zero hits across its full text) and the textbook is not in the library with resolvable pages \cite{song2025spaceborne,cumming2005digital}. The verification route is therefore the internal identity chain of Section 3.3 and its pytest guard, and the absence of external cross-validation stands as a limitation in its own right. Consistent with the objective-level scope note of Section 3.3, no *calibrated* radiometric quantity enters the objective; the gain term shapes the resolution index and the uncalibrated relative range factor carries the range dependence, but no noise model is imported.

**The range-resolution anchor is a design constant.** $\rho_{\mathrm{rg}}$ is held at the design-anchor constant of 0.5 m, registered at 45° incidence with scene-centre semantics. Under the real-window incidence band (30--60°) a physical range resolution would vary by roughly −18%/+41% across the band (the $\sin\eta_i$ projection of the frozen design-incidence bandwidth: −18% at 60°, +41% at 30°, each relative to the 45° anchor); the constant is a modeling choice that keeps the quality readings comparable across windows, and the magnitude narrative of any quality reading inherits this caveat.

**The quality index depends on the scheduled window.** The range factor $(R_0/R)^3$ of Section 3.3 is evaluated at the slant range of the window in which the selected start falls, and a task's windows are enumerated at different transits, hence at different slant ranges. The index of a task is therefore not a property of the task alone: moving a selection to a different transit changes its index, and with a frozen mode assignment that change is one of the quantities a schedule can still choose. The variation is not corrected anywhere in this paper, and it is the same non-normalisation that leaves index levels incomparable across families (Section 5.2).

**Two quality calibers, and the one this paper reports.** The fulfillment ratio is computed on the library caliber, while the delivered variant $f_{Q,\mathrm{deliv}}$ is computed on a delivered caliber; the two are different quantities at different calibers, are never reported side by side in one table, and are never combined into a ratio of one to the other. The reading this paper reports is the library-caliber one, and its limitation is that it measures a task's realised value against its own zero-squint optimum over its own windows --- a within-task, within-family quantity. It is not a calibrated radiometric statement, it does not compare across families (the units expand scene-dependently, Section 5.2), and it is not the delivered resolution of the schedule. The delivered variant carries a limitation in the other direction: it has no stripmap domain at all, because stripmap delivers the library floor and a task-level mode-parameter request on that mode raises, so the variant is undefined for every task the control family serves.

**Per-mode calibers are not economically comparable.** The spotlight admission in the mode library is defined per point target while the sliding-spotlight scenes are scene-level, and the scheduler consumes the scene-level dwell; the two calibers are registered as asymmetric. Any statement that trades dwell economy across modes --- a spotlight dwell against a sliding-spotlight dwell as if they purchased the same thing --- is outside what the model supports, and none is made.

## 7.4 Design semantics and pre-answers

**Why not jointly optimize mode and time?** Because the mode is a product-order property, not a scheduler preference. The mode follows from the ordered (resolution requirement, scene size) pair by the coarsest-sufficient library table; making it a decision gene would let the optimizer silently upgrade or downgrade the product the customer ordered. The sliding factor is likewise a system parameter. The evidence this paper can offer for the joint formulation being unnecessary here is narrower than that argument needs, and stating it precisely matters: the coverage response is a construction response (Section 4.5), so in this scenario world a unit's outcome is set before the search runs, and a mode gene would add decision variables to a search whose measured contribution is two units out of 260; whether a mode gene would change the served set under a different demand mix is untested. Joint mode-and-time allocation remains future work for regimes where that activation exists.

**The reading is single-budget.** The curve of Section 6 is read at one registered budget, and a future budget extension can move it: nothing in the registered design forbids a re-run at a larger budget, and if a larger budget changed the served sets, the registered criteria of Section 5.3 would be re-measured rather than assumed. In practice the budget was fixed by a registered decision --- raising it was judged to buy a different search rather than a different construction, since the construction face carries the response (Section 4.5) --- so the extension is available on paper and priced out by policy. That asymmetry is disclosed rather than hidden, and it bounds every reading in this paper.
