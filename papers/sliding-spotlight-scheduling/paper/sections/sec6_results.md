# 6. Results and Analysis

All readings in this section are taken from the L8 artifacts at caliber v5 (Section 5.2) and are cross-checked against the reading ledger that stores each of them with its unit, caliber label and source path. The grid is the nine-point demand scan of Section 5.3, every point read at the registered 40/60 budget.

## 6.1 The coverage--demand curves

The result of the paper is the coverage--demand curve (Fig. 3): the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, median over ten seeds, as a function of the demand-tightening factor. Three families produce three different shapes, and the difference between the shapes is the result rather than a nuisance variation to be averaged away.

The 100-drawn / 99-admitted family is the one the tightening actually reaches. Its median coverage falls monotonically with the tightening: 0.4827 at $s=0.8$, 0.6884 at $s=0.9$, 0.9485 at the order point $s=1.0$, then 0.9766 at $s=1.1$, 0.9812 at $s=1.2$, 0.9882 at $s=1.3$, 0.9882 at $s=1.4$, 0.9900 at $s=1.5$ and 0.9900 at $s=2.0$ (family R3H). Tightening the order from the order point to the tightest point that still has a feasible individual costs about half of the served priority mass; loosening it from the order point to the coarsest point buys back under five points. Most of the response therefore sits on the tight side of the order point, which is the side an operator would actually consider.

The 50-drawn / 46-admitted family is nearly flat and sits high. Its median coverage reads 0.9764 at $s=0.8$, 0.9737 at $s=0.9$, 0.9764 at $s=1.0$, 0.9764 at $s=1.1$, 0.9960 at $s=1.2$, 0.9960 at $s=1.3$, 0.9924 at $s=1.4$, 0.9924 at $s=1.5$ and 1.0000 at $s=2.0$ (family R2F). On this family a tighter order is close to free in coverage: the two local declines, of 0.268 and 0.359 percentage points, are small enough that they are given their own accounting (Section 6.4) rather than read as a trend.

The control family is the single-orbit one, 20 drawn and 20 admitted, and its response is a step rather than a curve. Its median coverage reads 0.5398 at $s=0.9$ and 1.0000 from $s=1.0$ all the way to $s=2.0$ (family R1C): the family either serves the whole priority mass at the stripmap floor or serves a strict subset of it, and there is no intermediate regime for it to occupy. Its tightest grid point behaves differently again and is kept apart from the curve (Section 6.2).

Read together, the three curves say what the mode assignment decides. A family whose tasks are all stripmap has a floor and therefore a step; a family that mixes sliding spotlight and spotlight absorbs a tightening in its parameters and pays almost nothing in coverage; and the family that mixes all three modes, at the largest task count, is the one on which the tightening bites. That ordering follows the construction of Section 4.5 --- the modes and their parameter adaptation decide which tasks stay serviceable --- and it is not a property of the search.

## 6.2 The order point, the floor step, and the cost of a tighter order

Every cost in this subsection is measured against the family's own saturation point --- the first grid point at which its median reaches the family's maximum --- and never against another family or another world. That choice is what makes each number comparable to nothing but itself, and it is stated on every line that carries one.

R3H pays a coverage cost of 4.15 percentage points at the order point against its own saturation at $s=1.5$, the first point at which its median reaches 0.9900. R2F pays a coverage cost of 2.36 percentage points at the order point against its own saturation at $s=2.0$, where its median first reaches 1.0000. R1C's cost against its own saturation is 0.00 percentage points, and that zero is a statement about where the family's saturation sits --- the order point is its saturation point --- not a claim that a tightening is free for every family.

The control family's tightest grid point is a different kind of fact and is separated from the curve on purpose. R1C at $s=0.8$ has no feasible individual in 10 of 10 seeds: every unit of that cell exited with the no-feasible-individual code, so the family admits no admissible schedule at that order under the frozen mode assignment. That is not a coverage of zero, and the two must never be interchanged. A zero would say that the search considered schedules and served none; what happened is that the order at $s=0.8$ asks for a resolution finer than the stripmap floor the family is fixed to, so there is no admissible schedule to consider. The figure draws the point as a cross against the axis carrying that label, the ledger stores it as a status rather than as a value, and no average, no smoothing and no line segment passes through it. Every statement about this point in the rest of the paper keeps the same distinction.

## 6.3 The frozen criteria and their verdicts

Table 1 reports the three criteria of Section 5.3 as measured. The control family and the large family meet all three; the control family's infeasible tight point enters the criteria as the zero-coverage outcome registered in Section 5.3. The control family's tight-arm verdict --- met --- is a direct consequence of that convention: under an exclusion convention the tight cell would be undefined and the verdict would carry no value; the monotone verdict (C1) is unchanged under either convention. The 50-task family meets the saturation arm and fails the other two: its medians are not monotone across the grid (C1), and its tight arm does not fall (C3). Both failures are reported as results about this world.

C3's failure carries information about the model, and it is the falsification of a reading rather than a null result. In the previous world this family's tight arm carried a large penalty --- the median at the tightest point was 0.446 of the served priority mass --- and that penalty is *withdrawn*: under the caliber-v5 model the same family reads 0.9764 at $s=0.8$, above its own order-point value, so the tight arm no longer falls at all. The withdrawn value is a historical comparison and is labelled as such wherever it appears; it is not a reading of this world and it is not an estimate this paper stands behind.

What replaces it is smaller and better localized. The tightening still costs this family something, but the cost is measured at the order point against the family's own saturation (2.36 percentage points, Section 6.2), not at the tight arm. The mechanism is the parameter adaptation of constraint C5: the mode parameters are derived from the order at the selected start, so the delivered resolution equals the order by construction, and a tighter order therefore spends dwell rather than serviceability. The family pays in imaging time; it does not pay by losing tasks, until the dwell term grows enough to collide with the transfer constraint. Saying that the cost has vanished would be wrong on both halves of that sentence: the tight-arm penalty is gone, and the order-point cost is not.

C1's failure is the pair of local declines that Section 6.4 accounts for, and it is not removed by re-tuning. A criterion edited after its verdict is not a criterion: both failures stand as this world's answer to questions that were fixed before its data were read.

## 6.4 The two local declines: an anchor-construction effect

Two local declines interrupt the flat band of the 50-task family: 0.268 percentage points between $s=0.8$ and $s=0.9$, and 0.359 percentage points between $s=1.3$ and $s=1.4$. Both are attribution results, and the attribution is not the one a reader would expect.

Neither is a search miss. Over all 180 sweep units of the two treatment families the best-covered member of a unit's tie-set equals the greedy anchor's value exactly, and the search-only maximum is empty; across the whole 260-unit world the search exceeds its anchor in the two control units of Section 4.5 and nowhere else. A decline produced by a search that never misses is not a search miss. It is produced by the anchor construction itself, which is deterministic and reproducible: the anchor re-times the all-selected schedule in a single bounded pass, and at a given order that pass can serve one task fewer or one task more than at the neighbouring order.

The per-seed accounting makes the mechanism visible. Between $s=0.8$ and $s=0.9$ two seeds move in opposite directions --- one loses 1.83 percentage points and one gains 0.42 --- and the median falls because the net of those two displacements is negative. Between $s=1.3$ and $s=1.4$ the same shape recurs with different seeds: one loses 1.14 points and one gains 0.42. In both cases the decline is the median of ten readings following two of its members, not a property of the demand axis.

This is a different object from the search-artifact verdict of the earlier line, and the difference matters for how the reading is used. That verdict retired a reading because a larger search budget removed it: what was retired was an artifact of the search. Here no budget changes the picture, because the effect sits in the construction, which does not depend on the search at all. And nothing physical follows from either decline: two opposite single-seed displacements are not a monotonicity claim in either direction, and no statement about the demand axis is made from a movement in the third decimal place.

## 6.5 Demand satisfaction and retention

The curve says how much priority mass is served; a second reading says what that service amounts to. Over the nine demand points the tie set serves 477,308 task instances, distributed over 9,430 tie-set records: a served task instance is one tie-set member's selection of one task, not a distinct task, and the count is the sum over every record of the tasks that record selects. The delivery identity that constraint C5 enforces --- delivered resolution equal to the order at the selected start --- holds by construction and is checked by a raise rather than by a score, so no artifact stores a per-record verdict; the observable is the constraint violation, whose maximum over every record is 0.0, and no violation is recorded relative to the evaluator for any unit of the sweep. Ten of the 270 launched units exited with no feasible individual (the control family at $s=0.8$) and are excluded from the served count by the driver ledger rather than folded into it as zeros.

The retention reading measures the same service from the schedule's side, and it travels with a caliber that must travel with it. On the STR-clip-only caliber --- the feasible measure retained after the stripmap clip --- retention reads 51.14% for the control family, 100.00% for the 50-task family and 71.00% for the 100-task family. The earlier line's retention figures, which were computed under the retired SLS/SPT narrowing, are superseded and are not quoted here.

## 6.6 Reported readings: fulfillment, its relation to coverage, and the delivered variant

Three readings are reported beside the curve. None of them is an optimization target, and each one travels with the caliber label that defines the quantity it states.

**The fulfillment ratio.** For a served task the ratio is its f$_q$ at the realised window and the selected start's squint, over the best f$_q$ the task could reach over all of its windows at zero squint, so the ratio is at most one; it is averaged over all tie-set members and reported, never optimised. At the order point the fulfillment ratio reads 0.6580 for R1C, 0.7764 for R2F and 0.7447 for R3H. Its label travels with every number it states: *[library caliber $f_q$ | per-task $f_q$ at the realised window and squint over the zero-squint optimum over all windows | mean over all tie-set members | reported reading, not an optimization target]*. What the reading says is narrow and is kept narrow: it measures how much of the zero-squint optimum over a task's own windows survives the choice of window and start, not how good the image is by any calibrated radiometric standard (Section 7.3).

**Its relation to coverage.** No directional claim is registered between coverage and the fulfillment ratio, and none is made here. What is reported is the coefficient and its sample size, with the caliber named on the same line: the Pearson coefficient between the family-by-grid-point coverage and fulfillment readings is $r=0.329$ over $n=26$ points, and over the pooled units it is $r=0.249$ over $n=260$. These are relation readings of this world's data; the sign is not a property of the mode continuum, and no mechanism is claimed for it.

**The delivered variant, and the platform capability behind it.** The descriptive delivered variant $f_{Q,\mathrm{deliv}}$ of Section 3.3 is reported once, under its own label: *[delivered caliber $f_{Q,\mathrm{deliv}}$ | descriptive variant, not an optimization target | not comparable with the library caliber | no stripmap domain]*. At the order point its median for R3H (100 drawn / 99 admitted) is 37.62 m$^{-2}$, and the median squint-loss factor on the same cell is 1.3286. This is a descriptive variant, not comparable with the library caliber, and the two are never placed in the same table. What the platform fixes is the capability statement of Section 3.3: the per-mode index levels are separated by orders of magnitude and are flat in squint (Fig. 4), so which mode a task receives dominates the value any plan can reach on this axis, and the scheduler moves a plan inside the range the demand template sets rather than setting the range itself.
