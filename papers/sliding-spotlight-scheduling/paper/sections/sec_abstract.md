# Abstract

Agile Earth-observation satellites that steer a single antenna across stripmap, sliding-spotlight, and spotlight modes are usually scheduled under an exogenous, fixed imaging duration. For a sliding-spotlight platform that assumption fails measurably: planning with the fixed library dwell violates the transfer constraint in 20--29\% of consecutive pairs on the dense families and lands 18--30 points below the endogenous dwell. This paper makes the dwell endogenous and carries the required resolution as a per-task hard constraint: the mode parameters follow the requirement, the delivered resolution equals it at the selected start, and the stripmap tier stays at its antenna floor. Served priority is the only objective; image-quality readings are reported, not optimised. A frozen nine-point demand grid scales every requirement from 0.8 to 2.0, and three shape criteria are frozen in advance and reported with their failures. Tightening is where coverage moves: the dense 100-task family drops 26.0 percentage points from the as-placed requirement to the next point down and 46.6 points to the tightest feasible point; the single-satellite family drops 46.0 points at $s=0.9$ and becomes infeasible at $s=0.8$; the 50-task family is nearly flat, within 0.27 points throughout. Loosening from the as-placed point recovers 4.15 percentage points for the 100-task family against its own saturation and 2.36 points for the 50-task family (0.93 points paired per seed). Which mode a task receives decides what a requirement change costs; conclusions are scoped to requirement sensitivity under this fixed mode assignment.
