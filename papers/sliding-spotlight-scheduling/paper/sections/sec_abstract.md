# Abstract

Agile Earth-observation satellites that steer a single antenna across stripmap, sliding-spotlight, and spotlight modes are usually scheduled under an exogenous, fixed imaging duration. For a sliding-spotlight platform that assumption fails in a quantifiable way: the dwell is produced by the mode continuum and by the selected start, and it enters the inter-task transfer constraint that decides what a pass can carry. This paper makes the dwell endogenous and carries the order resolution requirement as a per-task hard constraint: the mode parameters follow the order where the mode has them, so that the resolution delivered at the selected start equals the order, while the stripmap tier stays at its antenna floor. Served priority is the only objective; a fulfillment ratio, computed on the library quality caliber and averaged over all tie-set members, is a reported reading and not an optimization target. A frozen nine-point demand grid scales every order from 0.8 to 2.0 without regenerating the scenario population or re-routing modes, and three shape criteria (monotone, saturation, tight arm) are frozen in advance and reported with their failures. The dense 100-task family R3H pays a coverage cost at the order point of 4.15 percentage points against its own saturation. The single-orbit family R1C meets a floor step: at $s=0.8$ it admits no feasible individual in 10 of 10 seeds. R1C at $s=0.9$ reads a median coverage of 0.5398. The 50-task family R2F pays a coverage cost at the order point of 2.36 percentage points against its own saturation. Over the nine points 477,308 task instances are served, with no delivery-identity violation recorded by the evaluator; the identity holds by construction and any deviation aborts the run. Conclusions are scoped to order sensitivity under a fixed mode assignment.
