# 5. Experimental Design and Setup

This section describes the scenario set, the family and convention system, and the frozen analysis design.

## 5.1 Scenario construction

The scenario set is built from *real ground-track transit enumeration*, not from an arbitrary orbit phase point: visibility windows come from enumerating true ground-track passes over the scenario span, with a 15 s scan step, gap merging at 300 s (a registered band parameter, invariant across the registered 150/600/1200 s sensitivity band, not a physical lower bound), and 33-point edge refinement. The registry's own ledger records why: under the earlier phase-point pass rule only 0 of 8 clusters were serviceable after the incidence fix, against 6 of 8 under true enumeration. The demand mix itself --- resolution requirements and scene sizes --- is a registered synthetic template parameter, not a measured campaign profile; the priorities that drive the objective are uniform random integers with no physical provenance (Section 3.4), so the mode composition of the served set reflects the template's requirement distribution. The template draws each task's resolution requirement from two frozen tiers --- a coarse tier spanning [2.5, 3.0] m and a fine tier spanning [0.3, 0.6] m --- so the stripmap floor of 2.4669 m (Section 3.3) sits inside the coarse tier, and the tight end of the demand grid scales requirements below what the floor's mode can serve; Section 6.7 shifts the tier multiplicatively and watches the floor step move to the predicted cell.

Orbit propagation is two-body only --- no $J_2$ or atmospheric terms --- so the registered repeat figure is the *simulated* ground-track repeat of 14.97 orbits $\approx$ 23.93 h (one sidereal day over the two-body period); the figure that includes $J_2$ nodal drift describes the physical orbit and is deliberately not the one this simulator produces. The surface normal used in incidence computations is an *approximate* normal: its maximum deviation from the true ellipsoidal normal is 0.125° (at $\approx$30° latitude; 0.109° at the dense-domain centre), with no effect on band-boundary judgements at the registered margins. The dense domain is a 1000 km square centred at 20°N/0°E, a 5×5 node grid at 250 km spacing with per-cell target jitter, a 3-day span in coordinated universal time (UTC). The serviceability criterion is per-node: every node carries at least one in-band transit within the span, measured 25/25; the literal best-incidence reading of the dense-domain constraint fails at the centre node (near-nadir passes exist there but yield no in-band window), which is why the operative criterion is the per-node one. The $25/25$ reading is a construction-time measurement on the full-span domain; the manifest ledger stores aggregate admission counts, not per-node columns.

The AASR admission criterion is evaluated per family, and the two families differ by design --- a discrepancy we disclose rather than harmonise post hoc. Synthetic M-family windows are admitted by a window-edge single-point readout (the AASR at the window edge squint only), whereas the real-window R families sample the AASR at 11 evenly spaced in-window instants ($\mathrm{lo}+(\mathrm{hi}-\mathrm{lo})\,j/10$, $j=0,\dots,10$) and admit the window only if the worst of the 11 samples meets the threshold; a sample outside the mode envelope contributes an exclusion, so it can never mask an unserviceable window. The window-edge single-point rule *underestimates* the in-window worst case by about 4 dB, so the two admission rules are stated per family wherever admission is discussed; the R-family rule is the discrete in-window worst-case convention that all feasibility statements in this paper inherit (Section 3.3). This sampling is *discrete*, *not continuous* evaluation: a window admitted under it is feasible with respect to the 11-point rule, and the evaluator performs no AASR recheck of its own. Two further conservative narrowings bound the admissible window set and are carried with the same convention: a pass is not admitted when the scene along-track footprint exceeds the beam-limited bound $R_0\,\theta_a$ at the closest-approach slant range of that pass (binding for SPT), and the envelope mask admits starts only inside a $1\%$ inward guard of the mode envelope, within a $\pm120$ s span around the pass closest approach, with each admitted mask run inset by one scan step ($\approx$1 s) per end. Across the 30 released R-family manifests the generation ledger records zero tasks removed by the AASR threshold (removals: aasr 0, footprint 0, below-finest 0; no-window 15), so the threshold is part of the admission convention without ever binding in this scenario set.

## 5.2 Families and conventions

Three real-window families form the experiment matrix, and their sizes are stated in the two numbers the registered density ladder fixes, the tasks drawn and the tasks admitted (serviceable): R1C (20 drawn, 20 admitted, 145--162 windows) is the all-stripmap control; R2F (50 drawn, 46--50 admitted, 337--391 windows) and R3H (100 drawn, 99--100 admitted, 731--779 windows) are the dense-cluster treatments. Counts are stated as intervals over the ten seeds of each family; a single-seed value is not a family constant. Task counts follow the registered density ladder $N=20/50/100$; R4H ($N$=500) was retired at registration because the single-transit STR pass-count scaling exceeds the registered span at $N\ge 150$ (the retirement rationale is registered; no capacity number is asserted anywhere in this paper, since the serial bound has not been recomputed under the present real-window scenario set).

The mode composition of each family is inherited from the grandfathered assignment of Section 3.5 rather than re-derived at run time: every R1C task is stripmap, R2F routes its tasks between sliding spotlight and spotlight and carries no stripmap task, and R3H mixes all three modes. That composition sets the scale of the quality index a family can reach (Section 3.3), but it is a property of the demand template that generated the scenario rather than of any schedule, and no reading in this paper compares index levels across families. The control family's served fraction saturates at 1.0 from the as-placed point upwards; the saturation is anchor-construction-carried (Section 6.5) and is read descriptively, never as a performance result.

The artifacts are the released artifact set of this scenario generation (measurement conventions v5), and they are not a refresh of the previous generation but an explicit re-judgement. A registered decision made the sliding-spotlight and spotlight parameters follow the requirement per task (constraint C5), so the delivered resolution at the selected start equals the requirement by construction; that changes what is admissible at the data level rather than merely how a reading is labelled, and the earlier artifacts therefore no longer describe the same model. The released set re-runs the sweep over the same frozen scenario population --- the task set, the visibility windows and the mode assignment are carried over unchanged, so the demand axis is the only thing that moves --- at a single registered budget of 40/60 population/generations. The artifact set carries its own lineage --- parent generation, frozen code state, and adjudication --- in its manifests, and every reading in Section 6 is recomputed from those artifacts by the manuscript-number checker before the section is read as a result.

The R-family f$_q$ is *not normalised*: the per-window slant-range geometry expands the units scene-dependently, so index levels are incomparable across families and are never pooled into a single span or compared across families. The synthetic M families remain available as a controlled reference; their one still-valid artifact is annotated, wherever cited, as a version-2-convention controlled reference whose output provenance is unregistered, and no M-family number is mixed with R-family numbers in this paper.

## 5.3 The demand scan and its frozen criteria

**The scan.** The experiment is an external scan rather than a hypothesis test about a control: a scalar $s$ multiplies every task's required resolution (Section 3.5), nine values of $s$ are frozen, $s=1.0$ reproduces the requirement as placed (the as-placed point), and each grid point is read as the median over ten seeds --- ten independent scenario draws, each solved once, with the same seed driving both the scenario draw and the search, so scenario-sampling noise and search noise are not separated by the design --- of the served priority fraction of the best-covered member, at the registered 40/60 budget. The scenario population is not regenerated between points, so within a seed the nine readings are nine views of one scenario set through nine requirements. The search-noise half of that confound is bounded by the construction face: the anchor already attains the best-found coverage in 258 of the 260 sweep units, so the coverage response is carried by the construction rather than by solver stochasticity (Section 4.5).

**Three criteria, frozen before this scenario set's data were read.** Three shape criteria are registered against the curve and are inherited unchanged from their earlier registration rather than tuned to the data. They are labelled S1--S3 here to keep them apart from the constraint families C1--C5 of Section 3.4. S1 asks that the medians be monotone non-decreasing across the grid; S2 is the saturation arm, the median at $s=2.0$ at least the median at $s=1.5$; S3 is the tight arm, the median at $s=0.8$ below the median at $s=1.0$. A cell whose ten seeds all exit without a feasible individual enters the criterion evaluation as a zero-coverage median, and it is the tight arm (S3) and the monotone arm (S1) that read that cell; the saturation arm (S2) compares the two loosest points and does not. The zero-coverage entry is a recorded outcome rather than missing data, and the reporting discipline of Section 6.2 keeps such a point off the coverage curve and never averages it into one. They are the registered content of the preregistration amendment that also vacated the quality-headroom estimand of the earlier line and registered the coverage--demand curve as the main result form (Section 3.4). Their verdicts are reported as measured, failures included, and the criteria are not re-tuned after the fact: a failed criterion is a result about this scenario set, not a defect in the criterion to be edited away. The registration timeline is part of the record and is stated exactly: the nine-point grid and the three criteria were registered before this scenario set's data were read; the amendment that registered the coverage--demand curve as the main result form is dated 2026-10-21, after the sweep ran on 2026-10-20, so the headline curve is a post hoc descriptive reading and the pre-registered content is the grid and the criteria themselves. The refined reading of the criteria verdicts (an infeasible cell is undefined; the tight arm reads pairwise per-seed deltas against a one-priority-unit tolerance) is likewise a post hoc refinement, registered 2026-10-23 after the 2026-10-22 external review; the frozen criteria text is not edited. The one-priority-unit tolerance is one count of the priority numerator, 1/sum_i p_i of the scenario --- the smallest nonzero priority movement --- which is about 0.86 (R1C), 0.38 (R2F) and 0.19 (R3H) points at the registered task counts.

**What the amendment retired.** The earlier design carried an effect size, a power calculation and a power curve, all of them calibrated on the quality headroom of a front over its anchor. That estimand does not exist in this model --- a unit's front is a tie-set on coverage, so there is no quality quantity to be gained --- and the power figure is retired with it rather than reproduced: a power curve about an estimand this paper does not report would be a number in search of a claim. Nothing in Section 6 depends on it.

**One budget, stated.** Every point of the grid is read at the single registered budget of 40/60; no budget ladder is run in this scenario set, and no frontier-richness statement in either direction is made from these artifacts. The wall-clock cost of one unit at that budget is 5--15 s (single-threaded; median 4.9 s for the 20-task control, 10.0 s for the 50-task and 15.3 s for the 100-task family on the registered workstation), so the full 270-unit sweep with the counterfactual arm is a single-afternoon computation.
