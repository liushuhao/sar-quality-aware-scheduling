# 2. Related Work

This section positions the paper along three lines: sliding-spotlight SAR imaging (where the dwell physics lives), agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling (where the fixed-dwell assumption lives), and quality-aware scheduling (where image quality enters planning decisions).

## 2.1 Sliding-spotlight and spotlight SAR imaging

The sliding-spotlight family is standardly formulated as steering about a fixed virtual rotation centre. The concept has its lineage in the wide-swath sliding configurations of Currie and Brown \cite{currie1992wideswath} and the two-step focusing of hybrid stripmap--spotlight acquisitions \cite{lanari2001hybrid}; the TerraSAR-X extended chirp scaling processor is built on that geometry \cite{mittermayer2003sliding}, the baseband azimuth-scaling processor of Prats et al. handles both sliding spotlight and terrain observation by progressive scans (TOPS) \cite{prats2010processing}; in sliding spotlight the rotation centre lies beyond the swath, in TOPS it lies behind the sensor. Kim et al. derive the attitude-maneuver and synthetic-aperture time formulas for squint staring and sliding spotlight on the same geometry \cite{kim2021optimal}. Operational descriptions sometimes state the steering as a constant angular rate --- Zhang et al.'s Gaofen-3 (GF-3) account does so explicitly and describes it as a small-angle simplification of the same geometry \cite{zhang2018sliding} --- and processing work that pushes to high squint is likewise explicit that the linearised steering assumption breaks there \cite{guo2011extended,ren2025high}. This paper adopts the rotation-centre formulation throughout (Section 3.1); its scheduling windows reach squints where only the exact geometry is valid, which is why the two readings must not be mixed.

Two quantitative anchors from the imaging line matter to this study, and both are quoted as their own platforms' literature values. First, Kim et al.'s Table 2 defines a $\pm 12^{\circ}$ task envelope for its reference mission; the design envelopes registered in this paper --- $\pm 45^{\circ}$ for stripmap/spotlight and $\pm 26.9^{\circ}$ (a registered conservative value, a design choice) for sliding spotlight --- are separate fields and are never merged with the literature task domain \cite{kim2021optimal}. Second, Song et al. report for their sparse-imaging platform that the azimuth ambiguity-to-signal ratio is anti-correlated with the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) and degrades as the scan angle grows (by about 8.6 dB beyond $1^{\circ}$ of scan for conventional sliding spotlight, and about 15.2 dB for the sparse variant), alongside a range-ambiguity reduction of about 10.7 dB for the sparse mode \cite{song2025spaceborne}; as Section 7.2 shows, the direction of the ambiguity-versus-squint dependence in our model is the opposite of that literature anchor, and the contrast is a result of this paper rather than a contradiction to be smoothed over. The standard ambiguity and resolution relations otherwise follow the textbook treatment \cite{cumming2005digital}.

The dwell time itself is treated in the imaging literature as a deterministic function of mode and geometry --- Kim et al.'s synthetic-aperture time chain is exactly that \cite{kim2021optimal} --- but it stops at the imaging problem boundary: in the imaging references this paper draws on, the scheduling layer above it is not part of their treatment.

## 2.2 Agile Earth-observation satellite scheduling

The agile-scheduling literature is broad and mature: hybrid genetic-simulated-annealing planners for moving-target observation \cite{qin2024multisatellite}, deep-reinforcement-learning planners for large mission-planning instances \cite{long2024deep,chen2024learning,zhang2025deep}, adaptive large-neighborhood search with learned guidance or with neighbourhood repair \cite{du2025satellite,he2018a}, constraint-programming formulations for super-agile platforms \cite{caleiras2026a}, memetic and multi-chromosome cooperative planners \cite{wang2024memetic,wang2024research}, and profit models in which the observation value depends on the interval between revisits \cite{li2024earth,peng2019agile}. Across the agile-scheduling formulations surveyed here, the dominant structural assumption is that the imaging duration of a task is an exogenous constant of the task, known before the scheduler runs --- the SAR-specific line below is the nearest neighbour, and even there the geometry is fixed upstream of the planner. The scheduler then packs fixed-length intervals into time lines, and whatever the objective --- profit, coverage, latency --- the duration never feeds back.

For an agile SAR platform in sliding-spotlight operation that assumption is false in a specific, quantifiable way: the dwell is produced by the mode physics (the tan-exact closure chain of Section 3.2), it varies per task with the assigned mode and the squint of the selected start, and it enters the inter-task transfer constraint that decides what else the pass can carry. This paper makes the dwell endogenous, carries the required resolution as a per-task constraint on that dwell, and measures what tightening the requirement costs in coverage.

## 2.3 Quality-aware scheduling

Image quality reaches the planning literature in three roles, and the difference between them and this paper is worth stating plainly. Image-quality research on the SAR side carries a substantial assessment literature --- fidelity metrics, quality checkers, and radiometric corrections applied to formed images \cite{cumming2005digital,mittermayer2003sliding,prats2010processing}. Imaging-mode design starts from a resolution requirement and trades it against swath \cite{song2025spaceborne}, and mission-level SAR planning computes the required synthetic-aperture time and steering span from the required resolution and the incidence angle \cite{kim2020optimal}. Multi-objective Earth-observation and SAR mission studies optimise more than one criterion explicitly alongside coverage or priority \cite{chang2021integrated,kim2019multi,kim2020optimal}; that line of work exists, it is not the line this paper takes, and nothing here claims its absence. What changes here is the *placement* of quality. It leaves the objective set: the per-mode index of Section 3.3 states a platform capability and supplies the numerator of a reported fulfillment ratio.

## 2.4 Positioning

A recent SAR-specific line is the closest neighbour and is worth separating explicitly. Liu et al. \cite{liu2025a} and Xin et al. \cite{xin2024sar} plan SAR regional observation with weighted multi-objective and genetic-algorithm planners; Shen et al. \cite{shen2024multistrip} plan multistrip stitching missions under an onboard energy budget; Li et al. \cite{li2026efficient} generate visible windows and screen them for quality before any planning runs. Across these four, the imaging geometry is fixed upstream of the scheduler --- by the request, the strip design, or the generation-side screen --- and none carries a per-task dwell that responds to a per-task resolution requirement inside the scheduling loop. Table 3 positions the neighbouring work on the three axes this paper treats as one chain.

| Work | Duration | Quality | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agile EO scheduling | exogenous constant | outside the planner | fixed by design |
| SAR regional scheduling (liu2025a, xin2024sar) | exogenous input | not a scheduling readout | fixed by design |
| Multistrip planning (shen2024multistrip) | derived from strip design | energy constraint | designed per strip |
| Window screening (li2026efficient) | generation-side pruning | screening filter | fixed per request |
| SAR imaging design (kim2021optimal, song2025spaceborne) | mode + geometry at design time | design criterion | designed, not scheduled |
| This paper | endogenous per task and start | reported reading, never optimised | frozen per task by its requirement |

**Table 3.** Positioning on the three axes the paper treats as one chain: what fixes the imaging duration, where image quality enters, and who decides the imaging mode.

The three lines meet at a seam rather than at a gap, and the seam is narrow. The imaging line supplies the resolution-to-aperture chain and stops at the imaging problem boundary: a required resolution and incidence angle fix the synthetic-aperture time and steering span of a designed observation \cite{kim2020optimal}, and the sliding-spotlight processors deliver such a design at a fixed rotation centre \cite{mittermayer2003sliding,prats2010processing}. The scheduling line supplies the packing machinery for tasks whose duration is an attribute fixed before the schedule is built \cite{chen2024learning,qin2024multisatellite}. The quality-aware line supplies assessment conventions and mission-level weightings \cite{cumming2005digital,kim2019multi}. This paper puts the required resolution into the scheduling constraint set: the mode parameters follow the requirement where the mode has them, the resolution delivered at the selected start equals the requirement, and the coverage cost of tightening the requirement is measured on a frozen grid under a fixed mode assignment (Section 3 and Section 6). Sections 3--6 build that problem on a registered, preregistered footing --- the demand grid, the shape criteria and the convention labels are fixed before this scenario set's data were read --- and report what the tightening costs.
