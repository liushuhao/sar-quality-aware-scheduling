# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Motivation

Agile Earth-observation satellites steered by a single antenna can operate their synthetic aperture radar (SAR) in a continuum of imaging modes: stripmap, sliding spotlight, and spotlight are one physical mode family parameterised by where the beam's virtual rotation centre sits. Which point of that continuum a task receives is not a free stylistic choice --- it is fixed by the requested resolution (the requirement, as this paper calls it from here on), the scene, and the geometry of the pass, and it determines how long the satellite must dwell on the scene; the assignment itself is frozen at generation time in this study's data (Section 3.5). Scheduling therefore cannot treat the imaging duration as an exogenous constant, as the classical agile-scheduling literature largely does \cite{long2024deep,qin2024multisatellite,li2024earth}: in sliding-spotlight operation the dwell is produced by the imaging physics, it changes from task to task, and it feeds back into how many tasks a pass can carry and at what image quality. In that literature the resolution requirement, when it appears at all, is an attribute of the request that is settled before the schedule is built: the observation duration is a task attribute fixed in advance \cite{chen2024learning,qin2024multisatellite}, and on the SAR side the required resolution already fixes the synthetic-aperture time and steering span of a designed observation \cite{kim2020optimal}.

This paper studies that coupling on a single satellite in a regime of single-pass dense task clusters under true orbit geometry. Scheduling decisions select which tasks to serve and when to start imaging within each task's visibility windows; the mode and its parameters follow from the product requirement, and the dwell, the maneuver, and the image quality follow from the mode and the window. The density ladder is fixed at registration (Section 5.2).

Two discipline risks shape the methodology as much as the physics does. First, a measured effect on coverage can be a property of the *search* rather than of the *scenario*: in this scenario set the greedy anchor already attains the best coverage found in this study in 258 of the 260 sweep units, so the coverage response reported here is produced by the mode and parameter adaptation rather than by the optimiser's ability to find it. Second, quality statements are only as good as their measurement conventions: several formally plausible formulations (an ambiguity sum that includes the principal image, a range resolution that varies per task, a squint-dependent routing resolution) are registered as not used, and the paper says so where it matters.

## 1.2 Contributions

The paper's contributions are three: the platform that carries the requirement into the physics, the family-shape contrast in what a requirement costs in coverage, and the discipline that keeps both claims testable:

1. **An enabling physics-to-scheduler platform** (the platform leg). A three-mode continuum formalisation (Section 3) that carries the scene-level dwell closures and the maneuver-coupled transfer feasibility into one scheduling loop: the imaging mode is resolved from the product requirement, the mode parameters follow the requirement where the mode has them --- so that the delivered resolution equals the requirement at the selected start by construction, an identity the search cannot violate rather than a constraint it could --- and the requirement excludes solutions in exactly one place, the stripmap antenna floor, where a finer order has no admissible mode. The dwell duration is a consequence of the requirement rather than a constant --- under a frozen mode assignment (Section 3.5).

2. **The mode-dependence of what a requirement costs** (main result). Three scenario families swept over a frozen nine-point demand grid at a single search budget and under a frozen mode assignment: a floor step on the stripmap family, a non-monotone response on the 50-task family whose two dips are traced to the greedy anchor construction, tightening costs of 26.0--46.6 percentage points on the dense family against a 0.27-point tight-side plateau on the 50-task family, as-placed costs of 4.15 and 2.36 percentage points against each family's own saturation, and a fixed-dwell counterfactual that quantifies what the endogenous dwell buys (Section 6).

3. **A discipline that keeps the result falsifiable** (methodology). Shape criteria (S1--S3) registered before this scenario set's data were read --- the grid and the criteria are the pre-registered content, while the main-result form and the verdict-reading refinements were registered afterwards and are dated as such (Section 5.3) --- and reported with their failures rather than re-tuned to the data; measurement-convention labels that keep the two quality conventions apart and keep the fulfillment ratio a reading; and an explicit separation of the search face from the construction face --- the anchor attains the best-found coverage in 258 of 260 units, so the coverage response is a construction result, not a search finding (Sections 5 and 6).

Throughout, the trade-off the study claims is the resolution-versus-dwell-time axis of the mode continuum; the antenna-design question of resolution against swath and the serial-bound capacity reading are registered scope statements rather than results.

The move this paper makes is to take the resolution off the request and put it into the constraint set: the requirement is what the scheduler must satisfy task by task, the mode parameters are what carry it into the physics, and the coverage loss is what the paper measures. The claim is scoped to the literature surveyed below: the survey rests on the project's curated literature pool --- a converted and catalogued bibliography of the field plus an incremental paper-search sweep over the months preceding writing, whose nearest neighbours Section 2 engages directly. what the tested coupling in fact produced in this scenario set is reported in Section 6, where every reading is registered and reproducible.
