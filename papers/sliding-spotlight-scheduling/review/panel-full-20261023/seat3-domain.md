## 2. Manuscript summary (≤150 words)

The manuscript makes the imaging dwell endogenous to agile-satellite SAR scheduling: on a Kim-2021 X-band reference platform (h = 570 km, λ = 0.031 m, θa = 0.36°, 5 km scenes), stripmap/sliding-spotlight/spotlight form a rotation-centre continuum (k, A = 1−k); the required azimuth resolution is a per-task hard constraint whose task-level parameters (A_task, β_task) set the dwell through tan-exact Kim-derived closures. Three scenario families sweep a nine-point requirement grid under a frozen mode assignment; the result is that tightening costs 26–47 coverage points on the stripmap-floor families and almost nothing on the SLS/SPT family, and that fixed-dwell planning violates the transfer constraint in 20–29% of consecutive pairs. An inverse-pixel-area quality index and an AASR admission gate are reported readings, not objectives. Disclosure discipline is unusually extensive.

## 3. Assessment

### 3.1 Geometry and dwell physics — verifiable and almost exactly reproducible; two real defects in convention and error accounting

I recomputed the full §3.1 constant chain independently (spherical-Earth look geometry, circular-orbit inertial speed, D = λ/θa, A = 2ρ/D, r0 = A·R0/(1−A)): every printed value matches — R0 = 775.43 km, v_s = 7578.06 m/s, A = 0.202683, r0 = 197,119 m, r0/R0 = 0.254207, tier ρa = 2.4669/0.5000/0.3000 m — including the θa/A = λ/(2ρ) identity, exact to displayed precision. The worry "A = 0.202683 → 0.5007?" does not materialise: A·D/2 = 0.50001 m; the constants are mutually consistent because A is derived from the 0.5 m design point, and the symbol discipline (r0 vs R0) is correct and well enforced, including in Fig. 1.

The inertial-velocity convention ("inertial scene velocity $v_s=7578.05$~m/s", §3.1) is defensible but only with the evidence the paper omits. Kim defines V_s as the ECEF relative velocity (formula registry §1, Kim p.7 assumption 3); the registry's own reproduction table shows Kim's Table-3 SAT values match at 0.2–0.5% under the inertial figure and 4.7% under ECEF-relative (7221.69 m/s) — i.e., Kim's own numbers are near-inertial despite the stated definition. The paper freezes inertial with an honest scope statement (§3.1) but then contradicts itself in §7.3 (issue D1 below).

The dwell layer is sound at its core: the production SLS closure is the scene-level Eq.(17)/(19)-derived sweep span evaluated tan-exactly at squint, with the 1% window-tail pad and the planar-oracle deviations (+0.4185% @12°, +2.1% @26.9°, +5.3% @45°) all reproducing exactly in my recomputation (+0.419 / +2.102 / +5.305%). The SLS cap 26.9° as "registered conservative value (design choice)" — "not a physical boundary" — is the correct final semantics (the retired 26.7567° boundary was a linearisation artifact), and §2's warning against mixing constant-rate steering readings with the exact geometry at high squint is domain-correct. The two genuine defects: (i) the production chain's own squint error is explicitly unquantified even though the exact Eq.(20)–(23) chain is implemented in the fork and used only as an internal identity check (D2); (ii) the Earth-curvature-free planar closure at the ±45° STR/SPT design edge is physically weak — a 45° squint projects the beam-edge geometry 775 km down-range, where flat-Earth and spherical geometry diverge well beyond any 1% pad — acceptable for a scheduling paper at the SLS cap, not at 45° without a number (D9).

### 3.2 Quality and ambiguity model — internally consistent and numerically exact, but one derivation and one label are physically wrong

I reproduced the entire AASR machinery from the registered recipe (two-way sinc⁴ integrand, m ≠ 0, |m| ≤ 6): stripmap −21.35 dB at 0° squint and −23.32 dB at 26° (the paper's exact values); the SLS sequence degenerate with STR point by point (my values coincide to 0.01 dB; A·B_D = 2v cosψ/D exactly); spotlight −7.05 dB squint-invariant (degenerate collapsed integral — I verify both the collapse and the value); f_dc(45°) = 345.7 kHz = 95.2×PRF; B_D = 15.2/25.3 kHz. The m ≠ 0 exclusion is the correct Cumming–Wong convention and the paper's reason for it is right. The single production PRF = 3630 Hz satisfies the azimuth sampling constraint 2vθa/λ = 3072 Hz with 18% margin — physically sane for a scheduling world, honestly declared as a constant rather than a selection. The §7.2 direction contrast (model AASR improves with squint; Song's platform degrades ~8.6 dB beyond 1° scan) is flagged, not smoothed, and the stated mechanism — squink-narrowed processed band under a squint-constant compression factor — is a faithful consequence of the registered semantics (Song's G(θ)→Doppler mapping carries no cosψ pattern scaling, so band narrowing is the only channel). What is wrong: the (R0/R)³ normalisation rests on a false premise and g_T is misnamed (D3) — the section quotes magnitudes (24.3×, 67.6×) whose physical meaning depends on fixing this.

### 3.3 Literature engagement — fair, verified against full texts; one lineage gap

I verified the domain characterisations against the archived full texts: Kim Table 2 does register ±12° squint, 0.36° beamwidth, 5×5 km scenes, 2 s settle; Song's anchors (8.6 dB conventional SLS AASR degradation, 15.2 dB sparse, 10.7 dB RASR reduction) are quoted accurately; Zhang's GF-3 account does steer "at a constant rotation rate". All 31 cited keys resolve in refs.bib and the SAR entries match their claimed content (Kim Aerospace 2021; Song Sensors 2025; Zhang Sensors 2018; Mittermayer IGARSS 2003; Prats TGRS 2010; Cumming & Wong 2005; Guo PIER 2011; Ren RS 2025). The §2 statement that Song's A = 1 − R0kθ/v_g is "a restatement within the same rotation-centre model — not an independent literature identity" is correct (Song's R_rot = R0 + r0). The one gap a domain referee will note: the sliding-spotlight concept lineage (Currie & Brown 1992; Lanari et al. 2001 hybrid stripmap/spotlight) is absent (D8).

## 4. Issues

### CRITICAL

None. No issue found invalidates the headline scheduling results; all defects are in the physics-layer derivation, labelling, or error accounting.

### MAJOR

**[D1] §7.3 ("the coverage readings compare schedules inside one convention, so they are invariant to it") vs §3.1 ("every reading that mixes dwells with velocity-independent constants (the coverage readings) ... carries the convention") — direct self-contradiction, and §7.3 is the false side.** Problem: the C2 chain charges (1+0.25)·T_dwell + t_man + t_settle with t_man = 4.07 s and t_settle = 2.0 s unscaled by any velocity; a global v_s change (inertial → ECEF-relative 7221.69 m/s lengthens every dwell ~4.9%) therefore changes which schedules satisfy C2, hence changes coverage. Why it matters: §7.3 is the limitation-section argument that dismisses the convention question for the main result; a reader who believes it will under-state the convention dependence of every headline number (the 20–29% counterfactual violation rate included, since both arms of that comparison scale differently against the unscaled maneuver/settle terms). Fix: delete or invert the §7.3 sentence to match §3.1; add the registry's actual justification for choosing inertial (Kim Table-3 reproduction at 0.2–0.5% inertial vs 4.7% ECEF — currently only in formula-spec §6 Table 3, not in the paper); state a one-line sensitivity (dwell +4.9% under ECEF-relative tightens C2; the 25% pad absorbs it directionally but coverage levels can move).

**[D2] §3.2 dwell: "the Kim Eq. (22)/(24) chain is a zero-Doppler-clamped (broadside) attitude construction; applying it at nonzero squint is a registered approximation" and "not separately quantified here" — the production closure's squint error is unquantified although the exact chain is implemented.** Problem: the production SLS dwell (verified in the fork, duration.py: kim_checker_scene_sls) is the broadside scene-sweep span (Eq.(18): Δθ = (R0θa + Wa)/r0, α = Δθ + θa) placed symmetrically about ψ̄ in a tan difference — not the exact squinted chain Eq.(20)–(23) with the sine-law end squint; kim_eq23_theta_s1 implements that chain but only checks an internal identity. The quoted deviations (+0.4185%…+5.3%) quantify the planar oracle against production, not production against the exact chain — and the paper says so — but "tan-exact Kim Eq. (22)/(24) chain" will make domain readers assume the exact squint chain runs. Additionally W_a is squint-unscaled in production while the oracle sec²-scales it, so the +5.3% @45° conflates linearisation with a structural difference in the scene term. Why it matters: the dwell feeds C2, which produces the central counterfactual (20–29% violating pairs); at the ±45° envelope edge an unquantified few-percent dwell error is the same order as the effects reported. Fix: run the already-implemented Eq.(20)–(23) chain over each mode envelope and report max symmetric-vs-exact deviation; state the W_a-at-squint semantics; reword to "Eq.(17)/(19)-span symmetric construction".

**[D3] §3.3: "two-way spreading falls as $R^{-4}$ while the resolution cell grows linearly with range" — false premise in the model's own terms; and "$g_T$ the transmit-path antenna gain term" is a misnomer.** Problem: in the frozen model ρ_rg is held at 0.5 m and ρ_a is range-independent (both stated two sentences later), so the resolution cell does not grow with range; the classical R³ scaling of NESZ comes from the integration time T ∝ R/ρ_a at fixed resolution, not from cell growth. And g_T is (verified in the fork, quality.py: dwell_gain) the point-target dwell ratio T_point(m)/T_point(STR) = 1/A for SLS — an integration-time/energy ratio containing no antenna gain. Consequently the index double-weights azimuth resolution (g_T ∝ 1/ρ_a on top of the explicit 1/ρ_a); the fork's own registered note (D05-5, quality.py docstring: "Papers must state BOTH") requires disclosing this, and the paper states dominance but not the double weighting. Under the physical chain T·ρ_a is mode-independent, so the quoted capability magnitudes (24.3×, 67.6×, §3.3) are properties of the chosen index, not radiometric statements. Why it matters: §3.3 presents these constants as platform capability; a SAR referee reads "SLS is 24× the quality of STR" as an NESZ-shaped claim, which it is not. Fix: rename g_T (dwell-gain / integration-gain ratio), re-derive R³ via T ∝ R/ρ_a, and state the deliberate ρ_a² weighting as registered design semantics.

### MINOR

**[D4] Frozen-registry residual vs paper — formula-spec §3.1 still reads "SLS 物理滑动域 ~26°" (physical sliding domain) while v1.7 (§8.14) abolished the 26.7567° boundary as a linearisation artifact and the paper correctly says "not a physical boundary".** Problem: the registry the paper points to for freezing carries a stale phrase contradicting the newest adjudication the paper follows; also the cap 26.9° sits 0.14° above the retired boundary, a numeric coincidence that invites misreading. Why it matters: the paper's provenance chain ("frozen formula registry") is a headline discipline claim; one stale line undermines it for exactly the most contested constant. Fix: one dated erratum line in the registry; one sentence in the paper noting the cap/boundary proximity is coincidental.

**[D5] §3.1: "STR and SPT carry a design domain of $\pm 45^{\circ}$" — the reachable SPT start domain is requirement-dependent and narrower.** Problem: the fork (duration.py: task_spt_span, §8.17-11) enforces |ψ_sel| + β_task/2 ≤ 45°, so a 0.3 m order caps starts at ~42.9°. Why it matters: the envelope statement is quoted in Fig. 1 and §3.1 as a mode property; readers will take ±45° as reachable for SPT at all requirements. Fix: state the formation-point clip in §3.1 or §3.2.

**[D6] §7.1 caveat: "the omitted term is a two-way attenuation applied to the ambiguous returns and not to the beam-centre principal image, including it could only lower the modelled ratio" — wrong direction-of-bias argument for the azimuth integral.** Problem: azimuth aliases return from the same slant range and elevation angle as the desired signal, so a two-way elevation pattern cancels to first order between numerator and denominator of the azimuth-only AASR; the "could only lower" argument is valid for range-type far ambiguities, not for what is integrated here. Why it matters: the caveat claims a conservative direction the model does not possess (the true effect is near-cancellation, which is benign but for a different reason). Fix: replace with the cancellation argument (and keep the honest "magnitude not quantified").

**[D7] §7.2: "a degrading compression factor competing with bandwidth narrowing --- is not modeled here" — the attribution can be sharpened at no cost.** Problem: Song's AASR also grows with the target-ensemble scan span (scene Doppler occupancy grows with scan angle), a channel absent from this model's per-target processed-band integral; saying only "different steering semantics" leaves the contrast less falsifiable than it could be. Why it matters: the direction contrast is presented as a result of the paper; naming the missing channel makes it a checkable statement rather than a caveat. Fix: one sentence identifying the scene-scan-span channel as the candidate reconciliation, or a small sensitivity arm if the authors wish to close it.

**[D8] §2.1 lineage gap: no citation of Currie & Brown (1992, wide-swath SAR — the origin of the sliding-spotlight rotation-centre concept) or Lanari et al. (2001, hybrid stripmap/spotlight two-step focusing).** Problem: the section's history starts at Mittermayer 2003. Why it matters: a domain referee expects the concept's origin in a paper whose entire mode formalism rests on it. Fix: add both to the §2.1 first paragraph.

**[D9] §3.1: "$45^{\circ}$ dwell readings of STR/SPT are extrapolated" — acceptable at the SLS cap, not at 45° without a number.** Problem: the planar closure omits Earth curvature while R0 itself is full-curvature; at 45° squint the beam-edge geometry projects ~775 km down-range where flat and spherical geometries diverge by far more than the 1% window-tail pad, and the C3/C4 envelope reference T_env,max = 10.5830 s is taken at that edge. Why it matters: registered constants inherit an unquantified extrapolation. Fix: one full-curvature checker value at 45° (or an explicit list of which registered constants inherit the extrapolation) — for a scheduling paper the omission is acceptable, the silence is not.

## 6. Questions to authors

1. Which statement do you stand behind — §3.1 ("coverage readings ... carry the convention") or §7.3 ("invariant to it")? Please show the C2 arithmetic under ECEF-relative v_s = 7221.69 m/s.
2. What is the maximum deviation of the production symmetric-span SLS dwell from your own implemented Eq.(20)–(23) exact chain over 0–26.9°, and over 0–45° for STR/SPT?
3. Given g_T = T_point(m)/T_point(STR) = 1/A exactly, why label it "transmit-path antenna gain"? Would you accept "integration-gain (dwell) ratio" plus the registered ρ_a² double-weighting statement?
4. Does the scene-scan Doppler span (Song's degradation channel) or the compression-factor channel dominate the §7.2 contrast if you switch the integral to the scene-ensemble band? Even a scaling argument would sharpen the "different semantics" claim.
5. T_env,max = 10.5830 s is quoted at the envelope edge attained by SPT — at which squint and requirement exactly, and does it inherit the 45° planar extrapolation?

## 7. Recommendation: Major Revision

**Confidence**: 4/5 (high on the SAR physics layer, which I recomputed independently; the scheduling-layer verdicts belong to other seats).

**Rationale**: The physics layer is unusually reproducible and honestly disclosed — every headline constant, the AASR sequence, and the literature anchors verified to the digit, no critical defect. But three major issues must be fixed before the domain layer can be certified: a self-contradiction on whether the main coverage result is invariant to the velocity convention (D1, the limitations section asserting the false side); an unquantified squint error in the production dwell closure that feeds the central counterfactual, despite the exact chain already being implemented (D2); and a false derivation plus a mislabel in the quality model that let index magnitudes masquerade as radiometric capability (D3).
