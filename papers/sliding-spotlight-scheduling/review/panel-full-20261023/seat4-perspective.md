# Review Report — Seat 4: Perspective Reviewer (Operations × Scheduling Theory)

## 1. Persona card (first person)

I spent eight years on constellation ground-segment operations (mission planning for an imaging constellation, interface between the planning toolchain and the payload/imaging groups) before moving to academia; I now work on AEOS scheduling benchmarks and time-dependent scheduling. I read this paper asking two questions the other seats will not ask: (a) does the scheduling-theory framing in §4 hold up as a formulation statement, and (b) would a working mission planner or a ground-segment tool owner learn anything they would act on. I deliberately do not re-adjudicate the SAR physics (ambiguity models, dwell closures) or the statistics of the criteria verdicts.

## 2. Manuscript summary (≤150 words)

The paper makes the imaging dwell of an agile SAR satellite endogenous to scheduling: the required azimuth resolution is a per-task hard constraint, mode parameters follow the requirement, and the dwell follows from mode, squint, and selected start. On one synthetic scenario template, three families (20/50/100 tasks, differing mode mixes) are swept over a frozen nine-point requirement-scaling grid under a frozen ("grandfathered") mode assignment, at one search budget, maximizing served priority fraction. Headline readings: the dense family loses 26.0 percentage points from as-placed to one step tighter (46.6 to the tightest feasible), the all-stripmap control becomes infeasible at s=0.8, the no-stripmap family is flat within 0.27 points; a fixed-dwell counterfactual shows 20–29% transfer violations after replay. The greedy anchor attains the unit maximum in 258/260 units, so the response is attributed to construction, not search.

## 3. Assessment (theory framing / practical value / generalizability & scoping)

**Scheduling-theory framing (§4): correct skeleton, imprecise joints, decorative citations.** The single-machine, time-window, setup-time skeleton is right and standard for AEOS. Two joints are mislabeled. First, the setup is called "a sequence-dependent setup in the classical sense" (§4), but the maneuver time depends on the start squints of both endpoints, i.e., on the chosen start times — it is start-time-dependent setup, strictly harder than sequence-dependent, and this is precisely the "time-dependent transition times" formulation of Chen et al., which the paper itself cites in §2 but never connects to §4. Second, the dwell is called "a position- and time-dependent processing time" (§4); in scheduling terminology "position-dependent" means rank-in-sequence dependence (the Gawiejnowicz program's other half), which is not what happens here. What happens is a per-window nonmonotone (approximately U-shaped across a window, since squint is zero at closest approach) start-time-dependent processing time with window-choice discontinuities — and the cited Gawiejnowicz results (linear deterioration equivalences; lp-norm structural properties) are for monotone linear forms, so no structural result cited actually transfers. Given the paper's explicit disclaimer — "No complexity claim is made in this paper" (§4) — a theorist will accept the framing as honest but will note it is one introductory paragraph never used again: no notation mapping, no Graham-triplet-style statement, no observation that the fixed-dwell selection core already embeds orienteering/knapsack (a one-line NP-hardness remark would cost nothing and would anchor why an evolutionary instrument is legitimate). The Habet et al. bounding pointer is well chosen. Net: acceptable, but currently decorative rather than load-bearing; a half-page rewrite makes it load-bearing.

**Practical/operational value: one genuinely actionable result, one useful decomposition, embedded in a synthetic world that is honestly labeled.** The fixed-dwell counterfactual (§6.6) is the most operationally valuable object in the paper: "The transfer constraint is violated in 20–29% of consecutive pairs" (§6.6) after planning with zero-squint library dwells, with an 18–30-point post-repair coverage shortfall. That is exactly the failure mode a ground-segment planner wants quantified before trusting a fixed-duration planning proxy in dense single-pass clusters; I would cite this in a tool-requirements review. The floor-exclusion bound decomposition (Table 2; "the floor accounts for 20.01 of the 26.01-point drop" §6.1) is the second useful object: it tells a product/tier manager that most of the tightening cost on the mixed family is the discrete stripmap floor, not a smooth dwell physics trade. Against this, the operational distance is real: two-body propagation with no J2, "no dynamic task arrival, no downlink or ground-station modeling, and no replanning loop" (§7.1), no wall-clock runtime, and priorities that are "uniform random integers with no physical provenance" (§5.1). All disclosed; still, the absolute coverage numbers are readings of one registered synthetic world, and the paper never lets the reader forget it.

**Generalizability & scoping: honest protection that occasionally protects the headline into ambiguity.** The five scope statements (§7.1) are, in my judgement, necessary and mostly well-drawn; the self-limitation is not excessive — the paper survives it because the counterfactual and the floor decomposition are within-family. The one place scoping does not save the claim is the title/abstract framing "Mode Composition Decides What Tighter Requirements Cost": the paper itself concedes "the three families covary in mode composition as they do in task count" (§6.1), so the family-to-family shape narrative (step/plateau/fall) cannot attribute the difference to composition rather than density. The within-family evidence (R3H floor bound; R2F flatness; R1C step) carries the qualitative claim, but the compositional reading across families is confounded by design. What survives for a cross-disciplinary reader: (i) fixed-duration planning proxies break measurably where transfer chains are tight; (ii) cost of a requirement change is tier-structured (cliff at the library floor, near-flat above it); (iii) a disclosure discipline that is unusually strong. The post-hoc admissions — "the headline curve is a post hoc descriptive reading" (§5.3) — are handled with a candor I have rarely seen; they lower the evidential temperature of the headline but raise my trust in everything else.

## 4. Issues

### CRITICAL

None. The paper is internally consistent, its claims are repeatedly scoped to what was measured, and I found no place where an operations- or theory-facing claim is made that the disclosed design cannot support at the stated caliber.

### MAJOR

**[P1] Title/abstract composition claim vs confounded family design.** Location: title; abstract ("Which mode a task receives decides what a requirement change costs"); §6.1 ("the three families covary in mode composition as they do in task count"). Problem: the only cross-family contrast that would isolate mode composition (same task count, different mixes) is absent; R1C/R2F/R3H differ in both axes simultaneously. Why it matters: the title-level claim is the paper's most quotable sentence, and as stated it is supported only by within-family readings plus a confounded between-family narrative. Suggested fix: either (a) add one composition-matched family (e.g., 100 tasks, no-STR mix) — the generator template appears to make this cheap; or (b) reword title/abstract to the within-family form ("the stripmap floor, not dwell growth, carries most of the tightening cost; mode mix sets where the cliff sits") and state the confound at first mention, not only mid-§6.1.

**[P2] §4 theory paragraph: mislabeled setup class, colliding "position-dependent" term, non-transferring citations.** Location: §4 ("a sequence-dependent setup in the classical sense"; "a position- and time-dependent processing time"). Problem: the setup is start-time-dependent (both endpoints' squints move with start times); "position-dependent" denotes rank-based dependence in the cited literature; the cited Gawiejnowicz results concern monotone linear processing times, while the dwell here is nonmonotone per window. Why it matters: this paragraph is the paper's handshake with my community, and as written a scheduling theorist would mark two terminology errors in the first five lines; it also misses the AEOS-native formulation (time-dependent transition times, cf. Chen et al. 2024 already in the reference list) and a free NP-hardness remark. Suggested fix: rewrite the paragraph as: single-machine selection-and-scheduling with time windows, start-time-dependent setup times, start-time-dependent nonmonotone processing times; one sentence that the fixed-duration core embeds orienteering (hence NP-hard), so no complexity proof is attempted; replace or supplement the Gawiejnowicz cites with the AEOS time-dependent-transition line; keep the Habet bounding pointer.

**[P3] Headline magnitudes ride on a crude transfer-time composition whose sensitivity is stated, not swept.** Location: §7.3 ("every inter-task transition carries the measured 4.07 s bound instead of the model value"; "halving the pad moves the median requirement by roughly 5%"). Problem: C2 is one of the two binding constraints, and its maneuver component is a constant worst-measured bound (the analytic model "under-predicts" its own calibration anchor, per §7.3), plus a 25% dwell pad; the paper's only sensitivity statement is an arithmetic note. Why it matters: the 26.0/46.6-point headline costs inherit this conservatism in absolute level (comparative across grid points is more robust, being common-mode); an operator cannot price how much of the cost is physics versus margin policy. Suggested fix: a two-point replay sensitivity (pad 12.5%/25%; maneuver 2.4 s median/4.07 s) on at least the R3H curve — this is a reanalysis of existing artifacts, the same instrument the paper already used for the fixed-dwell counterfactual, so it is within reach without new experiments.

**[P4] The sole objective weights arbitrary bookkeeping numbers.** Location: §3.4 ("The problem maximises the served priority fraction"); §5.1 ("uniform random integers with no physical provenance"). Problem: every headline number is a fraction of randint(1,10) mass served; no served-task-fraction control reading is reported, and no priority-distribution sensitivity exists. Why it matters: a planner cannot translate "26 points of priority fraction" into tasks lost without the unweighted control; and if high priorities ever correlate with tier (in operations they do — premium products), the cliff shape could move. Suggested fix (cheap): report served-task fraction alongside served-priority fraction at every grid point (the artifacts already contain the selections), and add one correlated-priority robustness seed set or state explicitly that weighting is inert by construction within this world.

### MINOR

**[P5] Verbatim duplicated passages.** Location: §3.4 ("Across the five constraint families…" block repeated twice) and §5.1 ("Across the 30 released R-family manifests…" sentence repeated twice). Problem: copy-editing residue; grep confirms two occurrences of each. Why it matters: signals rushed assembly in the two sections a reviewer reads most closely. Fix: delete the duplicates.

**[P6] Solver apparatus vs the measured no-op.** Location: §4.5 ("the greedy anchor already attains the best coverage found… in 258" units). Problem: four subsections of evolutionary machinery (SBX, polynomial mutation, NSGA-II tie-sets) measurably contribute in 2/260 units; the paper says so, but §4 still reads like a solver contribution. Fix: state the anchor-carries-the-response finding in §4's opening paragraph and demote operator detail, or add the anchor-only curve as a reference line so readers can see the EA's measured contribution.

**[P7] No wall-clock figure.** Location: §7.1 ("no wall-clock runtime figure is registered for the promoted runs"). Problem: for a 40–60 budget on ≤100 tasks this is minutes-scale and harmless, but "how long does planning take" is the first question an operations audience asks; the omission is disclosed, not justified. Fix: report wall-clock per unit for the registered budget in one table line.

**[P8] Missed connections already half-present in the bibliography.** Location: refs.bib contains yang2021onboard, araguz2018applying, malladi2017satellite (onboard autonomy, distributed autonomy, constellation case study) — none cited in the text; §3.4 ("a system design input with product requirement semantics"). Problem/opportunity: the counterfactual's lesson (fixed-duration plans do not survive replay) is most acute onboard, where replanning under real dwells is the operational cure; and the requirement-tightening question is most alive at constellation/tier level, where resolution tiers are priced products. Fix: two or three sentences in §7 connecting the fixed-dwell finding to onboard replanning, and the tier-cliff finding to product-line design; no new experiments needed.

**[P9] Uniform scaling of all requirements is not the operator's intervention.** Location: §3.5 ("a scalar s multiplies every task's required resolution"). Problem: real spec changes move tiers, not the whole demand book simultaneously; R1C's s=0.8 infeasibility is pure arithmetic of the coarse tier crossing the floor (§6.3). Fix: acknowledge per-tier tightening as the operationally meaningful axis in §7.1, ideally committing it to future work explicitly.

## 5. Strengths (evidence-based)

1. **The fixed-dwell counterfactual is the right experiment, done with the right controls** — same solver, budget, population, then replay against real dwells (§6.6); the 20–29% violation and 18–30-point shortfall numbers are the paper's most exportable findings.
2. **Attribution discipline between search and construction** — "attains the best coverage found for the unit in this study in 258" (§4.5) with the two exceptions quantified (+0.0427, +0.0463); this level of solver honesty is rare in the AEOS metaheuristic literature and should be praised publicly.
3. **The floor-exclusion upper bound (Table 2)** is a clean, assumption-light attribution device that converts the headline 26-point cost into "20.01 floor + residual" — exactly how a practitioner would want the number decomposed.
4. **Infeasibility-vs-zero discipline** (§6.2: "an infeasibility record, not a measured coverage of zero") prevents the most common misuse of such grids.
5. **Disclosure cadence**: post-hoc amendment dates stated exactly (§5.3), priorities declared bookkeeping (§5.1), never-binding budgets declared by construction (§3.4); the data-availability statement includes artifacts and a recomputation checker.

## 6. Questions to authors

1. Could you run one composition-matched family (same N as R3H, no-STR mix) to de-confound the title claim, or do you consider the within-family floor decomposition sufficient and would reword the abstract accordingly?
2. What fraction of the R3H tight-side drop survives if the replay uses the median measured maneuver (2.409 s) instead of the 4.07 s bound — is the P3 sensitivity reanalysis feasible from existing artifacts?
3. Can you report served-task fraction next to served-priority fraction, and did you check that weighting is inert (uniform priorities) rather than merely unexamined?
4. In scheduling-theory terms, do you accept describing the setup as start-time-dependent rather than sequence-dependent, and would you add the one-line NP-hardness remark (orienteering containment)?
5. The four unused bibliography entries on onboard autonomy and constellation scheduling suggest you considered that framing — is the onboard-replanning connection (where the fixed-dwell failure mode bites hardest) worth a sentence in §7?

## 7. Recommendation: Accept | Minor Revision | Major Revision | Reject — confidence — rationale

**Major Revision** — confidence: high on my two axes (scheduling-theory framing; operational relevance), medium on the composite, since seats owning the SAR physics and the statistical apparatus may find issues I did not audit. Rationale: the paper's core objects (endogenous-dwell platform, fixed-dwell counterfactual, floor decomposition) are sound, honestly calibrated, and of real interest to my community, and nothing I found is fatal. But four majors stand between this and publication for my readership: a title-level claim resting on a confounded design (P1), a theory-framing paragraph with two terminology errors and non-transferring citations (P2), headline magnitudes whose dependence on crude transfer-margin policy is asserted arithmetically but never exercised (P3), and a sole objective whose weighting a practitioner cannot translate (P4). All four are addressable by rewording plus reanalyses of existing artifacts; I saw no evidence any requires new data collection. With those fixed I would move to Minor Revision.
