## Strongest counter-argument

The strongest coherent case against this paper is not that its numbers are wrong — the receipts largely agree with the printed numbers, and the manuscript is unusually candid about what its design cannot support. The case is that the paper's central result is a **property of its own constraint model rather than of SAR mission planning**, and that the manuscript knows this and says so only in subordinate clauses.

The headline is a degradation result: under a fixed-dwell assumption, the dense families violate the C2 transfer constraint in a median 27–42% of adjacent pairs, and after repair replayed plans land 7–30 points below the endogenous-dwell schedule. That is an honest finding, and the paper correctly attributes it to the mechanism: "the fixed-dwell assumption fails exactly where the C2 chain is tight" (line 404). But "the C2 chain is tight" is a statement about a chain the paper itself constructed — a 25% pad on dwell, a 2.0 s settle, and a maneuver model that the paper's own receipts show is below its own bound in 84% of pairs (1,325 of 1,577), with a median of 2.409 s against a bound the paper anchors at 1.84 s. A reviewer can therefore read the main result as: *a deliberately tight transfer chain makes dwell assumption matter*. The paper offers no controlled variation of chain tightness — no arm in which the C2 chain is loosened — so the claim that the effect is a general consequence of endogenous dwell is licensed by the mechanism story, not by an experiment.

Second, the experimental design forecloses the comparison a reviewer would most want. The paper states plainly that "Densities and mode composition covary by design across the three families, so nothing in this figure is a controlled comparison" (fig. 4 caption), and the abstract concedes "The cost's shape covaries with mode composition and density, which vary together by design." Those two concessions are correct and commendably explicit, but they mean the three-family contrast is a demonstration, not an inference: no family isolates density from mode composition, and the paper's own family-ordering flip across arms (fig. 8 caption: "neither panel supports a between-family ranking") shows the demonstration is not even ordinally stable.

Third, two of the three frozen shape criteria fail on the 50-task family, and the tight-arm criterion is undefined for the control under the refined reading. A pre-registration that fails two of three criteria is a negative result about the pre-registered hypothesis, and the paper's response is to re-register a refined reading (2026-10-23) and re-narrate. That is legitimate if and only if the refined reading was fixed before the data were seen — which an outside reviewer cannot check from the manuscript.

Fourth, the two repair rules that most soften the counterfactual (priority-aware and re-timing) are dated 2026-10-24 and labelled "reanalysis additions of this revision, not preregistered arms" (line 404). The paper says so. But a reader comparing the abstract's "7–30 points below" with the pre-registered arm alone gets a narrower, less favourable number.

Finally, the method section misstates its own dwell closure (Finding F1), so a reader who tries to reproduce the SLS path from the text alone will not land on the code. That is the one place where the paper is *less* careful than its own evidence base.

This is a real contribution wrapped in a design that cannot fully support its framing. The right disposition is major revision: keep the demonstration, cut or subordinate the general claim, and fix the formula.

## Rejected hypotheses

- **H: the abstract's "per-seed 17–50%" violation range is unsupported.** Disproved: `counterfactual_summary.json` gives `violation_rate_pure_min/max` of 0.1739/0.3441 (R3H) and 0.383/0.5 (R2F), i.e. 17–50% exactly as printed.
- **H: the pair denominators are inconsistent (926/458 vs 324/354).** Disproved: the first pair are the pure-plan arm (`adjacent_pairs_pure_total` 926 and 458), the second the conservative pre-shrunk arm (354 and 324); the manuscript reports both arms and both counts, and the conservative arm is sparser by construction.
- **H: the family ordering flip is an unexplained anomaly.** Disproved: the abstract states the covariate structure ("The cost's shape covaries with mode composition and density, which vary together by design") and the fig. 8 caption states that "neither panel supports a between-family ranking". The paper does not claim an ordering.
- **H: the SPT AASR figure (−7.05 dB, "squint-invariant") contradicts the −13 dB per-task admission.** Disproved: line 433 carries an explicit reconciliation — −7.05 dB is the library-edge constant at 5 km platform-level scene extent, while admission is a per-task 11-point in-window check at −13 dB; the two are different objects, and the paper says so.
- **H: the abstract conceals the two failed pre-registered criteria.** Disproved: lines 338–342 disclose both failures in the body ("Two of the three frozen criteria fail on the 50-task family --- the monotonicity criterion and the tight-arm criterion"), with the refined reading and its registration date. The abstract's statements are all factually accurate and none asserts criteria success; omission from an abstract of a result reported prominently in the body is a framing choice, not a misstatement.
- **H: "no artifact stores a per-record verdict" contradicts "the maximum over every record is 0.0".** Disproved: the delivery identity is checked by a raise, so the absence of an exception is the verdict and the maximum violation over all 477,308 instances / 9,430 records / 13,845 distinct is 0.0. The paper states this mechanism explicitly at line 380.
- **H: the retention figure 51.14% is unsupported.** Disproved: `reading_ledger_l8.json` carries `retention.R1C = 0.5114`, `R2F = 1.0`, `R3H = 0.71`, under caliber "L8 / caliber v5 / STR-clip-only retention", with a note that the value is mirrored from the gate test which owns the computation.
- **H: the frozen 1% window-tail pad is claimed to bound the 1.9% squint deviation.** Disproved: line 143 states the opposite explicitly — "it is not claimed to bound the production closure's own squint error, which is not separately quantified here."
- **H (withdrawn finding): the symmetric-vs-exact deviation is not quantified in the manuscript.** Withdrawn: an earlier `grep` for `1.87`/`0.31` returned no hits, which I had read as absence. Direct reading of main.tex:143 shows both numbers are present ("$+1.87\%$ at the envelope corner, $+0.31\%$ at the reference squint of $12^{\circ}$"). The grep was unreliable — the project's own CLAUDE.md warns that a shell wrapper on this machine can swallow or replace grep stdout — so the absence claim was unsound and the finding does not survive. Standing lesson applied for the rest of this review: absence claims were re-checked with a second independent method.
- **H: the per-node 25/25 serviceability reading is an undeclared substitution for the registered criterion.** Disproved: line 250 discloses both readings and the reason — "The serviceability criterion is per-node: every node carries at least one in-band transit within the span, measured 25/25; the literal best-incidence reading of the dense-domain constraint fails at the centre node ... which is why the operative criterion is the per-node one" — and further discloses that "the manifest ledger stores aggregate admission counts, not per-node columns", so the 25/25 measurement is not independently re-derivable from a receipt. That is a stated limitation, not a silent substitution.

- **Disclosure of negative and inconvenient results.** Two of three pre-registered criteria are reported as failing, in the body, with the refined reading and its registration date. This is consistent with the paper's stated seam, though see the coverage limit below.
