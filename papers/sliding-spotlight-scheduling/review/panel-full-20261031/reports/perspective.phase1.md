## Contract Paraphrase

D1 — From where I sit, this dimension asks whether an outside reader could take the study's design, its handling of inputs, its numerical reporting and its reproducibility affordances at face value and rebuild or extend the work. It is not my dimension to score, but as a practical reader I read it as the question of whether the study would survive being reused by someone outside the authors' modelling idiom.

D2 — This dimension concerns whether the statements made about the domain match what practitioners and domain evidence actually support, and whether prior work is represented without distortion. For me the practical edge of it is whether a mission-operations reader would recognise the described system behaviour and the attributed prior art as accurate, rather than as a plausible-sounding abstraction.

D3 — This dimension asks whether the central argument holds together: thesis, evidence and conclusions in one consistent chain without fallacies that break the spine. My practical reading is whether a reader who accepts the premises would be forced toward the conclusion, or whether the chain only closes if one already shares an unstated disciplinary habit.

D4 — This is my dimension. It asks whether the framing, definitions and implications can be followed by readers from adjacent fields such as Earth-observation product engineering, ground-segment operations or mission planning, and whether any interdisciplinary or cross-domain claim is actually substantiated rather than merely asserted. Concretely I will be asking whether a chosen-start resolution equality, a fixed mode assignment and a reported quality index are given meanings that survive translation into operational product language, whether the counterfactual is stated in terms a customer or mission planner could act on, and whether the reproducibility and mission-use pathway is credible to someone who would have to adopt it.

D5 — This dimension covers organisation, clarity, figure and table quality, and conformity to venue conventions. From my seat the practical concern is whether the structure lets a non-specialist locate the operational takeaway early, and whether figures and tables are legible to someone who does not share the authors' background.

D6 — This dimension asks whether the work suits the configured venue and offers an original, significant contribution for that readership. I read its practical counterpart as whether the claimed contribution would matter differently to an operations readership than to a modelling readership, and whether the paper situates itself for the right one.
